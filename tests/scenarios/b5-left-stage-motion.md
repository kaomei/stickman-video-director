# B5 left-stage motion regression scenarios

Scope: local contract/review fixtures. These are expected decisions, not claims of generated previews or successful motion evaluation. Automated documentation checks live in `skills/directing-stickman-videos/tests/b5-left-stage-contract.test.mjs`; actual choreography still requires project motion strips and playback.

1. **New psychological explainer, approved presenter/audio.** Ask for left-stage “fear of accepting help.” Expect story-timed action cards with concrete load/resistance/release, conspicuous actor before/after states and varied consequences. Preserve the accepted host and narration. Reject an anxiety circle plus gift icon beside a bobbing actor.
2. **New celebrity-news explainer.** A giant archive folder can resist a stickman's pull, then release a pile of clearly illustrative photos that changes the actor's balance. Preserve factual uncertainty and distinguish illustration from evidence. Reject fabricated letters, romantic events, incriminating screenshots or a celebrity recast as the left actor. Do not treat the criticized Jensen preview as an approved animation baseline.
3. **Unrelated science topic.** Plan a cause-and-effect demonstration using the story's own mechanism. Reject reusing the source's anvil/battery/scissors order with new labels. The same physical-comedy grammar must support a different gag, not copy the source's branded copy or IP.
4. **Large object, almost no consequence.** Board drops, actor lifts a few pixels, burst appears and both settle independently. Expect rejection even with thirty SFX cues, valid IK and many headings. Author an actual contact/constraint, changed silhouette or trajectory and recovery before rendering again.
5. **Accidental anatomy versus deliberate squash.** A hand reaches beyond normal arm length: fail the normal rig. A named weight-contact beat intentionally flattens the whole body through entry/extreme/recovery: permit the deformation layer, inspect it separately and restore the rig. Do not disable normal geometry checks or preserve a round inverse-scaled head automatically.
6. **Grip during rotation and squash.** A rope's endpoint tracks only the undeformed hand while the body rotates. Fail the contact check; bind to the evaluated world transform. Review before, contact, extreme, delayed response and release, not only the first/last frame.
7. **Long held shot.** Trapped figure strains then gives a timed deadpan stare before escape. A purposeful annotated hold can pass. A standing figure with a rotating magnifier for several seconds cannot pass because captions, the host's mouth and BGM keep moving. Do not repair this by random cuts every second.
8. **Heavy sound, weak action.** A boom is on the prop's clip-start but the visible contact occurs later. Correct the shared action ID/time and source transient offset, then inspect the actor consequence. Reject both louder-music masking and sound as a substitute for motion. Do not purchase/regenerate assets under a documentation-only instruction.
9. **Local instruction repair only.** User says study CrazyMei and update B5. Read the source instructions/spec/code and supplied media, change B5 references/tests locally, preserve earlier dirty changes and record evidence. Do not render a new film, change the presenter, revoice, commit, push, install or publish. Do not describe updated instructions as proof a new film already matches the source.
10. **Native portrait pair.** User requests 16:9 and 9:16. Preserve accepted narration and identity, natively restage contact arcs, ground, captions and host in both ratios, review both. A motion contract supports the requirement but does not validate an unrendered portrait. Do not generate a second ratio when only one was requested.
