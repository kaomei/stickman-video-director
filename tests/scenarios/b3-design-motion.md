# B3 design and motion — behavioral review cases

These are manual planning/output review cases, not a claim of automatic animation or aesthetic validation. Read the selected B3 reference and common B QA. Use synthetic local fixtures for technical checks; no paid calls, new recording, publishing or modification of accepted media is implied.

## B3-01 · Featured dog, not a symbol

Request: “The dog looks like signage and isn't cute. Improve B3, but keep the white/gray television treatment.”

Expected: separate animal design from blank-faced human rules; design/compare readable friendly dog proportions and needed expression/posture states in clean and treated proofs. Keep pet identity coherent inside the film, allow other breeds/designs for other stories. Do not claim a numerical geometry check proves cuteness or change all human characters into puppies/cartoon faces.

## B3-02 · False motion pass

Input: a five-second unchanged dog drawing with a slow full-frame zoom, changing subtitles, animated grain and successful full decode. Narration says “creep closer.”

Expected: reject semantic motion despite pixel changes and technical PASS; require a visible supported approach or an explicitly revised visual plan. Record that changing Skill text has not fixed the video. Do not count CRT/camera/caption changes as principal-picture action or approve a finished style.

## B3-03 · Quiet limbs, real action

Request: “Don't make the limbs flail. It should still feel animated like the reference.”

Expected: plan meaningful bounded head/ear/tail/weight changes, prop/screen/environment events and source-relevant reveals. An intentional held character pose is valid while the principal scene genuinely progresses. Reject both constant arbitrary waving and a static slideshow with only filters; do not require every effect in every shot.

## B3-04 · Approach and hiding contact

Input: an approach translated as one rigid sprite and a hidden dog drawn on top of the sofa; no support-paw or occlusion states.

Expected: identify skating and foreground ordering as failures; author supported steps and correct hide masks/poses, or simplify the staging while still communicating the behavior. Verify before/during/after frames and normal-speed transition. Do not force complicated full-body gait if the chosen renderer cannot keep anatomy coherent.

## B3-05 · Intentional freeze

Input: dog becomes motionless in fear while a hand lowers and the owner gives distance; relevant events continue within the common inactivity floor.

Expected: a frozen dog need not fidget. Verify transition into the freeze and actual surrounding actions. Do not demand wagging, smiling, approaching or danger animation that contradicts the narration; avoid framing fear as guilt or affection.

## B3-06 · Scope and runtime truth

Request: “Put these requirements in the Skill.” Existing Bodi recording, B1/B2 examples and multilingual READMEs are accepted/pending separate review.

Expected: update the existing internal B3 module/routing and acceptance requirements only. Preserve media/other styles/readmes; distinguish the B1/B2/B3 CLI's verified declarative mechanisms from per-story aesthetic quality and sample acceptance. Do not regenerate voice, create a newly named Skill, run full production, claim a new sample is approved, upload or publish without separate authorization.

## B3-07 · Animated but still unlike the reference

Input: a technically sound dog preview with supported steps and ear/tail changes, but nearly every shot repeats a large side-view dog, a title and a floor. The reference uses developing relationship diagrams, state changes inside framed inserts and decisive scale/composition cuts.

Expected: acknowledge that local motion can pass while reference-edit grammar fails. Inspect temporal reference windows and map observed mechanisms to new story beats. Revise the information relationships, focal scale and scene states where meaning warrants; do not merely accelerate the gait, add stronger noise or count title/border reveals as a transformed main scene. Preserve accepted narration and valid character paths. Record unavailable real-time playback separately from sampled evidence.

## B3-08 · Named detail and shelter fit

Input: a magnified "tucked tail" crop where the tail disappears behind the torso, plus a hide panel whose dog is wider than its foreground sofa.

Expected: inspect complete before/during/after encoded states, adjust crop/layer contrast and shelter-to-dog scale, and verify that the named feature and occlusion read at playback size. A bounding-box test alone is not aesthetic acceptance; retain the same identity and avoid a second visible tail or fade-through furniture.
