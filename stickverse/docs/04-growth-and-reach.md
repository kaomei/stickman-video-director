# 04 · Reach: how YouTube actually decides

Short version: **YouTube does not promote channels, it promotes videos that keep people
watching.** Every view comes from one of four doors, and each door has its own entry test.

| Door | What the viewer sees | What decides it | Your job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Thumbnail + title in a personalised feed | Predicted click-through × predicted watch time for *that viewer* | Strong packaging + a topic that matches their history |
| **Suggested** | "Up next" beside a bigger video | Co-watching: do the same people watch both? | Be in the same "lane" as bigger channels |
| **Search** | Query results | Title/description match + watch time | Answer one specific question per video |
| **Shorts feed** | Vertical swipe | First 1.5 s hold + loop/re-watch | Hook in the first frame, payoff before the swipe |

Two numbers rule everything:

- **CTR** (impressions → clicks): is the packaging attractive?
- **AVD / average view duration** (or Shorts "viewed vs swiped away"): does the video deliver?

A video needs *both*. High CTR with low retention gets suppressed faster than low CTR with
great retention, because the second one is a hidden gem the system will keep testing.

---

## 0 · The honest starting position

New channel, zero history, English content, audience mostly India but hopefully global:

- Your first **10 videos** are practice. Expect 20–200 views each. This is normal.
- Your first **30 videos** are the training phase for the algorithm *and* for you.
- Somewhere between video 15 and 40, one video will do 10× the others. That video tells you
  what your channel actually is. Build the next 10 around it.
- 1,000 subscribers in 90 days is achievable but not guaranteed. 1,000 in 6 months with
  consistent quality is a realistic, defensible target.

Anyone selling you "guaranteed 10k subs" is selling you a channel that will be removed.

## 1 · Packaging is 80% of reach

Before upload, answer these five in writing. If you cannot, do not upload.

1. **Who is the one person this is for?** ("A 22-year-old scrolling at 1 AM who feels stuck.")
2. **What do they already believe?** Your hook should contradict it.
3. **What is the promise?** One sentence, no jargon.
4. **Why now?** A season, an event, a current anxiety.
5. **What will they comment?** If the answer is "nothing", the video is a dead end.

**Title rules**

- 40–60 characters; the first 5 words carry the meaning.
- Put the emotional word first: *Why*, *The*, *How*, *Nobody*, *Stop*.
- No ALL CAPS, no more than one emoji (ideally zero), no clickbait you cannot deliver.
- Test the title by asking: would a stranger repeat it to a friend?

Formulas that work for this niche:

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| The hidden mechanism | *Your brain deletes 90% of today (here's what it keeps)* |
| The contradiction | *Why rest makes you more tired* |
| The number + time | *The 3AM habit that costs you 10 years* |
| The underdog story | *The man who refused an empire* |
| The unfair truth | *Why hard work stopped paying* |
| Identity mirror | *You're not lazy. You're under-stimulated.* |

**Thumbnail rules** live in [`02-thumbnails-and-brand.md`](02-thumbnails-and-brand.md).
One rule to repeat: it must work at 320×180 px on a phone.

## 2 · Shorts strategy (your discovery engine)

Shorts do not need subscribers to travel. They are the fastest way for a nobody channel to be
seen by 100,000 people in a week.

What to do:

- **1 Short per day, 7 days a week** if you can sustain it; **4–5** is fine.
- **Hook in the first 1.5 seconds**: a question, a contradiction, or a striking image. No
  logos, no "welcome back".
- **Deliver the payoff inside 20 seconds**, then give a reason to stay: a twist, a second
  question, a loop.
- **Captions always** — most Shorts are watched muted at first.
- **Under 60 s** for maximum loop rate; 30–45 s is the sweet spot for story beats.
- **One idea per Short.** A Short with two ideas gets neither remembered.
- **Series branding**: same font, same character, same "kicker" label. A viewer who liked one
  should recognise the next instantly.
- **End with a question** you actually want answered. Comments are a ranking signal.
- **Pin a comment** linking the related long-form: *"full 9-minute version on my channel"*.

Where Shorts fail: they convert to subscribers badly and pay very little in India. Do not judge
your channel by Shorts views. Judge it by **subscribers gained per 1,000 Shorts views** — a
healthy number is 2–6. If you are getting 100k views and 40 subscribers, your Shorts are
entertaining but not *yours*: add a recurring character and a reason to come back.

## 3 · Long-form strategy (your watch hours)

Watch hours are the monetisation bottleneck. They come from people staying 4–8 minutes.

- **8–12 minutes** is the target length. Long enough for mid-rolls later, short enough to
  finish.
- **Retention shape**: expect a drop in the first 30 s, a slow decline, and a bump at any
  "reveal". If you drop 50% in 30 s, the hook is the problem, not the topic.
- **Chapters** (timestamps) keep people in long videos and appear in search.
- **One video answers one question.** Series ("The Psychology of…") build binge sessions,
  which is where suggested traffic comes from.
- **Playlists** are free watch time: a viewer who finishes video 1 should be pulled into
  video 2 automatically. Create a playlist per pillar and order it deliberately.
- **End screens** (last 20 s) with two specific videos, not "check out my channel".

## 4 · The other doors people forget

| Tactic | Effort | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Cross-post Shorts to Reels, TikTok, Facebook** | Low | Same file, new audience. Remove the YouTube watermark first. Never post other people's content. |
| **Answer real questions** | Low | Search "how to stop doomscrolling reddit" and make the video that answers the thread. Reddit/Quora traffic converts. |
| **Community posts** (after 500 subs) | Low | Polls: "which should I make next?" — voters show up. |
| **Collaborations** | Medium | A 60-second collab with a similar-size channel exposes you to a whole audience. |
| **Playlist placements** | Medium | Get your video into a bigger channel's curated playlist. Ask; the worst answer is no. |
| **Comments on big channels** | Low | Genuine, useful comments on videos in your niche. Not "check my channel" — that gets you muted and reported. |
| **Studio → Inspiration tab** | Low | Free topic and outlier research inside YouTube Studio. |
| **Hype** | None | A fan-driven boost that favours smaller channels. Ask your first 50 subscribers to hype a video. |
| **Premieres** | Low | A premiere turns an upload into an event with live chat; good for the Friday deep dive. |
| **Shorts → long-form funnel** | Medium | Every Short should point at a long-form in the pinned comment and the last frame. |

## 5 · What will get you punished

These are not "grey areas", they are account risks:

- **Buying views/subs, sub4sub, engagement pods.** Detected, and it poisons your audience
  signal — YouTube shows your next video to people who will not watch it.
- **Re-uploading other channels' videos** with a new voiceover → reused content policy.
- **Mass-producing near-identical videos from one template** → inauthentic content policy
  (see `06-policy-safe-automation.md`).
- **Clickbait that the video does not pay off** → retention collapse, and repeated offences
  affect monetisation review.
- **Fake AI "news" or fabricated quotes about real people** → misinformation and likeness
  problems.
- **Posting the same video to multiple channels you own** → duplicate/reused content.
- **Ignoring music licensing** — "no copyright infringement intended" is not a licence.

## 6 · Analytics routine (45 minutes a week, no more)

**48 hours after each upload** — three numbers only:
1. CTR (Reach tab)
2. Average view duration + the retention graph's biggest cliff
3. Views from "Browse" vs "Suggested" vs "Search" — where did it travel?

**Every Sunday:**
- Top 3 videos by *watch time* this week (not views).
- Subscribers gained per video (Content tab → sort by subscribers).
- One experiment for next week (new hook style, new thumbnail colour, new length).
- Kill or keep: any format that got 3 attempts and no traction is a hobby, not a pillar.

**Every 30 days:**
- Compare your thumbnails to the 20 best-performing thumbnails in your niche. What is
  different? Usually: bigger face, fewer words, more contrast.
- Re-cut your worst-performing long-form's first 30 seconds as a new Short. If it does well,
  the topic was fine and the packaging was the problem.

## 7 · A 4-week reach experiment calendar

| Week | Long-form experiment | Shorts experiment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Hidden mechanism" hook + 3-word thumbnail | Post at 19:00 IST daily, measure first-1.5s retention |
| 2 | Same topic, "contradiction" title | Add a recurring character sign-off |
| 3 | Add chapters + playlist placement | Make a 3-part Short series (part 1/2/3) |
| 4 | 12-minute length instead of 8 | Loop-ending Short (last frame = first frame) |

Log results in one sheet: `date | video | CTR | AVD | subs gained | what changed`. After eight
weeks you will have a personal playbook that no YouTube guru can give you.

---

## 8 · The reach flywheel, in order

1. A Short hooks a stranger (1.5 s).
2. They recognise the character → follow.
3. A long-form deep dive appears in their home feed for the same topic.
4. They watch 8 minutes → watch hours.
5. They comment → the next viewer sees a live conversation.
6. They watch the next video from the end screen → session watch time.
7. YouTube learns who your audience is → suggests you next to similar people.

Every decision you make should push one step of that loop. If a tactic does not push a step,
it is a distraction — including most "growth hacks".
