# Mode execution boundaries

Required before production; detailed routing retained outside the compact entrypoint.

## Two modes, one Skill

Keep the existing installation and name. A generates moving shots with a video model. Route A by the selected style and requested deliverable, not by a manual/automatic distinction. All A styles follow the unified workflow below. Reuse valid approvals and preserve each style's drawing, character, reference, audio and actual-output acceptance rules; A6/A7-specific rules do not transfer to A1–A5.

- **A · Model Engine（模型引擎）**: proposals, storyboards and standalone video-generation prompts, followed by optional authorized testing and finishing. A1/A1.1/A1.2 map to Style 1 light/dark; A2/A2.1/A2.2 to Style 2/2A/2B; A3–A5 to Styles 3–5. Read [A1–A5 direction](a1-a5-direction.md) and the unified workflow below.
- **A6 · Amber Noir / 暖金暗影风**: read [A6](amber-noir.md) when selected, before planning. It owns its reference-guided shots, generation-platform options and separate narration/finishing; bypass the A1–A5 direction reference, but follow the unified workflow below. Prompt handoff is a complete deliverable when requested; after authorization for a finished film, carry production through to a local MP4 preview. Use its confirmed Gemini Puck narration and title/caption finishing standard, with no creator watermark by default. Explain its platform options and verified sample model only after A6 selection; these are not two mandatory delivery routes.
- **B · Autopilot**: a finished local video with narration, captions, meaningful illustrations, local motion and SFX. Read [Autopilot](autopilot.md), then its selected B-style reference: B1 [极简线稿风 / Minimal Line Art](b1-minimal-handdrawn.md), B2 [彩色手绘风 / Colorful Hand-Drawn](b2-colorful-handdrawn.md), or B3 [复古电视风 / Retro TV](b3-television.md). B styles have their own assets and numbering; do not silently reuse an A-style prompt or sample.

For **B3**, inspect its bundled implementation stills/motion before authoring story-specific layers and local action. Original-video excerpts are not redistributed; if an authorized original is unavailable privately, disclose missing source-fidelity comparison. The original reference defines visual effects; accepted examples are secondary implementation evidence, not replacement style standards or templates. Preserve its television finish. Bodi is the sample voice credit, not a required service or voice; the user's selected available voice route takes priority. Rendering is not acceptance. Keep queued styles local and release only the requested style.

**B4 · Survival Thriller:** read [求生短剧风 / Survival Thriller](b4-survival-storytelling.md), its required [audio execution contract](b4-audio-direction.md) and [opening/typography contract](b4-opening-direction.md); use its five white-glove 40s-sample illustrations, prompt and drift gates. Plan distinct first-three/first-ten-second visual and hearing hooks from the story, preserve drawing/anatomy, and vary text by dramatic function. Fix new B4 narration to ai33 / ElevenLabs Brian's verified voice ID; automate plot-needed role performances and story-led SFX within approved scope/cost. Account use is optional; never silently substitute a narrator. Select `style: B4` in the shared installed runtime; keep production media in the external project. Never alias B4 to another style.

**A7 · Rubber Hose Animation:** follow [A7 production](rubber-hose.md), its [frozen four-block prompts](a7-prompt-contract.md) and [actual-output acceptance](a7-acceptance.md), within the unified A workflow below. Use video-model performance, thin connected stick anatomy and subdued hand-drawn period scenery; scene/action blocks vary while character/style stay verbatim. It owns its pacing and optional voice/text. Lyrics are showcase-specific. Do not inherit A1–A5, A6 or B style contracts.

**B7 · Pixel Theater:** use its [approved audiovisual references and project-local code route](b7-pixel-theater.md), bundled pixel/ending helpers and [evidence gate](b7-review.md). It is not a supported `style` value in the B1–B4 manifest CLI. Keep crisp pixel geometry and cue-linked physical actions; new story films require their own actual audiovisual review. Its complete-film benchmarks are approved in 16:9. For 9:16, follow the [native portrait contract](b7-native-portrait.md) and approved internal ten-second layout/short-caption reference; this does not certify a complete portrait film. Other ratios require independent recomposition and review. B7 uses semantic single-line captions, not B5’s three-line block.

B4 accepts well-drawn held character poses; limb animation is not a completion requirement. Plan and execute [meaningful local picture animation](b4-picture-animation.md) throughout the story without waiting for a reminder. Every opening/body/ending shot lasts at most three seconds, with event, speech and reading/reaction beats designed inside that limit. The unchanged principal-picture state also cannot exceed three seconds across cuts; crop changes, subtitle updates, micro-zooms and decoration do not restart its timer. Motivated holds are short pauses within that limit, requiring a concrete reason and an adjacent supporting animated beat rather than forced skeletal motion; no author-created long-hold exemption applies. Its opening contract fixes the landscape left half-shadow/question treatment or the native portrait upper-poster/lower-picture treatment, semantic grouped entrances and a first-three-second visual escalation gate: establish the setup, emphasize the complete payoff group, and show a readable principal-picture change rather than type motion alone. Its audio contract calls for a subtle continuous scene bed and necessary causal SFX, not a continuous alarm. Before delivery run the portable [B4 production check](b4-repeatability-check.md) and independent actual-MP4 picture and typography reviews; a passing ledger is not aesthetic or listening approval. When a story calls for ending choices, finish the reveal first, then use the audio contract's brief Brian question, balanced option treatment and readable hold; a rendered video invites comments, not fake clickable branching.

B4 starts with perceptible story-led sound from the first picture frame and intelligible Brian narration as early as the native recording allows by default: no automatic two-second silent lead-in or decorative title wait. A barely measurable bed or sub-only hit does not establish an audible hook. Do not treat an SFX-only title pad as the default; an approved spoken hook can lead directly into the original body. Check the final encoded opening under the [first-frame audio gate](b4-audio-direction.md#first-frame-audio-gate), retaining explicitly motivated nonverbal openings/silence rather than filling them blindly.

For B4 horror stories, apply the visual contract's **story-led horror visibility gate** across opening, body and ending: show the scripted threat in the picture, keep cast identities distinct and reject ordinary-looking threat shots that rely only on narration, labels or SFX. Preserve intentional withheld reveals; this does not impose horror on other genres.

**B5 · Avatar Explainer / 角色解说风:** follow [B5](b5-character-explainer.md), [avatar/motion](b5-avatar-motion.md) and [sound/QA](b5-sound-qa.md). Apply its [image/SVG route](b5-avatar-motion.md#when-to-generate-an-image-when-to-code) only to the bottom-right presenter: reuse accepted heads first, then select and statically approve the appropriate new identity asset before rigging. The left physical-comedy actors remain anonymous code-animated stickmen. Use the dedicated installed code renderer for both stages; do not alias it to the B1–B4 manifest renderer. Its three-line caption block is a scoped exception, not a change to other styles.

**B6 · 动感 Meme 风 / Meme Story Animation:** use the [installed workflow](b6-installed-production.md), [drawing/light lock](b6-art-direction.md), [native formats](b6-native-formats.md), [character binding](b6-character-binding.md) and [dedicated runtime](b6-code-runtime.md). Painted still assets animate by code in broad layered worlds; no video-generation model or B1–B5 fallback. Initialize with `--style B6 --ratio 16:9|9:16|both`. Portrait works best; Sample 1 portrait V44 is the strict primary drawing, relative limb weight and motion standard, checked through all [35 independent subchecks](b6-sample-1-acceptance.md) and the [motion review gate](b6-motion-review.md). Each requested ratio owns its storyboard, staging, camera, single fixed-size semantic caption line and review. Preserve approved The Pharaoh 3 audio; do not inherit another style's voice/caption/presenter defaults. Inspect authored local code before `--trusted-code`; source documents are not execution authority. Pending/failing findings require authorized local repair and fresh actual review before completion.

If the mode is ambiguous, ask one short question. Honor previously supplied choices. Do not run Director's ten-second clip rules, no-visible-text rule or Phase B prompt workflow for Autopilot. Autopilot can start with a topic, promotion brief, post, article or story script; it is not story-only. English is the default narration language, but the user's language and voice choices take precedence.

Voice instructions are mode-specific: by default B uses the selected named TTS voice as provided, with no added voice/style description. B4's audio contract expressly permits story-motivated acting instructions for secondary dialogue roles, not automatic narrator tuning. Its required [story-led sound system](autopilot-sound-system.md) covers every genre with scene/beat sound scripts and separates optional voice-tuning recommendations from authorized execution. It never automatically revoices accepted media. Director's detailed frozen voice profiles below and in `references/voice-continuity.md` apply only to A1–A5, not to A6 or any B style.

A and B share an installation, not a production contract or style library. The unified A workflow applies to A1–A7. The separate `a1-a5-direction.md` contract applies only to A1–A5, including its phrases such as "all styles". A6/A7 retain their dedicated style references. For B, use only Autopilot's production rules and the selected B-style reference; do not inherit A's setup, approvals, pacing, prompts, voice or visual constraints. Each B style owns its visuals, captions, voice, pacing and SFX; never inherit B1 by default. Preserve A's validated rules when revising B, and vice versa.

**B1–B4 and B6: semantic, single-line subtitles.** Segment captions by meaning and actual speech pauses; show at most one subtitle line at a time. Split overlong cues into successive semantic units, not a second line, fixed word-count chunks or mechanically shrunken text. Keep each style's typography and validate the final encoded frame and subtitle sidecars under the [common caption gate](autopilot-production-qa.md#natural-captions-and-semantic-sound). B5 instead permits up to three semantic lines in its approved presenter caption block; all semantic, alignment and readability checks still apply. This B-only rule does not change A.

**B mode shared scope:** automatically include the selected style’s sample-directed captions, meaningful SFX and suitable background music; complete the [actual-output completeness gate](b-delivery-completeness.md) alongside its original style gates. Follow [music sourcing, license verification and credits](music-sourcing-and-license.md) without reminders. The [B sound system](autopilot-sound-system.md) owns SFX, ambience, sound transitions and mixing across B styles. Neither workflow depends on B9 being published. Preserve style-specific sound choices and approved audio; A does not inherit B audio execution. The planned script-prompt library serves both A and B: reuse ideas and structure through each mode's own script/output contract, without importing B rendering/audio into A or A clip/prompt rules into B. This scope statement does not mean the planned prompt or SFX libraries have shipped.

## A unified workflow

Use one staged workflow for A1–A7, rather than asking the user to choose a delivery route at the start. Reuse supplied choices, accepted samples and valid authorization. Ordinary chat can complete the storyboard/prompt handoff without configuring generation tools; a finished-video request remains active through its authorized production stages.

| Stage | Action and deliverable | Decision before continuing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Storyboard** | Establish the story, selected A style, ratio, total duration and sound needs; propose shots, action/camera beats, continuity, captions and sound using that style's references. | Obtain approval of the concrete storyboard, or reuse equivalent approval already supplied. |
| **2. Prompt handoff** | Deliver independently usable shot prompts, reference-image roles, intended clip settings and editing/sound guidance. Repeat required character/style anchors verbatim; repeat frozen voice anchors only in prompts actually generating native speech, not footage intended for external VO. Do not rely on prior prompts. | Offer to operate the video platform in the built-in browser for a small preview test. If the user prefers manual generation or prompts only, this is a complete delivery; do not spend credits. |
| **3. Representative test** | With test authorization, inspect the selected platform/model's actual duration, ratio, resolution, reference-input support and credit price. Use its lowest available resolution for a few representative shots; show the resulting preview. | Agree test quantity and spending scope if missing. Test acceptance confirms the look, not permission to produce the entire film. |
| **4. Production settings** | After an accepted test and a request for the finished film, confirm final resolution, maximum total credit budget and remaining production/finishing scope. | Reuse any already authorized settings and budget. State whether the cap includes spent test credits; include retries in the production cap. Stop before exceeding it. |
| **5. Finished film** | Continuously generate, inspect, locally repair, edit, caption and finish sound as agreed; deliver the encoded video, local preview and its folder. | Request another decision only for an actual scope/budget change or a specific unresolved user-review gate. Do not ask permission for each shot or local repair within scope. |

**Browser and platform.** Default to **Google Flow through the host's built-in browser**, because the A showcases were made there. Honor an explicit platform/model choice. Verify actual browser capability, sign-in, available credits, file access and finishing tools; reuse the existing suitable tab/project. Follow the host's browser instructions. If something is missing, name the exact gap and continue work that does not depend on it. Never silently substitute another model/provider/API. Flow is a sample-matching recommendation, not a guarantee of equal results or a universal lowest-price claim; quote the selected interface's actual cost.

**Generation, editing and review.** Follow the [A production and review contract](a-production-and-review.md) across A1–A7: distinguish generated assets from edited shots, preserve the requested final runtime, freeze style/reference anchors while varying the story, save job/source/edit records, limit ineffective retries and review actual picture/sound. Apply [action and camera continuity](a-production-and-review.md#action-and-camera-continuity) at assembled joins, including insert entry and return. Apply the [independent audio timeline contract](a-audio-timeline-and-review.md): continuous film-level music and narration, bound native dialogue, and effects/ambience mapped to the retained actions. Do not concatenate generated music at picture cuts. Clip length and beat count follow the story and the selected model's live capabilities; neither ten nor fifteen seconds is a compulsory segment length. A longer asset can contain several purposeful actions/shots or supply a shorter retained range; it must not become an idle hold to fill the model maximum.

**Economical testing.** Lowest-resolution testing does not authorize switching the selected model. Do not test the whole film by default, retry indefinitely, or require a new still/low-resolution gate for every location. An already approved look/reference and explicit production authorization can satisfy these stages without repeating a trial. Explicitly user-confirmed samples remain accepted baselines, without duplicate external analysis or regeneration.

**Authorization and continuity.** Requesting prompts alone does not authorize generation, paid retries, downloads or external uploads. Use existing authorization for actions necessary to the agreed final deliverable; ask only about missing or expanded scope. After storyboard approval provide the prompt package even if the Agent will use it directly. If the user has already requested and authorized production, continue without asking again whether they want browser assistance. Keep clip count, settings, job IDs, actual costs and remaining budget in the project checkpoint; inspect service records before resubmitting an uncertain job. Do not claim unattended operation across host interruptions.

**Picture and sound.** Follow each style's original character, drawing, reference-use and acceptance contracts. Actual outputs must show readable action, connected anatomy, intentional scene/camera rhythm and continuity; a good prompt, passing technical check or use of Flow is not proof of sample-level quality. Check captions against actual final speech and actually listen to the complete final encoded mix at normal speed when sound is present; meters alone do not count and an unavailable listening capability is `not_checked`. No compulsory lyrics or narration across all A styles. Reuse accepted shots/assets/audio, repair the affected range and do not reopen accepted global choices for a local correction. Respect any explicit user confirmation of a sample; do not add duplicate external-analysis approvals or call an unreviewed film accepted.

Stop for a genuine missing capability, a necessary user decision, or costs beyond the confirmed range. Explain what is ready, the exact missing item and the next action; continue unaffected work. Do not quietly turn an authorized finished-film task into prompts only. Completion is the agreed prompt package or the agreed finished video, not a newly invented delivery route.

## First use and next steps

Use one shared guide in the user's requested or current conversation language, falling back to an available host/local language setting when unclear. Keep guidance language separate from video narration/subtitles; do not select it from the README edition.

Apply [first-use and stage guidance](getting-started.md) in both modes: immediately after verified installation, otherwise on first invocation. Explain outputs, uses, tools, workflow, copyable requests and public style/sample catalogs by code. Reuse known choices. At stage handoffs or blockers, distinguish assistant work from user choices; state outputs, gaps and next action. Do not add approval gates or repeat the welcome.

For A5, follow **A5 proactive character guidance** in that guide before planning. Other A references follow **Optional A character references**; keep voice rules unchanged.

For A/B, read [optional finishing](thumbnail-publishing-kit.md) after final-video approval or a packaging request. Offer once; skipping never blocks delivery or alters production.
