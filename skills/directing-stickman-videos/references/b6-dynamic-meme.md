# B6 · 动感 Meme 风 / Dynamic Meme Animation

Use this module inside **directing-stickman-videos**. B6 is not a new Skill, B5 presenter layout, a video-model preset or a promise of a one-click 1:1 replica. It combines deliberately imperfect painted meme characters, soft light, large layered environments and code-directed acting/camera work. Changing the story or cast must preserve the drawing grammar, not Victor's identity.

An older local experiment called “B6 / Animated Meme” was renamed B5. This current **B6 / Dynamic Meme Animation** is the separate wide-world story-animation route described here; keep those two routes distinct.

Required before planning: [Sample 1 acceptance matrix](b6-sample-1-acceptance.md), [drawing and light](b6-art-direction.md), [character binding and action acceptance](b6-character-binding.md), [native format direction](b6-native-formats.md), [B common QA](autopilot-production-qa.md) and [story-led sound](autopilot-sound-system.md). For execution read [B6 runtime](b6-code-runtime.md).

The required [installed production workflow](b6-installed-production.md) owns new-story execution. Apply the [motion execution and review gate](b6-motion-review.md) before authoring and at preview review: lock the relevant moving-reference abilities, verify their actual implementation, and separate technical results from motion quality and the user's decision. The user's brief must lead to a finished film through the agent; documentation, copied assets and unchanged V90 reproduction alone do not satisfy it. The default authoring project now carries the mature painted engine. The six-second blocking renderer is only an explicitly selected internal calibration example.

## Default settings

Explicit user choices prevail. B6 defaults to English narration with **ElevenLabs — The Pharaoh 3 - Energetic, Lively & Cheerful**. Verify the exact voice ID in the selected authenticated service before any new authorized synthesis; the scaffold deliberately leaves `voice_id` unset rather than inventing one. Use native named-voice settings with empty extra direction by default. Reuse the accepted track, exact words and SHA-256; no recasting, rate changes or paid auditions from a visual revision.

Subtitles use B6’s user-approved Archivo Black revision of the [Sample 1 portrait geometry](b6-sample-1-acceptance.md#portrait-caption-renderer-fixed-reference-facts): at 720×1280, Archivo Black 47 px, pure white, 4 px `#171319` outline, fixed baseline 914 in band 864–951, side allowance 44, 3× rendering/Lanczos and immediate semantic-phrase cuts. No extra shadow, pop, bounce, auto-shrink or unintended blank-frame flash between continuous cues; intentional gaps during real speech pauses remain valid. Scale the portrait profile uniformly for another native portrait resolution; never shrink individual cues. The former blocking defaults and bottom-centered `mm` captions are not primary-standard values. Landscape needs its own native layout/reason and review; its supplied starting geometry is an unapproved proposal. Keep semantic phrases complete and align them to actual speech/readable holds; V44 cue counts are observations, not a change-frequency quota. The default English portrait profile is `eiffel-v44-portrait-archivo-black-v1`; it uses the bundled `fonts/ArchivoBlack.ttf`. New stories do not require Arial Black. Sample 1 remains the drawing and animation reference; its historical font is not the current default. An explicit user choice or language needing unsupported glyphs requires a separately named implemented profile, verified font/glyph coverage and repeated native/runtime validation before use; a recorded reason alone cannot enable an override. Use the approved profile explicitly; do not silently replace its font or layout.

## Complete production sequence

1. **Recover and lock.** Read the current project checkpoint, actual media and last user instruction. Resolve story, audience, ratio(s), duration and approved text. When an era/place is specified, save its look brief under [era, place and trend direction](b6-art-direction.md#era-place-and-trend-direction): context/references, distinctive clothing silhouette, hair/headwear, accessories and scene vocabulary. Default to a bold, readable selection of relevant period trends unless the user requests restraint. Reuse accepted art/audio/captions by fingerprints. No automatic continuation of an unapproved film. A request for one ratio never authorizes a second paid version.
2. **Translate the story into beats.** Each beat needs cause → action/information → reaction/payoff. Identify contact, emotion, evidence and scene geography. Lock the selected reference's relevant dynamic abilities and map them to the new beats using [motion execution and review](b6-motion-review.md); do not reduce the target to matching paintings and valid bones. Choose its information carrier using [environment and ensemble shots](b6-native-formats.md#environment-and-ensemble-shots): the protagonist may be absent, including in the opening; establish a living place or relevant object when that serves the story. Keep setup/reveal information readable; avoid the former sub-second `SOLD` flash. New information needs time to locate, read and settle (about one second is a review starting point). Review every cut under .9 s; shorter motivated action cuts are allowed with a recorded reason.
3. **Design both requested formats separately.** Save independent shot IDs/timing, staging, focal/occlusion order, camera keys, entrances/exits, prop contacts and caption bands. See [native format direction](b6-native-formats.md). Shared script, paintings, continuous voice and narrative beat IDs are reusable; a finished video or identical normalized coordinates are not the other format's plan.
4. **Build the art before animation.** Approve a whole character and face/body crops against the drawing baseline. For a specified era/place, also review costume, hair and accessories at the actual shot scale; a generic person beside an old prop is insufficient. Put the concrete approved look into all relevant still prompts and multi-angle identity records. Produce story-specific front, three-quarter, profile and back views, plus neutral/suspicious/greedy/excited/shocked/disappointed states as the story needs. The bundled front/side/back examples do not stand in for a missing three-quarter view or all emotions. Separate head/face/body, near/far limbs, hands, shoes, held props and occluders. Record pivots and ink-safe overlaps on the actual opaque pixels. Inspect a second character to test style transfer and distinct role-specific period styling.
5. **Build a large world.** Draw a continuous wide set with recognizable left/center/right regions, enough height and overscan for the most extreme frustum. Establish ground, horizon, scale, wall/floor relationship and front/middle/back layers. Apply the same era/place brief to architecture, shops/signs, transport, furnishings and background cast, distributing readable local landmarks along the camera route. Add purposeful dressing, independent background pedestrians and transport, not mirrored facade tiles. Plan the camera path before finalizing plate coverage. Generate new still art only within the authorized provider/reference/cost scope; animate entirely by code.
6. **Test action locally.** Pin and reuse the user's selected existing actor and compatible reviewed performance before fitting another rig. Calibrate each new identity/view on its actual sleeve, hip, sole and furniture points; verify natural reach during blocking. Follow [limb calibration and retained performance](b6-code-runtime.md#limb-calibration-and-retained-performance). Make a fixed-camera turn/contact test before extending the story, then recheck with the intended camera. Use real angle assets, occlusion and head/body delay; do not squeeze a frontal face into profile. Fixed arm chains use anticipation → approach → contact → shared hold → release → recovery; a held prop inherits the gripping hand or one shared contact transform. Review near/far arm ownership, return-side targets, elbow silhouettes, sleeve/table occlusion and grounded seated-to-standing transitions separately from numerical bone checks. Keep the user-selected straighter-leg gait, planted stance, heel/flat/toe progression, modest lift, opposite arms and very small vertical bob. Horse hooves carry weight; wheels rotate from world distance/radius. No actor rocking to imitate a camera move.
7. **Direct the camera in space.** Use motivated lateral tracks/reveals, foreground crossings, depth dollies, reaction cuts, object follow and readable settles. A broad FOV/near-layer sweep may stretch the environment through projection; never anisotropically stretch a face/body or the final canvas. Camera movement and independently moving background actors must coexist. Reserve brief shake/overshoot for earned accents; don't oscillate every person or every shot.
8. **Voice, captions and sound.** Align to actual approved speech, write semantic cues per format and retain original speed. Save the shared scene/beat sound script with material contacts, distance, environment state and quiet/payoff hierarchy. Retain original voice and stems. The B6 encoder takes a prepared full-length mixed audio track; it is not a Foley generator/mixer. Do not duplicate effects already in the master.
9. **Preview, then extend.** First render a representative 5–20 s with the story's contact, emotional reaction, wide-world camera and actual audio/captions. Apply the [motion review gate](b6-motion-review.md) to actual full-size frames, shot joins and intended-speed playback; compare against the selected moving reference and repair gaps before presenting the result as matching. Record what each promised motion actually does, not just its shot name. For a new story or quality regression, obtain the independent result review described there when available. Then ask the user to review the actual preview. Each requested ratio has its own preview and acceptance; never infer the second from the first. Technical fixtures and a successful encode are not creative approval.
10. **Finish and deliver.** Render only authorized production, preserve the style/audio lock and inspect every shot including the middle/ending. Check hand ownership, feet/hooves, wheels, expression changes, geography, camera overscan, caption fit/timing and audio intelligibility. Save MP4, captions, asset/hash manifest, native plans and checkpoint. Include a clickable containing-folder link with every video delivery. Publication is a separate user instruction.

## Stop-and-repair criteria

Repair the affected art or action before extending: limb weight drifting to Sample 2's thinner lines instead of matching Sample 1 portrait; a requested era conveyed only by a label/filter/old prop while clothes, hair and scenery remain generic; mixed or anachronistic period cues; uniform clean vector outlines; head/body brush mismatch; round emoji faces; polished 3D shine; constantly frozen expression; duplicate/broken limbs; sliding paper or feet; crouched gait; camera replaced by actor sway; empty/mirrored scene edges; only repeated zooms; unreadable flash text; per-cue font shrinking; reference audio used without permission. Preserve unaffected accepted material. Do not regenerate a full voice/film to fix a wrist or one shot.

Save project checkpoints at art, rig test, native preview and delivery stages: approved constraints, absolute output paths, hashes, tested scope, pending review, active process/job IDs and next action. No paid calls, uploading, commit, push or publication is implied by this module.
