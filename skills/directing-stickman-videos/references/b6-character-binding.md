# B6 · Limb binding and action acceptance

This is the mandatory character-binding contract for every B6 scene, new cast and revision. Preserve the selected painted art, expressions, voice, captions and camera unless the user requests those changes. **Sample 1, the Eiffel portrait V44, is the strict primary acceptance reference; B6 works best in 9:16.** Match its single-stroke limb weight, pressure variation and brushwork at matched head size. The Hong Kong reference is the exact **V90 20-second** Sample 2 gallery clip; its technical evidence is in `assets/b6/gallery.json`. It supplies secondary binding/engineering evidence, not a replacement visual target: its finer arms must not become the default for a new B6 character. Listing either reference is not approval of a new performance.

The [portable V90 project](b6-hong-kong-v90.md) implements these checks for its existing eight profiles and 600-frame performance: one shared arm solver, per-context 2D pose envelopes, arm/leg inventory, paired planted legs, uniform actor transforms and independently measured prop grips. Its source contract is `assets/b6/hong-kong-v90/character-bindings.json`; numerical passes remain separate from visual/user acceptance. Do not transfer the calibrated dimensions or envelopes to another cast without refitting and review.

## Executable character package for new work

New projects (schema version 2) must use the installed `b6_puppet.py` path. `characters.json` maps the native cast to per-view `b6-puppet/1` packages; every package includes actual RGBA head/body art, independently hashed sleeve-cap alpha masks, true sleeve outlets, fixed planar bones, bounded authored poses and near/far order. See the [executable API](b6-puppet-api.md). A prose checklist or old numeric `character-bindings.json` alone cannot satisfy this new-character route.

Author each fitting from that character's visible garment. Keep a calibrated package for every required view, and use the same loaded package across all scene branches. New identities do not acquire valid sockets by inheriting another identity's coordinates. `Puppet` preserves anatomical IDs; `ContactTimeline` is the sole owner of the shared prop and grip targets. An unused arm stays in its own rest action. New targets outside reach or the approved 2D pose envelope must be restaged, never stretched or accepted by widening the gate.

`scene.frame()` returns the compositor's final `RenderResult`, including actual occluders. The production runner verifies the registered package fingerprint/view, four limb IDs per actor, final alpha connectivity at the **real sleeve outlet**, elbow/wrist/hand connectivity, readable visible fractions and paper contact. It rejects changed beauty/mask/ID pixels after auditing. An additional foreground must supply its actual matching alpha; a whole garment is not a sleeve cap. A separate “correct skeleton” beside an unchecked image is not evidence.

Run the installed negative regressions (`tests/b6_puppet_test.py`) after changing the runtime, fitting or compositing. They intentionally remove/swallow/swap limbs, move grips and corrupt art/evidence. A new gate must both accept a valid rendered action and reject its injected failure; testing only returned joint coordinates is insufficient.

The new demo roles and stationary exchange fixture exercise front/three-quarter/side artwork, a short pose-based turn, approach, shared handoff and withdrawal. They do not establish back views, walking, arbitrary props, facial acting or film-wide creative acceptance. Old already-authored version-1 projects retain their frozen path. The `locked-v90-performance` exception is limited by embedded hashes to the installed frozen 7.5-second example; it cannot authorize new cast/actions. Version-2 projects reject `rigid_cast` declarations until actual pixel coverage exists. Changed project-local runtime code is rejected before import.

## Bind once per identity and view

Before animation, save a project-local `character-bindings.json` covering every visible character: foreground, supporting cast, cook, customer and street extras. For each identity/view record:

- Source asset paths/hashes, supported view and pose, local coordinate origin and uniform scale.
- Anatomical left/right identity and the current near/far assignment. Near/far may change during a turn; anatomical ownership cannot switch. Record that transition explicitly.
- Actual opaque-pixel sleeve exits, shoulder anchors, fixed upper/forearm control lengths, elbow bend side, wrist/grip sockets, rest targets and sampled ink width/pressure variation relative to the head, with the compared Sample 1 portrait frame and matched-scale crop recorded. Do not copy V90's finer line weight or invent a universal pixel-width threshold. Each arm has one named shoulder → elbow → wrist control chain; the painted silhouette may be one continuous line or shallow curve instead of displaying that control elbow.
- Hip anchors, upper/lower leg lengths when articulated, knees, ankle/sole contacts, standing height and seat/support contacts. Preserve the accepted straighter-leg walk.
- Painted sleeve/body/hand mattes and the furniture/prop occluders for each pose. Calibrate a new view on that view's art; do not copy another character's shoulder coordinates or dimensions.

Every scene entry point for that identity must resolve the same binding and shared solver/compositor. Do not leave counter, booth, arcade, walk, reaction or background-animation branches with their own ad hoc arm formulas. For new version-2 projects, static background people also need a registered package and validated resting pose; declaring them as rigid extras is currently rejected. Historical frozen films can preserve intact source poses without claiming coverage by the new runtime.

## Hard geometry and ownership constraints

1. All coordinates, lengths and transforms are finite. Lengths are positive and calibrated in actor-local units; apply the actor transform once. Do not mix local lengths and screen-space hand targets.
2. Preserve internal control-segment lengths through every keyframe and interpolated pose. The two-bone solver accepts only the declared bend side, never an arbitrary bend multiplier. An unreachable target blocks that action: restage the actor, table or prop, or author a genuine body reach. Do not stretch, silently clamp the grip, or add fictitious depth to make a numerical check pass. A continuous painted arm may show cartoon projection shortening; report its actual path/arclength separately instead of claiming its semantic halves have the fixed control lengths.
3. Keep control elbow direction and the resting wrist on the authored side. A crossing gesture needs a deliberate pose and depth order. Check the actual painted sleeve exit, stroke direction and whole silhouette in addition to internal bone lengths. A correct internal distance does not prove a readable arm, and an invisible control elbow does not require an imposed visible corner.
4. Transform each shoulder–elbow–wrist control chain together. Draw one complete coherent stroke in its assigned layer. The optional runtime `ink_path: {"mode":"continuous","sag":12}` joins sleeve root to exact wrist with a shallow downward quadratic; `sag:0` is straight. Its real curve must have no folds, emerge through the actual sleeve opening, and remain within the bounded painted arclength. Use the original garment matte at the sleeve root. Its `upper`/`forearm` masks are disjoint semantic halves of that single raster, joined at the real curve midpoint, not separately drawn bone fragments. Duplicate strokes, double-painted seams and disconnected fragments are failures. Omission retains legacy two-bone drawing.
5. Resolve far arm, body, sleeve, near arm, hand, tabletop/edge and prop order by the contact state. Do not draw all arms permanently over furniture or hide the full arm behind it. If an arm is occluded, retain the continuous hidden chain and inspect the visible transitions; a floating hand is not acceptable.
6. A held prop follows its hand socket. During an exchange, use giver → shared contact → receiver ownership and one shared prop transform. The hands and prop cannot slide independently. Verify approach, contact, hold, release and return, including the receiving hand's return side.
7. Feet must support the body. Keep planted soles in world space during stance or a rise from a chair; do not move both feet upward with the torso. Derive walk phase from world travel, with modest foot lift, readable weight transfer, opposite arm swing and very small body bob. Use the selected straight-leg silhouette without backward knees or a crouched shuffle.
8. Keep the pressure-varying painted single-stroke limbs at Sample 1 portrait's relative weight, including their visibly heavier arms compared with Sample 2. Calibrate every identity/view at matched head size and check the whole figure; a finer V90 wire line is a style regression even when the chain geometry passes. Camera zoom cannot substitute for changing the actor-local stroke proportion, and the character transform stays uniform. No flesh-filled anatomical forearms, oversized circular joints or frame-random contour noise.

## Action and screen-direction continuity

Block rest → preparation → approach → contact → short readable hold → release → recovery before adding camera motion. Assign FK action and phase per arm before bounded IK; an unused hand stays at rest. A contact outside the permitted pose correction needs a changed action or staging, never widened lengths or gates. Turns use compatible front/three-quarter/profile/back artwork, occlusion and a small head/body timing offset. Never flatten the front face or twist the torso repeatedly to mimic movement.

Stage target height against the control reach and torso, while keeping the painted arm, hand and prop readable. A reachable target may still force a sharply peaked visible elbow or an awkward continuous stroke. Inspect each end pose at native output scale and the encoded action at normal playback speed; adjust the target, shallow-curve fit or body staging without changing approved bones, poles or art. Legal IK and connected pixels alone do not establish naturalness. Do not force the painted arm into a boxy two-segment silhouette merely to display its internal solver.

For a demonstrated action-specific silhouette failure, calibrate `action_envelopes` on that arm and action, including an upper-angle interval inside the physical joint range. Do not ban all raised elbows or flip the pole during an exchange to repair one invitation pose. Check contact and recovery as well as the invitation before accepting the envelope.

During a handoff, keep giver and receiver grips on the same physical prop but separate enough to read both painted hands. Finish the recipient’s approach at its final grip before shared ownership; start the giver’s recovery from its last supported point and route the forearm around the actual prop alpha. Stacking complete hands, erasing the forearm behind a prop, and jumping to a default rest point are failures even when a held frame passes contact math.

For torn or grouped props, keep stable part IDs and independent real-alpha contact evidence. Gather their physical grips at the receiving hand before the other hand releases, retain a short readable shared hold, then withdraw. One hand holding multiple pieces requires coincident independently projected grips; conflicting targets, out-of-bounds UV clamping or a virtual unsplit prop cannot substitute for the rendered pieces.

Aim the eyes at the partner, object or discovered information before the reaction. Preserve the selected meme face and head/hair silhouette when changing expression; do not replace surprise with generic round emoji eyes. Across cuts, retain gaze direction, hand ownership, prop location and the action's phase. Do not replay the same person's entrance or reverse the gaze axis without a readable spatial bridge.

## Review sequence and blocking failures

**Input:** pinned art and bindings, blocked set/contact points, approved action/voice/captions, separately authored native camera plan.

**Operation:** first render a fixed-camera two-to-three-second turn/contact test. Inspect each identity/view at rest, maximum reach, contact, crossing, release and recovery. Then add the intended camera and decode the encoded output. Inspect cut boundaries and intervening action poses, using a timeline board followed by enlarged sleeve/elbow/hand/feet crops.

**Mechanical checks:** for every animated frame, record character/scene/view, chain ownership, finite values, bone-length deviation at a declared tolerance, reach validity, grip error, pose/bend transitions, ground contacts and render-branch coverage. Check that every animated character in the inventory actually uses the shared path. Do not create new rigid-art exceptions without actual pixel coverage; version-2 currently rejects them. Unchanged voice/captions/camera require matching fingerprints or recorded timeline/camera comparisons.

**Viewport check:** require root/painted-path join/wrist/ankle/grip connectivity when the actual center is inside the canvas, without enlarging the canvas by stroke radius. For a continuous arm the join is the actual curve midpoint, not its hidden control elbow. A just-offscreen socket is a legitimate crop, while any visible entering limb portions retain their original alpha-readability thresholds. Offscreen control geometry, fixed internal arm lengths and cast inventory remain checked; this exception cannot waive a limb swallowed inside the picture.

**Head/neck check:** new binding-version-2 packages declare an actual painted neck mount and minimum head/body overlap fraction/thickness. Verify the main opaque components over the full expression/nod sweep and record actual output alpha contact; bounds, a plausible anchor or a single paint pixel cannot prove support. Fit the original art before considering a new neck drawing. A collar gap at neutral or during motion is a blocking failure.

**Visual checks:** continuous sleeve–elbow–hand silhouette; correct bend/return direction; near/far/table occlusion; no missing, duplicate or floating limb; supported head/neck; natural contact and weight; pressure-varying ink weight consistent with Sample 1 portrait at matched head size, and expression/gaze. A numerical pass cannot override a visible failure. Stop and repair the affected action before extending; do not redo unaffected art/audio.

**Output:** bindings, per-frame technical report, decoded contact sheets/detail crops, separate observed visual findings, exact preview hash and pending/accepted/rejected user status. A fixed-camera pass permits camera testing; only actual user review permits extending an otherwise pending preview.

The Hong Kong V90 evidence covers a shared arm solver across the re-rendered counter/booth/arcade and animated restaurant extras. It does not establish a universal new-character auto-rig, a fresh leg-rig validation, or acceptance of another story/ratio.
