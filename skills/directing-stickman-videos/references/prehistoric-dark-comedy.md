# Style 3 — Prehistoric Hand-Drawn / Dark Comedy

## Fixed style, variable cast

Preserve the drawing grammar, not a single default face. Use crude hand-drawn 2D black outlines, white filled round heads, thin black stick limbs, expressive simple cartoon eyes/brows/mouths, animal-hide clothing, earthy ochre/brown scenery and small warm fire accents. Subtle paper texture and irregular outlines belong to this style; no photorealistic skin, glossy 3D characters or modern studio UI. Do not apply Style 1's hollow-head/no-face/no-clothes contract here.

Cast to fit the topic. Adults, children, elders and different genders can share this visual grammar without sharing a face. In Phase A provide a compact cast card for each named role: role and age range, head/body proportions, hair, eyebrows/eyes, optional facial marks, costume silhouette and one recognizable accessory. Give recurring characters at least two visible non-color differences. Do not identify gender through eyelashes alone or make every elder or child a stereotype. Keep the cast as small as the story needs; explicitly state which characters appear in each clip and forbid unintended duplicates.

New stories may use entirely new casts. Within one story, freeze each character's card and repeat its FULL description in every standalone prompt where it appears. Never use only “the same caveman” or an ellipsis as an identity lock. Lock white head fill, head/body proportions, hair, facial marks, costume and accessory. Expressions and poses may change; identity must not.

## Translate topics into concrete prehistoric situations

Identify the source's actual point, conflict and stakes first. Turn abstract mechanisms into physical tasks, incentives and props: endless recommendations may become a chief repeatedly offering snacks; workload may become a growing spear bundle; social pressure may become a small watching tribe. These are optional examples, not a recurring mandatory cast or plot. Settings can include caves, camps, forests, river crossings and rocky plains—not only the same cave bed.

Satire is fictional analogy. Do not present invented scenes as the historical origin of employment, technology or social behavior. Preserve scientific/technical explanations when relevant; if an analogy would distort the mechanism, explain it in VO or choose a clearer metaphor. Respect user requests for serious, gentle or non-comedic treatment rather than forcing ridicule.

## Story and timing

Plan premise/conflict → tempting solution/escalation → reversal/callback across the approved duration. Three ten-second clips are one possible 30-second example, not a fixed generation requirement; use the selected model's actual settings and trim when needed. Approximately 18–25 spoken words per ten seconds is a starting guide for narrated work, not a quota; scale to actual speech pace and leave room for reaction beats. A deadpan glance or held expression of roughly half a second to two seconds can be the payoff. Do not insert effects every moment or force four devices into a quiet joke. Prefer clear action, expressive reactions, a motivated camera cut and synchronized SFX over irrelevant spectacle.

Use explicit timed physical beats and track prop ownership. State where an object starts, who hands it over and where it ends. Avoid spawning objects, teleporting stones, duplicated tools or characters changing costumes between shots. Draw a simple verbal layout, keep screen direction stable and reserve subtitle-safe space without requesting generated subtitles. In 16:9 use lateral staging; in 9:16 re-block for depth and vertical readability.

## Continuity and generation

Phase A includes cast cards, location layout, exact English VO with translation when narration is wanted, a final-timeline music/SFX plan and named ending/opening states. Phase B repeats visual identity and continuity anchors in each relevant prompt. Name intentional time jumps in the approved proposal and stitching guide. Choose understated primitive percussion, dry pauses or another story-appropriate score, without inheriting mandatory optimistic piano. Apply `voice-continuity.md` and the [A audio timeline contract](a-audio-timeline-and-review.md): default finished narration to an independent complete master and music to a continuous master or arranged cues. Repeat a complete voice block and exact lines only in prompts generating native speech; external VO is an editing cue, not a video-model speech request. Deadpan reactions and cave acoustics must not recast the narrator or add reverb to dry narration. Narration stays off-screen; lip-sync needs an approved dialogue plan.

When supported, reuse approved character images and prior ending frames as references. For native narrator drift, inspect full voice-profile repetition and conflicting directions before an authorized targeted retest under `voice-continuity.md`. Prompt repetition does not guarantee matching faces, voice or music; the historical Style 3 native-audio setup was not separately provider-tested with that block. For finished work, assemble independent narration/music, map usable SFX to retained actions and actually listen to the final encoded mix. Follow the [unified A workflow](mode-execution-boundaries.md#a-unified-workflow): independent prompts, optional authorized representative test, then continuous finishing within the confirmed settings/budget. Reuse authorization; do not promise unavailable browser capabilities or spend indefinitely on material identity/action failures. Publication remains a separate user instruction.

## Prompt assembly

Each prompt includes: duration/ratio → full drawing-style anchor → full cast cards for visible roles → location/layout and opening state → timed actions and prop transfers → intended sound role and timed SFX cues → ending state → negatives. Only native-speech prompts add the exact complete line and frozen voice block; external narration and continuous music use their own final-timeline plan. Name colors naturally; never use technical color notation. Forbid visible text, subtitles, logos, speech bubbles, color swatches, extra humans, duplicate props, extra limbs and unintended identity changes. Do not forbid the cartoon faces, hair or texture that define this mode.

## Review checklist

- Source meaning retained; fiction is not mislabeled as history.
- Cast fits this story and is distinguishable without color alone.
- Every recurring role has the same complete anchor across clips.
- Correct head fill, clothing, drawing style and earthy palette.
- Actions readable; prop ownership and scene layout remain coherent.
- Payoff has breathing room; no forced comment question.
- Adjacent shots match or have an explicitly planned time jump.
- Generated footage contains no writing; subtitles are a post-production step.
- Voice/reference limitations are disclosed, not advertised as guarantees.
- One approved voice identity spans the complete narration master or relevant native-speech prompts, separate from the prehistoric score and cast design; alternative voices may be chosen for other stories.
