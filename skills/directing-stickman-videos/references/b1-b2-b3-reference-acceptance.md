# B1/B2/B3 · Reference effects, capability and actual-film acceptance

Required for **B1, B2 and B3** before planning, sample approval and full-film delivery. In Codex, Claude Code or another environment with a complete production toolchain, target an actual result comparable to the selected public sample: drawing/asset finish, acting and contact, local motion, visual hierarchy, editing, semantic captions and sound. A host or model name does not establish capability or guarantee quality; inspect the tools and the rendered result. A successful fixture, render, pixel-change check or CRT treatment is never a substitute for this target.

This contract supplements [common B production QA](autopilot-production-qa.md) and the selected [B1](b1-minimal-handdrawn.md), [B2](b2-colorful-handdrawn.md) or [B3](b3-television.md) guide. Keep their independent visual languages. Adapt equivalent design to the story, language and requested ratio; do not copy the sample's script, cast, props, scene order or English font onto every new subject.

## Resolve actual production capability

Inspect local editing/composition, artwork generation or supplied assets, layer/pose authoring, voice, alignment, fonts, SFX/mixing and actual playback/listening. Distinguish unavailable tools, untested access, missing adapters and an observed failure. Reuse a suitable configured tool; when authorized, build a small project-local adapter instead of assuming a missing bundled adapter means the service is unusable. Preserve approved voice/assets, host permissions and cost boundaries; no forced paid calls or automatic paid retries.

Record `reference_review.author_id` and `reference_review.capability` in the project. Its `mode` is `sample_target` or `limited`; record the actual `environment` and `limitations` entries with `capability`, `observed` evidence and the resulting `impact`. Do not select `limited` merely because story-specific artwork, rigging, sound or review takes work. A capable production path must not quietly downgrade to demo poses, crude symbols, static slides, camera drift or television noise.

When an observed tool limitation genuinely prevents the target, continue the authorized useful work and disclose the best attainable result. State which effects differ, which checks remain unknown and what would be needed to improve them. A playable **limited best effort** may be handed over with those limitations; it does not receive a sample-equivalent pass. Do not force unavailable capabilities, change provider/voice without authorization or describe a prompt package, missing track or partial render as a completed film. An actual tool limitation and a failed creative choice are different findings.

## Plan and inspect the representative sample

Plan from the actual narration and semantic caption timings. Select a representative approximately 20-second opening that includes the story's difficult acting/contact, information transition and sound—not only the easiest attractive hold. Plan body/end quality at the same time. Each shot needs the focal idea, action/state changes, necessary contact/occlusion, caption cues and SFX, with continuous coverage of the reviewed range.

- **B1:** compare slender hand-drawn proportions, varied readable whole-body expressions, fine line/prop detail, near-white faint-grid paper, hand-lettered hierarchy and voice-led, restrained finishing. In portrait, compare the approved sequence's meaning-led text/diagram/prop reveals and local actions through a readable held result. Flattening a staged build into an already-complete layout loses an accepted effect; the exact Ryan plot, card count and times are not mandatory templates.
- **B2:** compare warm paper, irregular fine ink, muted varied colors, recognizable illustrated environments/props, simple readable figures and small story-led reactions. Preserve handwritten scene hierarchy and dark semantic captions on the compact ivory label. Check meaningful prop/information/state progression, varied story-specific staging, contact and ownership across cuts; a new topic in the same room with changed labels is insufficient. Stable readable poses and person-free object/diagram shots are valid; do not import B1's exaggerated acting/proportion recipe, require constant limb articulation or add B3's television finish. Avoid a close-up isolated thin-line arm when a contextual or person-free view communicates the action better.
- **B3:** compare refined rounded white/gray artwork, readable silhouettes, supported movement and occlusion, progressive relationships/diagrams, varied viewpoints/details and restrained television finish. Review local motion and editing grammar separately: animated ears over the same repeated side-view layout do not establish reference-like editing. Test the hardest B3 contact/state change clean, with `television.enabled: false`, before evaluating the normal treated output.

The following observations are required on the **actual encoded MP4**, not only source layers or contact sheets:

1. **Artwork and design:** story-specific recognizable assets, coherent identity/style, clean silhouettes and appropriate detail; no placeholder fixture promoted to production art.
2. **Acting and contact:** expression, gaze, posture and weight communicate the line; hands/paws operate or support objects; approach, hide, touch and reaction are visible through their complete action, including occlusion and recovery.
3. **Motion and editing:** normal-speed local changes communicate meaning, with readable anticipation/action/result/settling and purposeful transitions; no sprite skating, disconnected limbs, repeated filler layouts or global effects used as motion coverage.
4. **Hierarchy and language:** one clear focal idea, uncluttered readable scene text and evidence; font/glyph/lettering adaptation preserves equivalent hierarchy and finish in every language. English uppercase is not a requirement for scripts without case, and a font swap alone does not preserve design quality.
5. **Captions and timing:** every cue follows semantic units and real pauses, covers the approved speech in order, fits the selected one-line B1/B2/B3 treatment and stays readable; mechanical word counts, proportional timing or no newline do not establish acceptance.
6. **Sound and rhythm:** listen to the actual mix for intelligible unchanged native-rate voice, motivated material-aware SFX, balanced beds and clean joins. Compare pacing and sound function rather than requiring the sample's voice vendor or copying its track.

An unapproved `caption_override.kind: language_fallback` is valid only when the selected style/ratio default font actually lacks glyphs in the current reviewed caption scope (B1 portrait uses the project's hand-font slot with bundled Caveat fallback; landscape B1 and B2/B3 retain Noto Sans), without changing the selected ratio's caption size (landscape B1/B2/B3 34/40/34; portrait 64/72/64); the replacement font must cover all actual captions in that scope. Changing an English caption to another aesthetic font is a restyle requiring real user approval, not a glyph fallback.

For **portrait B1–B3**, also apply the [native portrait acceptance contract](b1-b2-b3-native-portrait.md). The exact encoded scope needs independent 1× playback at a 320–480px-wide phone viewport and per-shot passes for native composition, caption readability, visible action/contact and platform-safe placement. Numeric type size, safe-box metadata, a contact sheet or a zoomed desktop frame cannot supply this review. Preserve the selected style's restrained caption design; B4 poster effects and caption micro-animation are not a B1–B3 requirement.

Watch the complete sample, inspect difficult intervals at normal speed, then use full-size frames and continuous clips to explain a finding. Record specific times, visible/audible observations and failures; “pass”, “looks good” or a list of file names is not a review. A continuous clip can demonstrate motion; a still can only support appearance. Fix failed scope and rebuild its evidence; do not rewrite observations to clear a detector.

## Independent review and full-film handoff

### Runtime evidence and commands

Before rendering, record the actual production author and environment in `project.json`. This is a planning fragment, not approval:

```json
"reference_review": {
  "author_id": "ACTUAL_PRODUCTION_AUTHOR",
  "capability": {
    "mode": "sample_target",
    "environment": "ACTUAL_HOST_AND_VERIFIED_TOOLS",
    "limitations": []
  }
}
```

For `limited`, list the observed `capability`, `observed` result and `impact` of each limitation; do not leave an empty list or put undisclosed limitations in `sample_target`.

`preview` and `render` extract evidence from the actual final MP4 into `output/preview-reference/` and `output/film-reference/`: `evidence.json`, an unknown `review-template.json`, each shot's start/mid/end PNGs and its complete audible `shot-N-continuous.mp4`. The ledger locks the actual video, project, scope, references and extracted artifacts. Use its exact IDs. Extracted evidence remains `pending_independent_review`; building it is not visual acceptance.

Copy the generated unknown template to a review file and fill it only after real inspection. Keep these fields bound to the current ledger: `schema`, `style`, `author_id`, `capability`, `video_sha256` and `evidence_sha256`. Record the actual independent `reviewer` (`id`, `kind: human|agent`, concrete `method`, `independent: true`); its ID must differ from the production author. `full_playback` needs actual `viewed_ranges` covering the entire submitted MP4 and concrete timed observations citing `source-video`. `baseline_comparisons` needs real nonempty intervals and observations from the selected style's available references: B1 `b1-ryan-v4`; B2 both `b2-colorful-sleep` and `b2-colorful-phone-weather`; B3 both `b3-tv-night-signal` and `b3-tv-dog`. For portrait, also compare the corresponding `b1-native-portrait-2026-11-13`, `b2-native-portrait-2026-11-13` or `b3-native-portrait-2026-11-13` video over a real nonempty interval; the existing landscape reference comparisons remain required. Reference observations cite their actual reference ID. Still and silent supporting evidence cannot substitute for audible reference playback.

Review every `shots` entry exactly once with every style-specific check:

- B1: `drawing_character`, `semantic_acting`, `contact_continuity`, `composition_evidence`, `typography_captions`, `timing_pacing`, `sound`.
- B2: `color_handdrawn_design`, `semantic_motion_states`, `contact_continuity`, `composition_progression`, `typography_captions`, `timing_pacing`, `sound`.
- B3: `silhouette_design`, `semantic_action`, `graphic_inventory`, `contact_occlusion`, `composition_editing`, `television_finish`, `typography_captions`, `timing_pacing`, `sound`.

B3 additionally applies the [semantic-line and purposeful-motion contract](b3-semantic-motion.md): the shot plan must cover cue-linked events and classified graphics, and independent review must cover each event, each graphic and visible-inventory completeness using that shot's actual continuous audible MP4. Record spoken meaning, before/process/after, deletion-test purpose and result readability for events; record correct objects, endpoints/direction, reveal timing, following/replacement, crossings and caption/subject interference for graphics. Inspect native 9:16 connections after re-layout too. These itemized findings supplement the B3 style checks; a generic `semantic_action: pass` cannot replace them. A legacy missing plan may produce a pending technical candidate but cannot be accepted. Wrong/missing targets, false semantic direction, unregistered marks and meaningless motion cannot be waived in `limited`; only sample-level effect shortfalls retain that disclosed route.

Each check needs in-shot timed `observations` with concrete `detail` and `evidence_ids` citing that shot's continuous clip, not a status word or unrelated still. Effect and sound checks also need `comparisons: [{reference_id, start, end, detail}]` to actual available references: video intervals are nonempty; still comparisons use null times; sound uses an audible video. Motion/contact/editing checks require a video reference.

In `sample_target`, every check must be `pass`. In `limited`, only style-effect checks may record an observed `unmet`; typography/captions, timing/pacing, sound and B3 graphic inventory still require a real pass. Unknown, failed, missing or blocked checks cannot be accepted. Keep `blocking_issues` empty only when no blocker remains. The CLI verifies the records and re-extracts evidence from the current MP4; it cannot verify the honesty of a reviewer or automatically judge aesthetic equivalence.

After independent sample review **and actual user approval/remaining-production authorization**:

```bash
node scripts/autopilot.mjs approve /absolute/project/project.json \
  --note "ACTUAL_USER_APPROVAL_CONTEXT" --visual-review /absolute/reviews/sample-review.json
node scripts/autopilot.mjs render /absolute/project/project.json
```

Then independently inspect the entire resulting film, fill its own film template and record that review:

```bash
node scripts/autopilot.mjs accept-visual /absolute/project/project.json \
  --review /absolute/reviews/film-review.json
```

This writes `output/film-reference-acceptance.json`: `sample_target_review_passed` with `equivalence: available_samples_reviewed`, or `best_effort_limited` with `equivalence: not_sample_equivalent` and recorded unmet effect criteria. Both retain `user_acceptance: not_inferred`. The original render report remains a technical/pending record; the separate acceptance file is the current review result. Changes to bound video, project, references or evidence invalidate review; regenerate affected evidence and actually recheck it.

### Truthful review and handoff

Use an independent human or agent who actually inspects the referenced media; the production author must not fabricate another identity or self-fill pass records. Evidence and review bind the current encoded file, project and references. Generated review templates begin **unknown**. Technical checks and file hashes cannot authenticate that playback/listening or an aesthetic judgment really happened, so the record must remain truthful.

Sample visual review and actual user approval are separate. The user still reviews the exact sample and authorizes remaining production under [Autopilot](autopilot.md#sample--approval--full-film). A review record, approval note or CLI status never invents user acceptance, extra budget or publication authority.

Apply the same standard to **every shot/caption and the complete final mix**, including body and ending. Sample approval does not cover a later downgrade. Keep continuous playback coverage, concrete baseline comparisons, scope findings and any limitations with the final-film review. Unknown or failed target checks block a sample-equivalent claim. Limited work remains explicitly limited through final handoff; passing technical validation does not erase its shortfall.

Deliver the actual MP4 and its containing-folder link, sidecars, source/checkpoint and QA summary. Distinguish technical completion, independent reference-effect review and the user's actual acceptance. Preserve valid approved audio/assets by fingerprint; revise only affected scopes. This protocol adds no paid generation, revoice, gallery replacement, external publication or installed-skill modification authority.
