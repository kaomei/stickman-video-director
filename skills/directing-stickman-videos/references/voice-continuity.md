# Shared voice continuity — all visual styles

Scope: **narrated A1–A5 projects**, including external narration and native model speech. A6/A7 retain their own voice defaults. B · Code Engine（代码引擎） uses named TTS voices under `autopilot.md`; do not copy this document's descriptive profiles into any B-style TTS request. Apply the [independent A audio timeline contract](a-audio-timeline-and-review.md) for sound assembly and final listening.

This is the common voice-identity contract for Style 1 Light/Dark, Style 2A, Style 2B and Style 3, plus Style 4 Cozy Storytelling. Voice identity belongs to the video, not to a visual style or a source clip. For a narrated finished film, default to a verified complete external narration master in the approved voice; tool-limited chunks join at semantic pauses before picture editing. Native speech remains an explicit alternative with its own mouth/performance constraints. Different videos may choose different voices; do not force every project into the tested male profile.

## Choose once in Phase A

Honor the user's voice choice. If unspecified, propose one suitable voice for approval rather than adding another mandatory setup question. Describe perceived gender/age, accent, timbre, pitch/register, resonance, texture (including breathiness/rasp), delivery/intonation, steady pace, and recording character. Name these in ordinary language, not a list of vague adjectives such as "warm, energetic, same narrator". Summarize the profile in the user's language. Resolve all attributes before producing prompts; no placeholders in production output.

For example, a male choice can become adult, General American, warm clean baritone, medium-low pitch, gentle chest resonance, no rasp/breathiness, calm conversational delivery, restrained pitch variation, steady pace, close dry studio recording. A bright female choice can instead become adult, General American, clear warm mid-high voice, gentle forward resonance, no rasp/breathiness, lively conversational delivery with controlled pitch variation, steady pace, close dry studio recording. Older, youthful or other requested voices need their own internally coherent profile; they do not inherit male/baritone traits. Alternative profiles are design examples, not independently provider-tested voices.

## Freeze once in Phase B

For actual native-speech generation, write one self-contained English voice block from the approved profile and copy it **verbatim** into every relevant standalone video prompt. Never abbreviate it to "same narrator", "continue the voice from clip 1", an ellipsis or a speaker label alone. For footage using an external narration master, do not inject this block or the narration text into video prompts. Keep the approved external voice/profile in its own synthesis plan and use only the selected voice tool's supported controls; descriptive text does not establish API support.

The following complete male block is retained as a **historically user-accepted native-audio prompt example** after a three-clip Flow listening comparison. Reuse it unchanged only when its attributes match the approved choice and native speech is actually requested:

```text
One off-screen adult male narrator throughout. General American accent, warm clean baritone, medium-low pitch, gentle chest resonance, no raspiness or breathiness. Calm conversational delivery, restrained pitch variation, steady speaking pace, and close, dry studio sound. Keep the same vocal timbre, register, accent, pace, and recording quality throughout. Emotional progression belongs to the visuals and music; do not change the narrator's voice or switch to character dialogue.
```

For a different approved voice, compose its own complete block using the same identity/pitch/texture/delivery/recording fields, then freeze that block. Do not append contradictory attributes such as "bright high-pitched youthful voice" to a low baritone or "now shout dramatically" to restrained conversational narration.

## Separate voice, performance and music

- Keep the narrator off-screen and narration audio-only unless the user explicitly approves another arrangement. A change in character gender, age, scene, costume, lighting or visual style does not recast the narrator. No spontaneous lip-synced character dialogue, added speakers or grunts presented as new spoken lines.
- Put escalation, anxiety, triumph, deadpan reaction and cinematic intensity in action, editing, music and SFX. Do not rewrite the narrator's register, timbre, texture, accent, pacing or recording treatment by scene. Natural sentence emphasis and pauses may vary within the frozen delivery, without adopting a new character voice.
- Keep the voice close and dry even in caves or large halls; location reverb belongs to environmental SFX unless another recording treatment was approved. Preserve voice-first mixing. Music/SFX may follow their own approved arc; they do not override this voice contract. Style 3's primitive-percussion or reaction-pause rules change music/timing, not the frozen voice.
- Fit the complete VO to the approved final story timeline, actual speaking time and natural pauses; fixed word counts and ten-second examples are not timing requirements. Do not cut words or speed the approved voice to fit retained visual scraps. Any wording or global voice change needs the relevant approval; preserve unchanged approved narration by fingerprint. If native speech is used, update its affected relevant voice blocks consistently rather than changing only one speaker instance.
- For explicitly approved multi-speaker dialogue, freeze a separate complete profile per recurring speaker and keep exact line-to-speaker assignments. Repeat each speaking role's full profile only in relevant native-speech prompts; an external dialogue plan keeps its profiles separately. Preserve native speech and mouth performance as a synchronized unit through trims. Do not add multi-speaker dialogue unrequested. For an explicitly silent video, omit speech instead of inserting a default narrator.

## Check and retest

Before delivery, compare the relevant native-speech prompt blocks for exact equality per approved narrator/speaker. Do not fail an external-VO footage prompt for omitting a voice block. Check for conflicting directions, exact complete lines and correct speaker assignments; keep generated speech-bubble/subtitle bans separate from requested post-production captions. Align subtitles to actual final speech, with semantic phrasing and no missing, repeated or reordered words.

When generation is authorized, listen to actual native clips or the assembled external narration for perceived speaker identity, timbre, register, accent, pace, texture, complete words and natural joins. Successful generation or screenshots do not prove consistency. For native drift, inspect missing/abbreviated/contradictory voice wording first; an authorized targeted retest may hold story, visuals, exact lines, music and settings fixed while correcting only the voice instruction. Keep the baseline and respect the confirmed retry budget. Prefer an independent sound repair when it preserves accepted picture; do not regenerate accepted shots just to replace off-screen VO. Native lip-bound dialogue may require repairing the affected synchronized utterance. An extra paid retry outside scope/cap needs authorization. Prompt-only delivery requires no generation/listening claim. A changed finished mix requires complete normal-speed listening to the final encoded file under the shared audio contract; absent listening capability is `not_checked`.

The Style 2A Hope test on 2026-10-12 used three independent 10-second Omni 1.1 Flash generations. Only this voice block was changed; the user reported that the new clips sounded like one person and requested the shared rule. This is sample-specific user listening acceptance, not a universal guarantee. Other voice profiles and styles have not had separate provider-backed audio comparisons. Apply the shared instruction contract to all styles as requested while keeping that evidence boundary explicit.

The historical test above supports that particular native prompt and user-accepted comparison, not guaranteed cross-clip music continuity, universal voice matching or a final-film listening pass. Voice/audio references are optional controls when supplied and supported; never claim an unattached reference was used. For finished narration, the default independent complete master replaces the old optional-external-VO fallback; preserve the selected approved voice and any explicitly accepted audio.
