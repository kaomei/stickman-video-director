# A1 Classic Minimalist · Prompt structure and approved Flow examples

These five original Flow prompts were supplied and confirmed by the project owner as a set of successful A1 examples. Use the set to learn how the simple drawing, graphic transformations, character performance, and camera work together. For new prompts, follow the [A1 direction](a1-a5-direction.md), [Omni Flash prompt contract](omni-flash-prompt-contract.md), and [A audio timeline](a-audio-timeline-and-review.md).

## Reference inputs

A standalone prompt can open a new scene from text alone. When an approved image helps preserve drawing or prop layout, follow the [A reference-image and first-frame guidance](a-production-and-review.md#reference-images-and-first-frames). For a direct continuation, use the last approved frame that will actually remain in the edit when the platform supports a start frame.

## Structure for new A1 prompts

Use this structure by default for both A1 Light and Dark. Open with one sentence specifying the source duration, aspect ratio and planned audio. Then use `STYLE`, `TIMED SEQUENCE`, `AUDIO` and `CONSTRAINTS`, in that order.

1. **STYLE fixes the drawing.** Repeat the full [character description for the selected variant](style-catalog.md#2-style-1-classic-minimalist) in every standalone prompt. Use pure white with black line art for Light and pure black with white line art for Dark. Keep the circular head, proportions, connected limbs and uniform medium line weight consistent. Describe each key prop with a few flat contours, such as a folded page with one crease. Choose props whose shape or behavior expresses the idea and gives the character something to act on. Keep one visual focus per beat and avoid assembling unrelated icons. Give each accent color a clear story function on props or graphic effects, with enough open space for the action to read.
2. **TIMED SEQUENCE follows the figure's actions.** Start with a concrete first frame that establishes the situation or key prop. Bring the figure into the action in the opening beat. Build a chain in which each major beat introduces a new visual metaphor or changes the situation. A push flattens a pressure mark, that pressure travels, and its return changes what the figure does next. Carry the grip, contact point, moving edge, direction or visible consequence into the next beat. Keep one main action readable at a time. Cut close to show contact, follow the motion to show where it goes, or cut wide to reveal what changed. Preserve the figure's proportions and the direction of the action across cuts.
3. **The final beat earns its time.** Show one more action or graphic change with a visible consequence before the final hold. A blocked gap can become a bridge the figure actually steps onto. A run along an unchanged line needs a visible outcome to serve as the payoff. For a continuation, specify the outgoing pose or graphic state and carry its position, scale, direction and momentum into the next prompt. Plan meaningful visual changes every few seconds. The 0–3, 3–7 and 7–10 second ranges below are an example. Set beat lengths around the action and give the ending time for its change and result.
4. **AUDIO follows the project plan.** Cue sound effects at visible contacts, changes and landings. If native narration is selected, supply the exact approved line separately and retain the established voice. Follow the [A audio timeline](a-audio-timeline-and-review.md) for edited narration, music and effects. The two trials below use effects only.
5. **CONSTRAINTS make the limits explicit.** Specify flat two-dimensional outlines and solid accent fills. Exclude depth, extrusion, bevels, gradients, surface texture, duplicated or disconnected limbs and unapproved screen text. Describe icons as icon-only where needed. Read the visual beats back as a silent storyboard. The viewer should be able to tell what the figure does, what each action changes, and how the last beat resolves or hands off the story.

The original examples below preserve the supplied timing, model settings, native narration, palettes, and any specifically approved text. Use the structure with the current project's duration, platform settings, palette, text policy, and sound plan.

### New-topic trial prompts

These two text-only Flow trial prompts illustrate the structure for different topics. The approved original sample prompts follow below.

#### Light · Turning an obstacle into a bridge

```text
Create one 10-second horizontal 16:9 kinetic motion-graphics clip with synchronized action sound effects.

STYLE:
One uniform digitally pure white canvas. Use flat black-outlined props. Vivid red marks wasted effort and electric blue marks the point where a lever changes the force. Keep the silhouettes clean and separate.
Use minimalist two-dimensional black line animation. Show the same consistent stick figure with a hollow circular head, no face, no eyes, no mouth, no hair, no clothing, no filled body, stable proportions, connected line limbs, and uniform medium line weight. Exactly two arms and two legs are visible per figure. Each limb appears only in its current pose: raising or lowering the right arm replaces that arm's previous line, never leaving a resting arm beneath an active arm. The figure stays black; accent colors affect only props and graphic effects.

TIMED SEQUENCE:
[0–3s] Wide side view. A tall black-outlined slab blocks a gap between two black floor lines. The figure braces and pushes against the left face. A short red compression mark flattens at the contact point. The slab stays still. As the figure steps back, reveal a long thin outlined rod and a small blue triangle on the ground beside it.
[3–7s] Cut in on the action. The figure places the blue triangle below the rod, tucks the short end of the rod under a corner of the slab, and presses the long end down with both hands. Cut wide as the slab pivots to the right in the picture plane. Keep the triangle still and the slab's outline intact.
[7–10s] Follow the slab as it rotates from upright to flat across the gap, locking against the far edge on a crisp impact. The figure plants one foot on the near end. A single flat blue line travels across the top of the slab to the far floor, then cut wide to reveal the two sides now connected. Finish on the bridge with the figure standing on it.

AUDIO:
A brief scrape and stop, a click when the rod meets the support, then a rising creak, a solid impact and a light footstep. Sound effects only.

CONSTRAINTS:
Flat 2D side view. No perspective, extrusion, filled bodies, shadows, gradients, grain, readable text, logos, captions or color labels. Keep the canvas pure white in every frame.
```

#### Dark · Releasing pressure

```text
Create one 10-second horizontal 16:9 kinetic motion-graphics clip with synchronized action sound effects.

STYLE:
Pure black canvas, with pure white outlines. The only accent is danger red, used for flat pressure marks. Keep the staging spare and readable.
Use minimalist two-dimensional white line animation. Show the same consistent stick figure with a hollow circular head, no face, no eyes, no mouth, no hair, no clothing, no filled body, stable proportions, connected line limbs, and uniform medium line weight. Exactly two arms and two legs are visible per figure. Each limb appears only in its current pose: raising or lowering the right arm replaces that arm's previous line, never leaving a resting arm beneath an active arm. The figure stays white; accent colors affect only props and graphic effects.

TIMED SEQUENCE:
[0–3s] Medium side view. One short red zigzag pulses out of a narrow slit in a flat white line. The figure kneels and slides a white-outlined cover over the slit. Cut close on contact. The zigzag vanishes, and a short red pulse shoots sideways under the line from the sealed slit.
[3–7s] Track with the pulse, then cut wide. The red pulse reaches the ends of the white line and breaks out as two tall red zigzags, one on either side of the figure. The figure stands between them and recoils. The same cover rattles at the center as the two red forms lean inward.
[7–10s] The figure pulls the cover aside. The red forms drop at once and race back toward the open slit as two flat traces. The returning traces meet and release as one clean red wave that sweeps up and out of frame. Behind it, the open slit gives one small, steady pulse. The figure straightens beside it. Land on the contrast between the earlier towering zigzags and the small pulse.

AUDIO:
A short warning tick, a dry click when the cover slides shut, a rising vibration, then one clean release and a quiet final pulse. Sound effects only.

CONSTRAINTS:
Draw every object as a flat shape or line on solid black. No text, subtitles, logos, gradients, glow, textures, shadows, depth, 3D or motion trails on limbs.
```

## Dark examples

The shared handoff is the red prison bars twisting down at the end of Clip 2 and becoming vines at the start of Clip 3. Clip 2 opens on the violet lines from the preceding clip. Its brief “WHAT IF?” overlay is specific to that prompt.

### Clip 2 · Thoughts become a prison

```text
Create exactly one 10-second horizontal 16:9 kinetic motion-graphics video with synchronized native audio.

STYLE:
Pure black background. Use the identical minimalist white 2D design for Stick Figure A: circular head, no face, no clothes, fixed proportions, and constant medium line thickness.

Use anxiety violet #8B5CF6 for imaginary thoughts and danger red #FF3B30 for failure, rejection, and confinement. Keep the visual style strictly consistent.

CONTINUITY:
Begin inside the same rotating violet lines that filled the ending of Clip 1.

TIMED SEQUENCE:
[0.0–3.0s] The camera flies through the tangle from left to right. A circular thought bubble opens on the left. Inside it, a miniature A attempts to run, trips, and falls as the ground cracks.

[3.0–7.0s] Move horizontally through three fear scenarios. In the left section, a crowd points at A. In the center, an envelope is hit by a giant red X. On the right, a huge red hand stamps another X over A’s work. The scenarios collapse toward the center while the real A covers its head.

[7.0–10.0s] Red panels slide inward from both sides and form a wide prison around A. Briefly display the exact text “WHAT IF?” above the prison. The red bars stretch downward and begin twisting at the final frame.

VOICEOVER:
Use the same bright adult female American-English narrator. Read exactly:

“Before the first step, you picture failure, judgment, rejection, and every possible way things could go wrong. Your thoughts turn possibilities into prisons.”

AUDIO:
Continue the minor piano. Add rushing wind, a restrained fall impact, indistinct crowd whispers, two sharp stamp sounds, and a heavy metallic prison slam. Keep narration dominant.

CONSTRAINTS:
No style changes, faces, clothing, extra limbs, broken proportions, photorealism, 3D rendering, random text, captions, subtitles, logos, or watermarks. The only readable text is “WHAT IF?”. Do not alter or repeat the voiceover.
```

### Clip 3 · Bars turn into vines

```text
Create exactly one 10-second horizontal 16:9 kinetic motion-graphics video with synchronized native audio.

STYLE:
Use a pure black background and the identical white minimalist Stick Figure A. Preserve its circular head, fixed proportions, no facial features, no clothing, and uniform line thickness.

Use danger red #FF3B30 for vines, anxiety violet #8B5CF6 for intrusive thoughts, and action gold #FFD54A only at the end.

HORIZONTAL COMPOSITION:
Keep A in the left third while obstacles, questions, and possible forward movement occupy the center and right side. Use left-to-right movement as the direction of progress.

CONTINUITY:
Begin with the same red prison bars twisting downward from Clip 2.

TIMED SEQUENCE:
[0.0–3.0s] The red bars transform into vines and coil around A’s ankles. A tries to step toward the right side of the screen, but the vines pull the body sharply back to the left.

[3.0–7.0s] The floor becomes a horizontal treadmill moving left. A walks toward the right but remains trapped in the same position. Violet question marks multiply across the empty space ahead. The camera tilts and the edges of the widescreen frame compress toward A.

[7.0–10.0s] The vines tighten while the question marks form a tunnel blocking the right side. A thin gold beam suddenly slices horizontally from the far left to the far right, cutting through the tunnel and stopping all camera rotation. End with the gold beam spanning the entire width.

VOICEOVER:
Use the same bright adult female narrator. Emphasize the two questions anxiously, then become firm. Read exactly:

“Then come the questions: What if I fail? What will people think? The more you search for certainty, the tighter fear wraps around your feet.”

AUDIO:
Use accelerating heartbeat, vine creaks, treadmill friction, nonverbal whispers, rotating wind, and a clean cutting sound when the gold beam appears. Keep the narration clear.

CONSTRAINTS:
No faces, clothes, extra limbs, distorted anatomy, changing line weight, new characters, photorealism, 3D rendering, subtitles, captions, logos, watermarks, or readable text. Speak only the supplied voiceover.
```

## Light examples

These cover an opening, a reward loop, and a recovery ending. The recovery begins from the falling phone at the end of its preceding clip, as specified in the original prompt.

### Opening · Screen, book and phone

```text
Create an approximately 10-second 16:9 animated video, 1280×720, 24 FPS, with synchronized English narration, background music, and sound effects.

Use a completely flat, uniform, digitally pure white background in every frame. The background must look like a blank white digital canvas, with no gray tint, off-white color, texture, shadow, gradient, vignette, lighting, floor shading, environmental depth, or colored sky.

Use minimalist two-dimensional black line animation. Show one consistent stick figure with a hollow circular head, no face, no eyes, no mouth, no hair, no clothing, no filled body, stable proportions, connected line limbs, and uniform medium line weight.

Use only three solid accent colors: vivid energetic red for digital stimulation, bright electric blue for attention and self-control, and warm golden yellow for purposeful action. These colors are visual art direction only. Never display color specifications, palette labels, technical annotations, hashtags, or production instructions inside the video.

Do not generate any visible words, letters, numbers, captions, subtitles, interface text, or written characters. Every screen, symbol, meter, and notification must be icon-only.

Use wide left-center-right staging, rapid visual changes, kinetic transformations, lateral camera movement, and frequent visual events while keeping the drawing style and character completely consistent.

FIRST FRAME:
Begin with an empty pure-white digital canvas. A small black line-art stick figure stands at the center.

[0–3 seconds]
A large black-outline cinema screen rapidly unfolds on the left. The stick figure sits in front of it. Two red fast-forward arrow symbols shoot repeatedly across the screen while a simple icon-only clock above it spins rapidly. The clock must have hands but no numbers. The camera quickly pushes from the left toward the figure.

[3–7 seconds]
The cinema screen folds into a giant open book stretching across the center. The figure attempts to read, but its head repeatedly droops. Black page lines rise and become heavy curved pressure lines pushing down on the figure. A blue attention ring beside the figure rapidly shrinks and breaks into fragments. The camera slides from center toward the right.

[7–10 seconds]
A black-outline smartphone snaps upward on the right. The figure instantly straightens and reaches toward it. A dense stream of red notification circles, play symbols, swipe arrows, and icon-only content cards erupts from the phone. The stream races from right to left and expands until it crosses the entire frame.

EXACT AUDIO-ONLY DIALOGUE:
“Have you noticed your patience disappearing? A two-hour movie needs double speed, while a single page makes you sleepy within five minutes.”

Speak the dialogue exactly once. Do not add, omit, repeat, paraphrase, or reorder any word. Do not display the dialogue visually.

Use one consistent adult male narrator with natural American English, a clear mid-low register, steady and intense delivery, confident but not theatrical, and a brisk controlled pace. Begin inquisitively and gradually become more urgent.

Use a restrained, slightly anxious electronic pulse with a faint ticking rhythm. Synchronize page flips, clock ticks, fast-forward swishes, the phone snap, notification pops, and the rushing information stream. Keep narration louder than the music and effects.

FINAL FRAME:
End with the red icon-only information stream crossing the pure-white frame from right to left. Preserve its direction, height, scale, and speed for the opening of the next clip.

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN:
Any visible text, letters, numbers, technical notation, color labels, gray background, off-white background, colored sky, paper texture, grain, gradients, shadows, ambient occlusion, realistic lighting, bloom, fog, three-dimensional rendering, facial features, filled human bodies, clothing, malformed limbs, disconnected lines, changing proportions, changing line weight, logos, watermarks, captions, or subtitles.
```

### Reward loop · Red capsule to blank receipt

```text
Create an approximately 10-second 16:9 animated video, 1280×720, 24 FPS, with synchronized English narration and sound effects.

Use a completely flat, uniform, digitally pure white background in every frame. The background must look like a blank white digital canvas, with no gray tint, texture, shadows, gradient, vignette, lighting, floor shading, or environmental depth.

Use minimalist two-dimensional black line animation. Show one consistent stick figure with a hollow circular head, no face, no eyes, no mouth, no hair, no clothing, no filled body, stable proportions, connected line limbs, and uniform medium line weight.

Use only three solid accent colors: vivid energetic red for instant digital rewards, bright electric blue for attention and self-control, and warm golden yellow for positive action. These colors are visual art direction only. Never display color specifications, palette labels, technical annotations, hashtags, or production instructions inside the video.

Do not generate any visible words, letters, numbers, captions, subtitles, interface text, or written characters. Every notification and content card must be icon-only.

Use rapid visual changes, kinetic transformations, lateral camera movement, and frequent visual events while keeping the drawing style and stick figure completely consistent.

FIRST FRAME:
A single flat red capsule-shaped reward icon floats in the center of the pure-white frame.

[0–3 seconds]
The red capsule snaps open. A black-outline smartphone, a simple line-art couch, a game-controller icon, notification circles, play symbols, and several icon-only content cards burst outward. The stick figure drops onto the couch and catches the phone. Keep the couch entirely as black outlines with no shading or filled surfaces.

[3–7 seconds]
A red conveyor belt races horizontally across the frame. Icon-only entertainment cards, game symbols, play buttons, notification dots, and novelty symbols arrive one after another. The figure remains seated while one arm swipes rapidly. Each swipe creates a small flat red reward pulse. A blue attention ring beside the figure repeatedly shrinks.

[7–10 seconds]
The conveyor belt accelerates into a continuous loop around the figure. The camera pulls backward to reveal that the figure is trapped inside an endless stream of rewards. All red icons suddenly compress into one long red receipt shape, which shoots directly toward the camera and fills the final frame. The receipt must contain no writing or symbols.

EXACT AUDIO-ONLY DIALOGUE:
“A phone, a comfortable couch, and endless recommendations keep delivering rewards without effort: games, gossip, novelty, another swipe, all on repeat.”

Speak the dialogue exactly once. Do not add, omit, repeat, paraphrase, or reorder any word. Do not display the dialogue visually.

Use the same adult male narrator with natural American English, a clear mid-low register, steady and intense delivery, confident but not theatrical.

Add a restrained electronic rhythm with synchronized capsule snaps, object pops, swipes, notification impacts, conveyor movement, and the final receipt whoosh. Keep the narration louder than the music and sound effects.

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN:
Any visible text or technical notation, gray background, off-white background, paper texture, grain, gradients, shadows, ambient occlusion, realistic lighting, three-dimensional rendering, facial features, filled human bodies, clothing, malformed limbs, changing proportions, changing line weight, logos, watermarks, captions, or subtitles.
```

### Recovery · Put the phone down and walk outside

```text
Create an approximately 10-second 16:9 animated video, 1280×720, 24 FPS, with synchronized English narration, background music, and sound effects.

Use a completely flat, uniform, digitally pure white background in every frame. The background must look like a blank white digital canvas, with no gray tint, off-white color, texture, shadow, gradient, vignette, lighting, floor shading, environmental depth, or colored sky.

Use minimalist two-dimensional black line animation. Show the same consistent stick figure with a hollow circular head, no face, no eyes, no mouth, no hair, no clothing, no filled body, stable proportions, connected line limbs, and uniform medium line weight.

Use only three solid accent colors: vivid energetic red for digital temptation, bright electric blue for attention and regained control, and warm golden yellow for action and recovery. These colors are visual art direction only. Never display color specifications, palette labels, technical annotations, hashtags, or production instructions inside the video.

Do not generate any visible words, letters, numbers, captions, subtitles, interface text, or written characters. Every meter, notification, and symbolic object must be icon-only.

Use wide left-center-right staging, purposeful lateral movement, rapid visual changes, kinetic transformations, and frequent visual events while keeping the character and line style completely consistent.

FIRST FRAME:
Begin with the exact same giant black-outline smartphone falling vertically through the center of the frame. Match its position, size, angle, and downward momentum to the end of Clip 5.

[0–3 seconds]
The stick figure steps in from the left and catches the falling phone. Red notification circles and icon-only content cards continue bursting from the device, pulling the figure’s arm downward. The figure pauses for half a beat, straightens its posture, and deliberately turns the phone screen away.

[3–7 seconds]
The figure places the phone onto a simple black ground line. All red notifications collapse back into the phone and disappear. A bright blue control ring sweeps outward from the figure. A black-outline book opens on the left, a simple training weight rises on the right, and a blue flowing thought path forms above the figure. None of these objects contains writing.

[7–10 seconds]
A warm yellow path draws itself toward the right side of the frame. The figure takes several strong steps along it. A flat yellow sun symbol and a simple black-outline tree unfold ahead. A blue attention meter made only from solid bars refills beside the figure. The camera pulls backward into a calm, wide final composition.

EXACT AUDIO-ONLY DIALOGUE:
“Real strength begins with friction: reading, training, thinking, waiting. Take back control now—close this video, put down the phone, and walk outside.”

Speak the dialogue exactly once. Do not add, omit, repeat, paraphrase, or reorder any word. Do not display the dialogue visually.

Use the same adult male narrator with natural American English, a clear mid-low register, steady and intense delivery, confident but not theatrical, and a brisk controlled pace. Transition from firm instruction to motivating resolve. Deliver the final words as a direct call to immediate action.

Replace the mechanical pulse with a firm, hopeful rhythmic track while maintaining a compatible tempo. Synchronize the phone catch, notification collapse, phone placement, blue control sweep, book opening, weight lift, walking steps, and final camera pullback. Keep narration louder than the music and effects. End with one clean musical resolve.

FINAL FRAME:
End on a wide pure-white composition. The phone rests behind the figure on the left. The figure walks purposefully toward the right along the yellow path. The flat yellow sun and black-outline tree are ahead. Hold this final composition briefly.

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN:
Any visible text, letters, numbers, technical notation, color labels, gray background, off-white background, colored sky, paper texture, grain, gradients, shadows, ambient occlusion, realistic lighting, bloom, fog, three-dimensional rendering, facial features, filled human bodies, clothing, malformed limbs, disconnected lines, changing proportions, changing line weight, logos, watermarks, captions, or subtitles.
```
