# B5: optional V5 physical-sequence study and internal repair loop

Use this optional worked case when a particular physical sequence needs contact ownership, retained support or a changed attempt. The user called dog-lap V5 **much better**, then rejected the later new-story result for distorted figures and continuous-acting tempo. Its local improvements must not become the whole-film default. Begin new B5 direction with the [verified Mei scene structure](b5-crazymei-directing-structure.md) and [fillable template](b5-mei-scene-template.md). CrazyMei + Spider remain the targets in both ratios; V5 is not a new accepted baseline or historical Mei prompt.

## For an event that actually depends on an occupied task

Find the story's observable task or physical situation: carrying, pouring, reaching, balancing, waiting under a load, settling, resisting. Decide what each hand owns and where body weight is supported. Draw this before state at the intended film scale. Keep that task only for as long as this physical event needs it. Another main visual may replace it at a semantic transition. For a calm subject, a small constraint may be appropriate; enlarge its readability rather than inventing violence or certainty the script does not contain.

Direct this chain before drawing incidental furniture:

`task → interruption/force → propagated response → retained constraint → changed attempt → resolution`

This is an event-local diagnostic model, not a whole-film formula. Do not use it to force every spoken idea into the same room, task or constraint. A scene may transition to a bold graphic, metaphor, new gag or visual replacement. Changing only the heading while replaying a movement still fails.

## Author in this order

1. **Source model, then three readable poses:** preserve source head/trunk/limb proportions and line weight; compare source and new figures at the same head diameter. A fixed but elongated skeleton fails. Then draw the before state, largest consequence and changed end. Draw them at delivery scale with title/presenter/caption safe areas already reserved. Check at roughly 320px-wide display size. If the action reads only after zooming into a tiny hand, improve framing or pose before tweening.
2. **Shared contacts and ownership:** name each surface/hand that owns the contact. Evaluate that point once; derive the attached hand, paw/prop and sound anchor from it. Compute support in world space after body transforms. A planted sole must remain on the surface while the body rotates; counter-transform its orientation if needed. Keep normal bone lengths and plausible elbows, then inspect silhouettes: reachable IK can still draw an ugly pose.
3. **Piecewise leading path, independent reactions:** choose event-local phase endpoints and unequal curves after the scene event relay is planned. Short decisive travel must contrast with loading and result reading. Separate paper/material, face, feet, torso and optional camera tracks. Use bounded, event-driven accents rather than permanent motion.
4. **Preserve local physical continuity:** keep constraints/ownership until release or handoff within the action. At a semantic transition, intentionally clear or transform the stage and hand off to the next main visual; a whole-film retained task is not required. A closer camera must preserve the cause, support, face/extreme and titles that the viewer needs. Crop only at the real stage/canvas boundary, not an arbitrary internal line through the actor.
5. **Inspect, revise, then encode:** follow the internal loop below. Keep already valid identity, audio, captions and unrelated shots. Record exactly which interval changed and why; regenerate only affected evidence plus the resulting candidate as required by its hashes.

## Internal repair loop: the agent owns it

Do not send a weak first render to the user as a way to discover obvious defects. The user's brief is sufficient to start internal planning, rendering, review and reversible repairs. A user reminder is not a production stage.

Create a small contact sheet of the actual composite, visual-event before/result/handoff strips and six-phase strips for physical events, then open full frames for ambiguous contacts/extremes. Give an independent reviewer the brief, both primary target references and actual candidate evidence. Ask for specific failed intervals and visible defects, not a checklist of approving adjectives. Record first-attempt defects and actual revisions in the project checkpoint. Do not pre-fill passes. Re-render the failed interval and adjoining boundaries; after encode, verify the actual encoded frames there. Complete continuous silent-stage and audiovisual review remains required by the existing preview gate.

Use these failure-to-repair decisions:

| Observed failure | Repair the picture or timing |
| --- | --- |
| More props, unchanged main visual | Re-direct the main-stage event/transition. For physical acting, make force change support, balance or grip. For graphics, make the reveal/transformation change the premise. |
| Smooth but slow, everything peaks together | Shorten decisive travel relative to preparation/recovery; give material and expression their own delayed tracks. Do not speed up approved speech. |
| Next scene feels like another icon demonstration | Make the new visual take over the stage through a meaningful path, transformation or replacement. Retain a prior constraint only when this physical action actually needs it. |
| Whole film feels like minor movements in a continuous domestic scene | Return to the four-field Mei scene template; choose distinct major visual events and semantic handoffs before refining limbs. |
| Contact is technically close but visually tangled | Repose/re-layer hands and props; expose the shared target and check normal-size silhouette. |
| Fixed but elongated actor | Restore the source model proportions; re-stage target and supports around a normal reach. Do not enlarge the head to disguise long limbs. |
| Correct IK with a pointed/robotic elbow | Change shoulder/root, bend side or task target. Numerical reachability is necessary, not an anatomy review. |
| Camera hides a planted foot, head or extreme | Change native framing or stage placement. Do not mask the defect with an interior crop. |
| Rest beat feels empty | Check that the viewer sees a changed premise and a purposeful exit. Add a specific attempted continuation only if the meaning supports it; don't add random wiggles. |

V5's internal review caught a too-similar BEFORE view, premature book closure that erased the task, a high elbow, title/lamp overlap, sloped planted paws and an internal crop. It repaired those pictures instead of strengthening the report's wording. These are examples of what to look for, not six universally required defects.

If an essential review capability is unavailable, record its exact missing scope and keep the acceptance pending. Never claim listening or complete playback from source code, sampled frames or a decode check. An explicitly requested cold-start evaluation can expose its diagnostic film with those limits; it does not silently become preview-ready or a new approved baseline.

## What a first-use test proves

A real cold start uses a clean installed copy, a fresh executor with no project/chat history, and only a new raw brief plus authorized inputs. Do not supply the intended choreography, prior repair notes or the expected answer outside this skill. Let the executor discover this workflow through the normal B5 entry. It must produce a film, not only action cards. Preserve the raw brief, first candidate, internal review, repairs, final encoded candidate, input/package hashes and honest remaining gaps. A failed test is evidence for another repair, not a pass with better prose.

One completed new-story test supports that tested setup; it does not guarantee every story, voice provider or native ratio. Report whether the user had to add reminders and whether the agent actually fixed its own observed defects. Documentation tests and a runnable V5 reconstruction are useful, but neither substitutes for this new-story film test.
