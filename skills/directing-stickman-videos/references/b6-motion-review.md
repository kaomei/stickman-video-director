# B6 motion execution and review

Use this gate with the [Sample 1 detailed acceptance matrix](b6-sample-1-acceptance.md) when authoring a B6 film or revising its art, acting, space, camera, rhythm, captions or sound. Its purpose is to catch a specific failure: a correctly encoded film with valid limbs can still lose the demonstrated animation quality. A shot name, a description such as “depth approach”, and the count of camera cuts are not evidence that the effect exists.

**Animation performance and spatial richness are mandatory outcomes, judged against Sample 1 portrait Eiffel V44.** A known shortfall keeps the task active: correct the responsible local asset/code, encode a fresh preview and inspect it again. A `pending` or `needs_repair` preview is a work product, not completed delivery or a reason to wait for the user. Stop only for an explicit interim-preview request, an explicit stop, or an evidenced blocker that prevents further authorized work. Existing cost, upload and publication permissions still apply; this loop does not authorize a new paid call.

## Lock the relevant moving reference

**Portrait (9:16) works best. Sample 1's portrait Eiffel V44 is the user-selected strict primary standard.** Inspect the actual installed `assets/b6/reference/eiffel-portrait-v44.mp4`, not only its poster or the gallery's landscape version. Save its path/hash, format and the ranges that demonstrate the required drawing, light, spatial reveal, travel/braking, contact, eyeline/emotion, object follow or payoff hold. The canonical `assets/b6/style-standard.json` records the selection. Sample 2 V90 supplies secondary scene-specific binding/engineering evidence; its finer limbs cannot replace Sample 1's relative stroke weight or the primary film's animation standard. A successful engine encode never establishes the whole style.

Before approving a new character or action, compare uncluttered arms/legs in similar views at **matched head width**, keeping the original body proportions. Record which decoded candidate/reference frames were used, how their scale was matched and what the stroke comparison shows. Preserve Sample 1's heavier, irregular pressure-varying painted stroke; do not invent a universal pixel width, bulk up arms into skin-filled anatomy, or hide finer lines by changing body/camera scale. Check supporting cast and successive poses as well as the main character. New identities and era-specific clothing must retain the same drawing grammar.

If the selected clip is missing after installation, locate an authorized available copy or record that comparison as unavailable. Do not silently replace the target with a weaker default or claim source fidelity from artwork alone. A new story may use different actions and shot order; preserve the relevant motion principles rather than replaying the old film.

## Direct before implementing

For each narrative beat, decide what the viewer must notice and how attention reaches it. Put the relevant reference range, intended action/camera effect, required assets and the planned verification into the native shot record. A brief table is sufficient; it is an execution contract, not a second screenplay.

- **Space:** identify the camera route, useful regions and depth/occlusion relationships. Check that the available plate, ground, near objects and masks support the requested extreme views. A wide bitmap alone does not implement depth. If the plan promises a depth move or foreground reveal, supply the projection/layer transforms and occluder that produce it.
- **Acting:** stage the body's contribution, gaze, contact and release before fitting wrist targets. For a large gesture, show how anticipation, weight/shoulder change, action and recovery create a readable result. Choose the actual angle and face parts/states needed. An intentionally planted performance is valid; an unsupported whole-body gesture is not repaired by animating its wrists alone.
- **Camera:** assign a visual purpose, subject to follow, acceleration/braking and landing information. Keep independent actor/background paths separate from the camera. A held shot can let an expression or joke register. Choose movement because it improves the beat, not to satisfy a movement/cut quota.

Check these prerequisites before final asset preparation and before extending the timeline. Reuse compatible accepted artwork/performance. If an angle, layer or occluder is missing, repair that dependency or redesign the shot truthfully within the brief; do not leave the promised effect only in the plan.

## Verify what the code actually renders

Use a representative 5–10-second segment to exercise the story's risky contact, emotion and spatial relationship. A short static exchange does not validate wide-world camera transfer merely because it has a new cast. Reuse valid prior local tests rather than generating every test again.

Inspect fixed-camera action where needed, then its intended native camera. Review real playback at intended speed and consecutive decoded frames around contacts, cuts and expression changes. Inspect camera extremes and near-object crossings. A contact sheet locates issues; it cannot establish movement quality by itself.

For each reviewed shot, record the **actual observation and evidence**, including:

| Review question | Evidence that changes the decision |
|---|---|
| Did the promised space/camera effect happen? | Rendered time range plus the actual projection/layers, path or crossing used; identify what changes in the spatial relationship. A uniform final-canvas scale is described as a reframe, not a depth dolly. |
| Does the gesture read naturally? | Anticipation/action/contact/release/recovery moments, body and near/far arm ownership, planted feet and prop support. Explain a deliberate still pose instead of adding arbitrary sway. |
| Does the reaction develop? | Eyeline target, face/body timing and the moment the emotion becomes legible. Switching a whole head image needs an intentional visual transition, not an accidental jump. |
| Does the rhythm guide attention? | The action that motivates the move/cut, where it lands, whether information can be located/read, and whether background activity competes with it. |
| Is the native drawing/composition intact? | Full-size limbs/face crops, soft-light/brush consistency, occlusion, edge coverage, captions and preserved sound. |
| Does limb weight match the primary sample? | Decoded candidate and Sample 1 portrait frames, comparable views normalized to the same head width, and the observed relative arm/leg stroke and pressure variation. A valid skeleton or V90-style finer wire is insufficient. |

Record a verdict of pass, needs repair or a specific justified non-applicability for each relevant item; identify remaining gaps against the chosen reference. Do not mark a whole shot as matching because only its anatomy passed. Do not require every shot to move, contain a crowd or show three depth layers. The representative segment must demonstrate the capabilities being claimed.

For **spatial richness**, describe the distinct usable world regions, near/middle/far layer relationships and the actual reveal or occlusion that changes as the shot unfolds. Compare the visible result to the selected reference range. A broad plate that repeatedly shows the same patch, flat canvas enlargement, or travel with no new spatial information fails the claimed depth effect. For **animation performance**, identify the preparation, main action, contact or action peak, release/recovery, supporting body motion and gaze/emotional result in a rendered time range. A deliberate still beat is valid in context; slow wrist-only posing and decorative body wobble cannot stand in for decisive acting. Adapt these observations to the story rather than imposing a cut count, a universal travel distance or identical gesture phases on every shot.

## Review the result separately from its authoring claims

For a new story/cast/space or a user-reported quality regression, use an independent reviewer when available and permitted. Give the reviewer the brief, selected reference ranges, native plan and actual rendered segment, without the author's intended verdict. The reviewer must inspect the media and identify observable issues; reviewing only code or repeating the plan is not a visual pass. This is an agent-side review, not another user permission step.

If independent visual review is unavailable, record who inspected which playback/frames and the limitation. Unviewed effects remain pending. Never invent a playback observation, independent reviewer or user approval. The agent repairs confirmed gaps before presenting the next version as matching the target.

## Three separate decisions

1. **Technical checks:** input integrity, encode, anatomy/contact inventories, dimensions, captions and resource audit. These establish technical constraints only.
2. **Motion/visual review:** reference-aware assessment of actual space, acting, expressions, camera, rhythm and composition. Pending or needs-repair items block a claim of matched quality and extension as an accepted production.
3. **User decision:** the user's genuine decision about the exact preview and ratio. An internal review never supplies this approval.

The production runtime's review pack binds observations to the exact preview/source fingerprint. `approve` and production `render` require a current completed motion review as well as the existing genuine user decision. Iterative `preview` remains available for repairs. Passing the review-file checks confirms evidence completeness and freshness; it does not automate aesthetic judgment.

## Detailed coverage before a pass

The canonical `required_subchecks` manifest has **35 subchecks within the eight existing groups**. The [acceptance matrix](b6-sample-1-acceptance.md#mandatory-detailed-review) defines each outcome, permitted variation, inspection and failure action. Every subcheck has an actual observation, reviewed range, candidate and primary-reference playback, fingerprinted implementation evidence and separate verdict. In schema v3, `checks[category].subchecks[id]` carries `status`, `observation`, `start`, `end`, artifact `evidence`, `implementation: [{path, claim}]`, `remaining_gaps` and `confirmed_defect`; use `reason` for an allowed N/A. Each row links an actual shot MP4 and primary-reference MP4. Typography/raster/ink/line-weight observations additionally need decoded candidate and primary PNGs. An N/A still needs its actual reviewed range/clip and why the activity/contact is absent. No broad category pass or preserved hash supplies a missing detailed verdict. Only `background_independent_activity` and `contact_ownership_release` can use a concrete justified N/A; a grounded deliberate hold remains an observation.

Review the entire cast/view/near-far limb inventory, important contacts frame by frame, both sides of every cut, native-size drawing/caption/coverage details and original-speed full-range AV. The native plan must already declare `cast_inventory: [{id, role, shot_ids}]`, including static people painted into the background. The review's top-level `cast_inventory` must contain exactly the declared identities and shot memberships intersecting the actual preview range; it cannot silently omit an extra. Complete `cast_inventory_observation` from the actual picture. An empty inventory is valid only for genuinely unpeopled footage. Each shot records `cast_ids` (empty is valid for an actual environment-only shot), `observed_expression_gaze`, `observed_limb_occlusion`, `observed_space_light` and `observed_caption_audio`. The `single_stroke_fixed_limb_ownership` subcheck's `cast_coverage` records every inventory identity, covered shots/views/poses, near/far ownership, deliberate hidden-limb occlusion and evidence. A baked-in extra is still visible cast; do not omit it from the audit because it lacks a solver. An approved source still or a low-resolution contact sheet cannot certify the final animation. Keep preserved-material checks separate from primary-reference matching: the previous 42 px/bottom-centered portrait captions remained unchanged and synced, but fail the actual 47 px/baseline-914 Sample 1 match. That confirmed defect cannot be erased by a broad pass or by relabeling it as a review correction.

The approved English Archivo Black portrait caption profile is mechanically checked with its font and caption renderer bound to the input fingerprint/report. The render report supplies `caption_contract` with `profile`, `renderer_sha256`, `font_sha256`, `cues_sha256`, `observed_cues` (times/text/raster bounds) and a truthful `limitation`; review retains that binding and `report_sha256`. Raster bounds/config metadata supplement actual encoded review, not replace it. Decoded PNG evidence must still show the actual glyph size/placement/raster and phrase transitions. Caption PNG evidence must include actual **active, visibly lettered cue frames** plus adjacent frames before/after the inspected cue switch, for candidate and primary reference as applicable. An evidence set containing only subtitle-free frames cannot establish a caption pass. The machine checks artifact identity/type and contract completeness; it does not automatically read the pixels or recognize text, so the reviewer must verify the lettering and transition visibly. Encoded compression changes edge pixels; renderer colors are the locked design values, not a claim that every compressed pixel is identical. Archivo Black is the user-approved B6 default; its difference from the historical Sample 1 Arial Black lettering is not a defect and does not require a local Arial installation. The default profile still has no arbitrary font/layout override. Language/glyph needs or explicit user design changes first require a separately named implemented profile, verified font/glyph coverage and repeated profile-specific runtime/native review; recording a reason alone cannot pass. Do not silently substitute typography or edit the primary manifest to lower the standard. Landscape has independent design evidence; its starting coordinates are not Sample 1 facts.

## Runtime evidence contract

Set `project.quality_target` to the project-relative quality-target JSON. New init copies the canonical style standard and full primary portrait movie into the project. Its `reference` records the Sample 1 portrait identity, native ratio, local `path`, exact `sha256`, and selected `segments` with `start`, `end` and `purpose`; `style_standard` records drawing, relative limb weight, light, animation and format criteria. `preferred_ratio` is a recommendation, not permission to change the requested delivery ratio. Keep V90's engineering role separate. Changing the primary standard requires an explicit user direction; a new story, cast, era or landscape output does not lower it. Existing in-progress projects must carry this selection and regenerate affected review evidence before a new quality claim; preserve their existing films and genuine approval history.

After preview encoding, run:

```bash
python3 /absolute/new-story/run.py review-pack /absolute/new-story --ratio 16:9 --report outputs/preview-01/report.json --output outputs/review-01
```

This prepares decoded shot clips/frames, selected reference clips and full-size reference frames, `index.html` and `motion-review.json` without executing the authored scene. The viewer displays the primary standard's criteria. Open the actual clips and frames. The generated observations start **pending**; creating the pack does not complete the review.

Complete the generated review rather than fabricating a pass file. It binds the project `fingerprint`, `ratio`, preview path/hash/range, reference path/hash/segments and artifact hashes. `review` records status, reviewer, method and review time. Every generated `checks` entry needs its actual observation and linked evidence; a non-applicable item needs its reason. Every covered native shot needs its actual observed action/camera, evidence and verdict; a deliberate hold may record `hold_reason`. `regressions` carries the current project's known rejection checks. Preserve the generated identities and hashes, and regenerate the pack after changing the preview or locked inputs.

Schema v3 retains `full_playback`: the exact current preview hash and full range at speed 1, who watched it, the observed rhythm/audio, and the primary-reference playback evidence. Watch the actual moving preview with sound; decoded frames cannot supply this record. `space_depth` and `body_action` cannot be marked non-applicable. Their `demonstration` records the structured observations above, an actual reviewed start/end range, candidate playback and primary-reference playback. Each generated check also contains its required `subchecks`; none may be omitted, unviewed or hidden by the group verdict. The review's top-level `remaining_gaps`, and any check, subcheck, shot, regression or repair row's `remaining_gaps`, must be empty before a pass. Do not erase a visible gap to satisfy the schema. Empty, unknown or unviewed results stay pending while other useful authorized work continues. Passing the JSON contract establishes evidence completeness, not that those observations are aesthetically true.

Validate the completed internal review before presenting it as quality-ready:

```bash
python3 /absolute/new-story/run.py check-review /absolute/new-story --ratio 16:9 --report outputs/preview-01/report.json --review outputs/review-01/motion-review.json
```

The agent performs this review; the user is not asked to complete the JSON. Each capability links actual shot playback, a primary-reference excerpt, and a fingerprinted implementation file with a concrete claim. The required `limb_line_weight` check additionally needs decoded candidate **and** primary-reference PNG evidence and a nonempty `comparison_basis` recording matched-head-width normalization and comparable views. Its `observation` explains the actual stroke result. It cannot be marked non-applicable in the default standard. This verifies review evidence, not automatic visual similarity. A shot must include its own playback evidence. The default regressions explicitly reject replacing Sample 1's limb weight with Sample 2's finer lines. Add other rejected behavior to `quality-target.json` → `regressions` before authoring the replacement preview. Missing review remains pending; a written assertion never substitutes for viewing.

After the agent's completed review and the user's genuine preview decision, record approval with both files:

```bash
python3 /absolute/new-story/run.py approve /absolute/new-story --ratio 16:9 --report outputs/preview-01/report.json --review outputs/review-01/motion-review.json --note "The user's actual decision"
```

The runtime refuses missing, pending, needs-repair or stale review evidence at approval/production rendering. It rechecks the locked preview, reference and review hashes. When the user genuinely accepts a replacement preview, `approve` preserves the previous ledger under `approvals/` before recording the new decision; this never invents a new decision or changes the old video. A technical pass still cannot populate observations or supply the user's decision.

After a rejection, retain its time range, visible failure and responsible implementation in the failed review and checkpoint. Keep that review and its preview immutable. Supply it when building the replacement pack:

```bash
python3 /absolute/new-story/run.py review-pack /absolute/new-story --ratio 9:16 --report outputs/preview-02/report.json --output outputs/review-02 --review outputs/review-01/motion-review.json
```

The new pack fingerprints the previous review and carries its unresolved checks, shots and regressions into `repair_log`. The first explicit predecessor import also records a frozen failure snapshot in `.b6-review-history/`; `check-review` records failed/pending findings there as well. The project's ratio-specific history index is independent of the editable review file. Subsequent `review-pack` calls inherit the active failure automatically if `--review` is omitted. Deleting `previous_review` and `repair_log`, or linking an unrelated predecessor, cannot clear that failure. A complete validated review closes it. Preserve this directory when copying or resuming the project; do **not** add it to `project.inputs`, because review administration must not change the film's authored-input fingerprint and force another encode.

Each repair must record the actual change, a new rendered observation, the candidate playback evidence and the fingerprinted implementation responsible. Its generated `origin` binds the rejected source/video identity. The default `resolution: implementation_change` requires a changed source fingerprint, changed video hash, and—when the older source inventory exists—a changed claimed implementation file. Re-encoding the same uncorrected scene or asserting a code fix against an unchanged source/video cannot close a failure. If the previous finding was actually a misreport or an uncompleted observation, use `resolution: review_correction` with a concrete, truthful `correction_reason` and real re-observation evidence; do not label that as a code repair. This exception cannot justify erasing a confirmed visible problem. Mark actual visible defects `confirmed_defect: true`; frozen `resolution_requirement: implementation_change` prevents closing them through `review_correction`.

The gate rejects dropped findings or repairs still pending. If the next preview still falls short, retain its failure and repeat this operation using that review as the next predecessor. The frozen history preserves unresolved earlier findings and their original source identity across iterations. Also carry the failure into the applicable regression checks so later iterations cannot forget it. Recheck affected neighboring cuts and earlier accepted constraints. Preserve unaffected voice, captions, art and performance; a wrist correction does not justify rebuilding the whole film. A machine-valid encode, a populated plan or a self-awarded score never closes a visible failure.

After full rendering, make a fresh review pack from the final `report.json` and run `check-review` again before delivery, including continuous playback for overall rhythm. The final can contain shots outside an approved preview, so preview approval does not certify unreviewed later shots. Final review does not require another approval question when the user already authorized completion.

Before declaring the work finished, confirm that animation and space were actually observed at the primary standard, no known gap remains, prior failures have current repair evidence, and the delivered file is the reviewed file. If any condition fails, continue the local repair loop. An interim or blocked report must state the observed shortfall and next repair clearly, and must not claim matched quality or completed production.
