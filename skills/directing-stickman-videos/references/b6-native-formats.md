# B6 native landscape and portrait direction

**B6 works best in portrait (9:16).** The user-selected strict primary acceptance reference is Sample 1, the portrait V44 Eiffel film, for drawing, relative limb weight, brushwork, soft light, performance, spatial depth, camera language and rhythm. Sample 2 Hong Kong V90 remains a secondary binding/engineering reference; its finer arms do not supersede Sample 1. This priority applies to both requested formats: adapt the composition and camera natively while retaining the shared style. Portrait preference does not authorize replacing a requested landscape delivery or treating one ratio as approval of another.

**Choose the ratio before storyboard, asset coverage and camera work.** Save requested formats independently. Reuse narrative beat IDs, approved stills, voice and story; re-author shot timing, framing, world staging, entrances, occlusion, action emphasis and caption breaks/safe zones. Changing width/height, cropping a film, adding sidebars, stretching a canvas or sharing normalized coordinates is not native adaptation.

The foundation is a **wide, large, deep world** in both formats. A camera can travel only through space that has been authored. A narrow static plate cannot gain good lateral/depth motion from shake and zoom. Before rendering, sample extreme camera keys, turning points and near-plane foreground crossings; show the world layout and frusta, not only a center frame.

| Same narrative beat | 16:9 native plan | 9:16 native plan |
|---|---|---|
| Establish place/relationship | Left/center/right landmarks, readable two-person spacing, foreground/middle/background; a motivated lateral track or reveal | Tighter width, stacked/deep space, readable faces in the upper field, purposeful lower foreground; depth/vertical reveal |
| Turn and approach | Show travel direction and the destination before cutting; permit a full body when it helps | Re-stage entry from depth or edge, then cut closer before the actor becomes too small; preserve screen direction |
| Handoff | Horizontal giver → prop → receiver triangle, common floor/counter and contact plane; lateral object follow or a motivated detail cut | Keep wrists/prop in a dedicated middle zone and expressions above; closer spacing/depth staging as needed, separately timed attention shift |
| Buyer reaction | Reaction close-up with contextual shoulder/counter or shot/reverse-shot; landscape is not always a wide shot | One larger readable expression and received prop, leaving UI/caption bands clear; hold long enough to register the change |
| Street/transport | Travel through a long continuous street, walkers and carriage move independently behind/between foreground objects | Use near/far crossings and depth entrances; select a shorter readable region of the same wide world, not a crop of the landscape movie |
| Payoff/information | Establish where to look, reveal and settle; use empty space purposefully | Prioritize one focal fact, then reaction; reflow semantic captions if needed without changing voice |

## Environment and ensemble shots

Choose each shot's information carrier before placing the cast: a street or room, a landmark or prop, a crowd, a relationship, an action, or a reaction. **The protagonist need not appear in every shot or in the first frame.** An environment or ensemble shot can establish place, era, everyday activity or a destination before the story introduces its lead. Use it when it adds readable information; do not make a scenic opening compulsory or slow an already effective opening to fit one.

- Author recognizable connected regions along the camera route. A street may reveal shops, crossings, traffic and a destination; a room may reveal occupied tables, a working kitchen and a counter. Give the regions different functions and visual shapes, rather than filling extra width with more copies of the same facade.
- Plan a clear primary focus for the environmental shot. Background people have concrete activities and independent start/stop times, paths and occlusion. Crowd density, motion and contrast should support the place without competing equally for attention. Sparse hold, crossing, pause and return can be more convincing than every person looping continuously.
- Compose the environment without reserving a giant central hero slot by default. Save enough usable floor and destination space for the later staging; separate any people, vehicles, steam or foreground crossings that must move. A clean plate with no people is an asset choice, not a requirement for an empty final scene.
- Let a motivated track or foreground reveal pass attention from the world to the relevant shop, entrance, object or person, then settle for the next beat. Preserve direction and geography. Match the relevant focus across a cut; matching a protagonist's eye position applies when the viewer is actually following that face, not when the narrative deliberately starts elsewhere.
- In 16:9, use connected lateral regions and readable groups; in 9:16, use depth crossings, vertical landmarks and a shorter readable portion of the same large world. Plan and review each requested composition separately.

For each shot, record the primary subject, new information, protagonist presence/absence and its reason, environmental regions revealed, any background activities, and the attention handoff into the next shot. These decisions supplement the shot record below; they do not impose a fixed shot order or a crowd quota. Review the authorized segment's opening, middle and ending for repeated face crops, unchanging set coverage and decorative motion. Keep B6's imperfect ink, matte paint, meme acting and soft light when learning spatial direction from another style.

## Required shot record for each ratio

Save beat ID, unique shot ID, actual audio in/out, composition, actor/prop world positions, view/expression states, foreground/occlusion order, camera intent/keys/easing, focus/eyeline, action/contact timestamps, transition and caption band. Add the relevant moving-reference range/capability, its concrete implementation and the rendered evidence to inspect under [motion execution and review](b6-motion-review.md). Record a reason for cuts below .9 s and a reading hold for new text/evidence. Keep subject scale and contact planes physically consistent across cuts.

Use a planned camera vocabulary, not a quota: lateral establish, foreground wipe/reveal, depth dolly, motivated reframe, object follow, reverse/reaction shot and a stable payoff hold. Acceleration, braking and stillness give fast movement weight. Reserve short overshoot or shake for a motivated event. Depth is created by projection, parallax, occlusion and moving actors, not by wobbling a person or stretching the final frame.

Walkers, horses and carriages have world paths and start times independent of camera position. Planted feet/hooves stay on the ground during stance; wheels use traveled distance divided by radius. Different camera speed never changes the walk phase or wheel rotation. Do not loop a walker/carriage through the same visible edge with a teleport inside a shot.

Keep one primary attention cue at a time. An opening track, a foreground walk and prominent traffic should not all demand equal attention. Settle the camera when the main gesture needs reading, while retaining restrained, staggered background activities such as tea drinking, page turns, cooking and steam. Cars still travel normally on an authored road path with attached wheels; freezing traffic is not the default solution to a busy opening.

Review the ending as carefully as the opening: consecutive beats must add a spatial or emotional fact. Use motivated changes among a relationship two-shot, prop/contact detail, reaction and wider consequence rather than repeating the same face crop with another zoom. Keep screen direction, gaze and action phase continuous; camera richness comes from the set and staging, not character wobble or nonuniform image stretching.

Caption geometry is a native composition constraint, not a last-minute bottom overlay. Apply the [portrait renderer contract and landscape boundary](b6-sample-1-acceptance.md#portrait-caption-renderer-fixed-reference-facts): B6’s approved Archivo Black default retains Sample 1’s portrait baseline 914 at 720×1280; the visible glyph center and the baseline differ. Reserve face/hand/prop space around that band. The landscape starting profile is not an approved V44 coordinate standard. Review the actual native output and record any explicit user/language deviation.

## Saved implementation and review

`assets/b6/starter/landscape.json` and `portrait.json` demonstrate separate compositions, timing, actor spacing, camera keys and caption zones for the same six-second **technical blocking example**. They are starting structures, not the approved story or a universal camera preset. The first uses a horizontal track and a 4.2 s reaction cut; the second uses a vertical/depth attention shift and a 4.5 s reaction cut. Author new plans around the actual voice and story.

Review each requested ratio in real playback with its own contact sheet, extreme-camera frames, hand/foot close-ups, captions and audio. Include matched-head-scale figure/arm comparisons against Sample 1 portrait so a format change does not silently thin the limbs or replace the drawing, acting and light with Sample 2's treatment. Compare the actual implementation with its planned effect: “depth approach” requires a changing depth relationship, “object follow” must track the object's action, and different shot IDs alone do not establish different camera language. Record a purposeful hold as a hold. Use the [motion review gate](b6-motion-review.md) to record gaps, corrections and review limitations. Record separate preview hashes and actual user decisions. A technical encode, a landscape like, or approval of a portrait sample cannot approve the other ratio. One requested ratio requires only that ratio's production; asking for both means two independently directed versions, not two generated voices.
