## Visual language

- Near-white warm paper `#FDFDFC` with an extremely faint broad square grid, not pure white, beige parchment or prominent texture. At 1280×720 use 64px spacing, `rgba(76,90,105,.036)` and 0.8px lines above y=610; keep the bottom 110px caption band the same solid paper color. Scale this treatment for other resolutions/ratios rather than stretching it. Do not add visible grain or stronger texture by default.
- Expressive hand-drawn stick figures with fine, slightly irregular black pen outlines. Follow the B1 character-and-acting standard below. The original three-pose sheet belongs to the included demo, not a fixed pose library for new productions.
- Hand-drawn colored props, marker accents and curved arrows; limited warm yellow/red/green plus ink/navy. Use recognizable, detailed-enough objects, diagrams and relevant marks rather than crude placeholder symbols. A coherent licensed icon family can support the drawings. Real screenshots may appear briefly as evidence in restrained rounded frames with fine outlines, light shadows and small accents; keep those frames subordinate to the hand-drawn world. Follow the checklist for sharpness, language and factual checks.
- Large, short in-scene text is the visual focal point. The original accepted demo used Chalkboard on macOS; the portable package includes OFL Caveat as a fallback, not a claim of pixel-identical typography. For other scripts/languages configure a readable licensed font with the required glyphs.
- At 1280×720, use the runtime's Noto Sans 600, 34px short phrase captions in the bottom safe band (baseline y=660), with readable contrast. The accepted historical sample's six-pixel offset is not a new typography preset. Keep the sample's hand-lettered hierarchy: large short scene text with selective bold/color/underlining as the focal point, quieter captions below. Scale for other ratios/resolutions; verify on a phone. Avoid crowding text, faces or evidence into the caption band.

## Stick-figure features and acting — B1 only

Before generating new B1 character assets, inspect the packaged [proportion and acting guide](../assets/b1/character-acting-reference.png). It records the accepted slender figure and whole-body acting direction, not mandatory scenes, props or a complete expression set. Keep the near-white faint-grid background; do not import reference-video parchment.

- **Slender proportions:** a modest round head, long single-line torso and limbs, and small oval hands/feet. An upright figure is visually about 4–4.5 heads tall; this is a proportion guide, not a rigid measurement for seated, bent or close-up poses. Avoid oversized chibi heads, short limbs, mitten hands and filled/triangular shirt-like torsos. Arm lines crossing the body are not a filled torso.
- **More exaggerated expressions:** push emotion beyond the calibration stills so it reads immediately at small viewing size. Combine black oval eyes, visibly raised/pinched/asymmetric brows and a clearly changing mouth shape: wide surprise, open-mouthed frustration, squinting strain, a broad grin or a deflated frown as the meaning calls for it. Small tears/sweat marks can support a strong beat. Do not reuse one worried face, tiny smile or constant screaming expression throughout.
- **Act with the whole body:** emotion changes gaze, head tilt, body lean, arm reach and stance together. Vary profile/three-quarter views, sitting/standing, shoulder/hip direction and weight-bearing legs. Make hands genuinely touch the keyboard, mouse, note or supported object; plant feet and show believable balance. Prefer purposeful asymmetry and clear silhouettes over front-facing pictograms or the same pose with a swapped mouth.
- **New content, new acting:** keep a video's cast design consistent, but generate the expressions, poses and prop interactions needed by that story. Build a small story-specific reference batch before composing; do not recycle the demo's three poses or the guide's two scenes across every video. Reuse valid assets for holds and continuity rather than redrawing accepted material unnecessarily.
- **Detailed enough, not cluttered:** let recognizable hand-drawn objects and relevant website/platform/company marks, real screenshots or person sketches explain the spoken idea. Use them only when supported by the content; avoid generic placeholder symbols and unrelated logo piles. Keep one clear focal action and leave room for the short scene text and captions.

## Rhythm and sound

Voice reference only: Ryan V4 uses Google Gemini **Bodi**; the older V15 uses Google Gemini **Sadaltager**. A new B1 project may use the user's chosen or available configured TTS service/voice. Help configure that route rather than requiring Gemini. Keep the selected voice's native rate and default treatment; preserve existing accepted recordings without automatic regeneration.

Voice leads at native **1×** speed. Retain natural pace and pauses; target duration is not permission to stretch speech. Character shots occupy roughly **60–70% of direct screen time** for the approved B1 promotional format, not 60–70% of image area and not a hard requirement for every possible story. Include character-free closeups and reading holds with purposeful picture progression. Fit pictures and information reveals to the actual spoken meaning, not speech to prebuilt slides.

Use semantic match cuts, a prop carried between ideas, closeups and a settled frame after an action. Vary text/character/prop entry order by meaning; sometimes show the complete composition immediately. Do not apply a repeating title/icon/underline build to every shot, speed up all cuts or keep the camera moving constantly. Animate relevant hands, head, held objects or props locally with clear contact and weight; whole-image zooms, pans and fades alone do not satisfy the baseline's micro-animation. Use narration, synchronized phrase captions and restrained action-specific SFX in every finished B1 preview/film; effects must remain below the voice. Keep the chosen voice fixed through the video.

Apply the mandatory [B common production and acceptance checklist](autopilot-production-qa.md): its first-30s picture/SFX/copy hook, natural captions, sharp evidence, content-matched icons, no filler repetition, principal-picture progress within 2.5 seconds and whole-film detail gates are shared by every B style, not B1-only. For B1, meet them through the approved hand-drawn acting/props and readable visual hierarchy, preserving settling time rather than forcing faster cuts. The B1 checklist supplements these gates with this style's independent design and audio defaults.

## Acceptance

Compare each new B1 sample against Ryan V4 for slender proportions, clearly exaggerated but varied expressions, whole-body weight and prop contact, meaningful local motion, readable text, clear visual hierarchy, uncluttered compositions, original speech speed, subtitle timing and balanced SFX. Check stills against the contact/character sheets, then watch and listen to the actual rendered sample against the baseline clip: static proofs cannot verify animation, rhythm or sound. Keep the style, not the Ryan script, exact shot sequence, creator props or repeated poses. New subjects require corresponding acting, evidence and scene design.

Apply the production checklist to **every shot of the full film**, not just the opening sample. Keep the accepted opening's linework, asset detail, typography, screenshot framing, motion and sound quality through the body and ending. The added Full V4 evidence is a quality reference, not a requirement to copy its marketing script, timestamps, screenshots, channel metrics or exact scene sequence. Do not alter A, another B style, the public showcase or existing accepted recordings when maintaining this standard.

The included three-pose atlas/declarative runtime example is an engineering fixture, not a fixed cast or a claim to reproduce Ryan V4 automatically. Author new layered artwork/poses for the story and inspect the rendered result. The Chinese README uses the same approved Ryan V4 baseline in its top preview and B1 detail; archived V15 media remains unchanged.
