# B4 · Survival Thriller / 求生短剧风

The **drawing style is user-approved and locked**. B4 uses this existing Skill's shared installed runtime: initialize with `node scripts/autopilot.mjs init /absolute/project --style B4`, then author its story-specific image layers, [required picture-animation plan](b4-picture-animation.md), timed captions and native-rate audio tracks under the [runtime contract](autopilot-runtime.md#b4-production-tracks). The same validate/preview/approve/render commands apply; do not alias B4 to B1/B2/B3. Read [Autopilot](autopilot.md) and [shared production QA](autopilot-production-qa.md) for production; neither supplies B4's visual design. Picture progression and local text animation are default production work, not options waiting for the user to request a GIF or remind the agent.

Read the [reference/output boundary](b4-asset-origin.md) before selecting story assets: the approved pack guides new artwork; its finished scenes cannot stand in for a newly requested story.

## Fixed visual language, variable story

The reference set and style prompt fix clear slightly organic black contours, broad painted color fields, restrained cel shadows and detailed but readable full-color 2D environments. Characters, architecture and props must share that treatment. Preserve the simple white circular head, minimal black face, unclothed black stick body/limbs and compact rounded white cartoon gloves as the default character grammar. The revised reference images now actually show white gloves; do not use the superseded black-hand versions. Example clothes/hair remain story identities. Do not alter the installed canonical references during new-story production.

Do **not** translate “refined,” “night,” or “cinematic framing” into glossy concept art, realistic skin/materials, volumetric light, heavy bloom, reflective 3D desks or crushed-black backgrounds. Subtle texture/shading visible in the approved anchor is allowed; this is not an empty vector-icon style. Use foreground/midground/background and recognizable story props without clutter. Night details remain legible; daylight can use a different palette without changing the drawing style.

Keep **style consistency separate from story identity**. Vary locations, viewpoints, lighting, roles, expressions and meaningful object states according to the script. Do not copy the office, rainy window, telephone, blue-night palette or headset into unrelated stories. The earlier Meridian Tower example's yellow beanie/scarf are also story-specific, not a universal costume. A hairstyle or occupational hat can identify another cast when useful; avoid default clothing or a detailed human body. Explicit story requirements can add a costume, such as this story's yellow-raincoat intruder, while preserving the simple drawing grammar.

The current dispatcher specifically uses a bald round white head, **large readable single-ear headset and connected short boom mic**, no hair/hat/clothes; keep eyes and mouth unobstructed. Do not apply that cast choice to every B4 protagonist.

Gloves have rounded palms, simplified fingers and a readable thumb, not wire fingers, realistic skin hands or inflated giant mitts. At a phone/switch/book, check continuous wrists, actual contact/grip, thumb/finger occlusion and attached cables. Front, side and three-quarter views preserve head/body/glove proportions. A nearby floating glove is not a completed interaction; use a simpler already-held pose if needed.

**Limb proportion and reach are a separate visual gate.** In each scene request, preserve the reference character's compact gloves and coherent shoulder–elbow–wrist proportions, including bent or partly hidden arms. Trace the whole arm, not just a connected wrist: upper arm and forearm must belong to the same plausible pose, with consistent lengths between shots. Do not stretch an arm to reach a phone, desk or book, or place an elbow arbitrarily low outside the crop; reposition the prop, bend the arm naturally or simplify the composition instead. Check the full pose as well as the hand crop; a convincing grip does not excuse an elongated limb. Keep this as simple stick-character anatomy, not realistic human rendering.

**Check the pelvis-to-leg fork, not only the hands.** When both legs are visible, one coherent torso must meet a common pelvis and branch into two distinct legs. In seated poses, keep the two thighs, knees and lower legs readable through natural bends and occlusion; do not branch a leg from the chest, arm or seat edge, fuse both thighs into one side, or hide a broken connection with a glove. Include this topology in the scene-specific pose brief alongside the complete unchanged style prompt. Check the full illustration and the relevant encoded body crop. A clean face/hand crop or passing file fingerprint cannot certify the body.

For local expression or threat-state changes, preserve the accepted body's topology, scale, prop contact and screen position unless a deliberate coherent pose change is needed. Two expression states should not dissolve into doubled eyes, mouths or limbs. Use a clean state switch or a controlled local reveal when a blended transition damages the drawing; inspect an intermediate encoded frame as well as the endpoints. A clean state switch alone does not satisfy the required animated picture progression in the opening or body. This is a picture-review requirement, not a demand for limb animation or identical bodies across different shots.

Scripted abnormal anatomy is a **named-character exception**, never B4's default and never permission to exaggerate further. Preserve the script's degree and the user's corrections: “arms past his knees” does not authorize adding “low-shin wrists” or “fingertips near the ankles.” Use only the minimum needed to communicate the stated abnormality, keep it consistent across views, and do not transfer it to the caller or dispatcher. A user-rejected proportion fails review even if the prompt and file-integrity checks passed; mark affected art for correction before reusing it.

## Story-led horror visibility gate

For **B4 horror stories**, frightening visual storytelling is a required picture-design and review criterion across the opening, body and ending, not an optional sound treatment. Apply it to threat-bearing beats; ordinary setup, contrast and breathing space remain valid. Other genres follow their own stakes, not a mandatory horror preset.

- **Name the visible evidence before generation.** In the shot ledger and scene request, specify the script-supported abnormality or threatening relationship, where viewers will see it and when it becomes readable. Use staging, silhouette, expression, spatial contradiction, obstruction or an object-state change as the story warrants. A held illustration can carry this; limb motion is not required. Do not invent a monster, injury or supernatural event to make an ordinary scene scary.
- **Separate cast identity from menace.** Keep the protagonist and other roles distinguishable through story-appropriate silhouette, head detail/accessory, placement and established screen geography while preserving B4's round heads, compact gloves and simple bodies. Do not automatically dress everyone or reuse the protagonist's distinctive identity on a different role. An intentional double or mimic must be supported by the script, with the two positions/roles still understandable. If a scripted threatening figure reads only as a friendly person waving, or an unrelated role is mistaken for the protagonist, the shot fails; fix identity/staging before adding effects.
- **Make the threat visible, not merely described.** Dark colors, fog, explanatory labels and ominous SFX cannot substitute for the relevant picture evidence. Keep the focal anomaly legible at normal playback size without crushing the environment or breaking the drawing/anatomy lock. Exaggerate composition and contrast, not unrequested gore or accidental limb distortion. Preserve the script's reveal order; when danger is intentionally unseen, show its supported trace or threatening spatial relationship and record what remains withheld rather than revealing the final creature early.
- **Review the picture independently, then in context.** Inspect threat-bearing encoded shots with sound muted and subtitles/labels disregarded: can the intended visible evidence and who/where relationship be perceived in the allotted time? Then review the real picture/sound sequence for escalation and reveal timing. This is not a demand that every plot fact be understandable without narration. Record the evidence, timestamp, identity check, held-back information and pass/fail result; correct only failed assets/cuts. Static instruction tests do not certify that a shot is frightening.

## Generation and anti-drift gates

1. **Resolve and record:** save `style: B4`, the manifest version and all five image/prompt SHA-256 values in the project checkpoint. Inspect the actual reference set, not merely filenames. Copy the set and prompt into the external project if needed, preserving fingerprints. Store the exact scene prompts, provider/model and the image references actually submitted. A different model/host does not waive the lock.
2. **Separate style and content:** attach at least one scene-relevant white-glove image from the bundled set as a drawing-style reference in every new scene request, plus any approved story-specific cast/prop reference as an identity reference. For an interior use motor-room/driver; for an exterior use rooftop/street; the opposite-rooftop image also covers distant-character readability. Use multiple when supported and helpful. Include the complete style prompt, then the shot's location, composition, cast, contact/action and lighting. Do not copy reference locations or assume “same style as before” reaches a stateless generator. For edits, retain a bundled white-glove style reference alongside the actual edit target when supported. Never replace this set with a derivative office still or the superseded black-hand images.
3. **Calibrate before batching:** inspect one representative new-story still against the anchor at full view and at a face/glove/prop crop. Compare contour character, flat-versus-volumetric rendering, head/body/glove scale, complete shoulder–elbow–wrist reach and segment proportions, hand contact, readable environment detail and palette treatment. Then locally render the hardest planned action/reveal with its intermediate state and readable result before producing remaining art. Test contact through the action, not only at endpoints. Fix failed categories before scaling up. Reuse an already approved equivalent first frame/action and retained audio; do not regenerate or ask for duplicate approval merely because the lock was packaged.
4. **Check each bounded batch:** carry the [per-shot state/action plan](b4-picture-animation.md#author-before-generating) into layered art or coherent state assets, then render and inspect the actual chronological clips plus suspect full-size frames against the same anchor. Reject realistic/3D drift, human anatomy or unrequested clothing, different head/hand grammar, unreadable dark environments, simplistic stock-icon scenery, invisible local motion and empty repeated loops. For horror, also apply the [horror visibility gate](#story-led-horror-visibility-gate); visually ordinary threat shots and accidental cast-identity duplication fail even if style integrity passes. Check the batch boundary and distant reused assets. Revise only affected assets/cuts; preserve accepted audio and passing shots. Record per-shot reference use and observed findings, not just “B4” or successful generation.
5. **Review the actual sample and full film:** the normal representative 20–30-second audiovisual preview must demonstrate this art direction across differing shots, body-picture progression, animated text and sound. Complete independent [picture review](b4-picture-animation.md#encoded-evidence-and-independent-picture-review) and [text review](b4-typography-acceptance.md#encoded-evidence-and-independent-acceptance) on its actual MP4. A still-style approval is not animation, audio or whole-film approval. After the normal scoped remaining-production authorization, finish bounded batches and review every final shot plus complete continuous playback; attractive opening clips do not approve unseen later sections. No automatic aesthetic test or pixel-identical guarantee is implied by the integrity checker.

An unchanged principal-picture state also cannot last over three seconds across cuts: crops, subtitle updates, micro-zooms and decoration do not reset the timer. Register a short held pose's purpose and adjacent supporting animated beat in `picture_motion`; routine holds need no extra approval, but `exceptional_hold` or author-written approval cannot create a long-shot exemption. A full static slideshow or repeated empty GIF loop still fails. Preserve the first-three-second hook as well as this whole-film limit, without sacrificing drawing quality to forced limb rigs. This exception does not change A or B1–B3.

Apply the [typography contract](b4-typography-acceptance.md) to authorized story/language adaptations without forcing an English font onto another script. Break at actual speech/meaning, not fixed word counts; verify every cue, safe area, readable local progression/hold and contact/action visibility.

Use the suspense/apocalypse module only for relevant stories, not as a universal B4 soundtrack. Do not reuse another film's mixed soundtrack as raw effects. Verify actual playback; neither a cue sheet nor a passing meter proves convincing sound. Keep the requested complete-film handoff and verified folder shortcut; repair excerpts are supplementary.
