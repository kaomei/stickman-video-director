# B5 action sound and acceptance

The accepted example is the exact V13 composite: its earlier approved V12 audio is retained. Do not substitute the louder V10/V11 mixes. The example uses **The Pharaoh 3 – Energetic, Lively & Cheerful**; this is a sample choice, **not a locked B5 default**. Explicit user choices prevail, otherwise use the common B settings order. Do not inherit B4 Brian or force a celebrity's real voice. Reuse accepted narration; obtain authorization before any new billable call.

## Design and mix

Use [the common story-led sound system](autopilot-sound-system.md), adapted to energetic physical comedy rather than suspense by default. Save scene/beat sound cues with action, material, distance, texture, purpose and exact contact time. The bundled [sample cue ledger](../assets/b5/sound-spotting.json) is an authored timing reference, not a downloadable raw sound library.

- Voice is clear and stable; action SFX are perceptible; BGM is secondary. Do not repeat the failed approach of lowering music while leaving unsuitable, soft or late effects unchanged.
- On each important action, design preparation/air movement → sharp contact transient → short material body/tail → rebound or reaction where motivated. Paper, wood, cloth, glass, door and spring motion must sound distinct. Trim silence before the contact; align the strongest transient to the visual anchor within one frame (30 fps baseline).
- Prioritize a few clear accents. Large visual actions need weight, but not a boom on every word, constant whooshes, piercing bells or a loud loop across the whole film. Small movements need not all have an effect.
- Keep music under speech and duck/reduce it around meaningful action accents; automate by scene. Preserve intentional quiet. Do not bury effects under a continuous noise bed or duplicate sounds already present in a mixed master.
- If impact is weak, inspect source/material, transient shape, crop, distance, timing and masking before simply raising gain. Check attack and short body, not just waveform peak.

The accepted sample measured about −14.95 LUFS integrated / −2.49 dBTP; these are reference observations, not a mandatory loudness prescription or proof of headphone/speaker listening. Check the encoded mix, voice intelligibility, clipping/true peak, mono and small-speaker translation. State unavailable physical listening checks honestly. Keep VO/SFX/BGM stems in the user's project where licensing permits; don't redistribute raw commercial/library sources inside the Skill.

## Acceptance gates and repeatability

1. Identity: recognizable but abstract, appropriate likeness/reference handling, no generic face substitution. Preserve approved head/body, presenter size and resting/keyword gesture behavior.
2. Motion: contact/force/rebound chain, grounded feet, reachable grips, consistent limbs, physical mirror and paper interactions. No unmotivated circles/arcs. Compare a contact-sheet first, then problem frames and continuous playback.
3. Type: title not clipped during camera motion; semantic captions aligned to real voice, all words exactly once, intended line count, safe placement, readable at playback size. B5 alone permits the approved up-to-three-line presenter caption block.
4. Sound: inspect and listen to synchronized action accents; speech > action SFX > music priority, source-specific rights records, no loop fatigue or unexplained layer pile-up.
5. Delivery: full duration requested (not just a 5s/24s test), metadata, full decode, preserved accepted-audio hash for picture-only edits, actual user approval and a verified absolute folder link.

Regression boundaries: a new avatar's static approval is not video approval; new scripts need new action/timing/caption/audio authoring. The supplied all-frame arm and word-coverage checks demonstrate this sample's technical repeatability, not universal anatomy, facial likeness, lip-sync or creative quality. Keep a checkpoint of approvals, fingerprints, jobs and gaps; resume valid artifacts without blindly rerunning paid generation.
