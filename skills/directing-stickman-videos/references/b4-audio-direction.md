# B4 · Locked narrator and automatic story-led audio

Required alongside the [B4 visual contract](b4-survival-storytelling.md) and [B sound system](autopilot-sound-system.md), before casting, voice generation, sound spotting or audio QA. This is agent-executed production guidance, not a new renderer or a guarantee that any TTS take will act correctly. It applies only to B4; other B styles retain their voice defaults and A is unchanged. Explicit user choices take precedence. Preserve accepted recordings when starting from an existing project.

## Fixed main narrator

Before any new voice call, save the exact narration script authorized for production and its fingerprint. A user-supplied script or an explicitly approved draft is required; a topic, future test plan, or credit-use approval alone is not script approval. Draft a script only when the request includes that work. If the user says they will supply it, wait; do not invent copy or start generation. Keep script approval and paid-call approval separate, and recheck both after a material script change. The project evidence check records these inputs but does not itself grant permission to call a provider.

For new B4 production, resolve **Brian — Deep, Resonant and Comforting** in ai33's ElevenLabs catalog by **voice ID `nPczCjzI2devNBz1zQrb`**, not by a short-name match. Record the resolved provider, model and ID before generation. The accepted baseline used `eleven_multilingual_v2`, High quality, speed **1.00**, no pronunciation dictionary, no audio tags and empty style/direction fields. Retain those observed settings when available. Do not invent unexposed stability/similarity/style values or automatically add whisper/low-register directions. Brian remains the narrator across scenes, locations and emotional beats; a scripted exact-voice mimic uses that same voice, not a new actor.

After B4 selection, explain ai33 registration/login and possible credit use once when relevant, not as an installation requirement. Reuse a working authorized session. If Brian is unavailable, unauthenticated or out of quota, preserve prepared work and report that specific gap. Offer an explicitly chosen alternative such as Gemini Bodi or a supplied recording; **never silently substitute** another voice/provider or a Brian homonym. Do not recommend/retry rejected Marcus, George or Michael unless the user revisits them. This saved policy alone authorizes no paid call, purchase, subscription or publication.

## Automatic character casting and performance

Once story and production scope are authorized, the assistant handles routine casting, supported performance instructions, acquisition/export, synchronization, SFX and mixing without asking the user to choose every role or manually download each file. Existing account, cost, rights and sample/full-film approval gates still apply. If an operation is blocked, continue unaffected preparation and request only the missing authorization/input. Before retrying a failed download, inspect the provider's completed job, existing player/export and local files; do not blindly regenerate audio or bypass access controls or download restrictions.

1. **Cast only real roles.** Use secondary voices for plot-needed dialogue, not forced splits of continuous narration. Keep Brian fixed. Reuse approved role recordings/identities; do not recast them merely because a scene changes. A female role is not automatically a frightened girl, and a frightened girl is not automatically Tavi.
2. **Research new casting.** Before selecting a new/additional voice, actually use [Google Search as required by B](autopilot.md#voice-led-production) for this genre, role, language/accent, provider and relevant popular voices. Save queries, Google URLs, date, useful sources, exact voice identity/availability and role-fit evidence. Search rank alone is not a popularity claim. If Google is inaccessible, record incomplete casting research; do not fabricate it or switch engines silently. Already approved Brian/roles do not need repeated casting research.
3. **Act the scene, not a preset.** B4 expressly authorizes story-motivated performance direction for secondary dialogue roles in supported provider controls. Keep exact spoken text separate from non-spoken direction. Specify what the character wants, their emotional state and the audible performance needed: urgency, hesitation, trembling, relief, anger or restraint as the script warrants. Preserve role identity and intelligible words. Use native defaults when no extra acting is needed. This bounded role exception does not authorize automatic Brian tuning, changed script, arbitrary voice parameters or new billable auditions outside approved scope.
4. **Approve the dry source before processing.** Compare exact text and actual audio for all words, pronunciation, identity, natural pace and emotional fit. ASR helps alignment; it does not overrule the script and audible evidence. A frightened caller should sound voiced and alarmed when required, not intimate or sleepy; use whisper only when the story calls for it. Do not create fear by automatically lowering pitch, volume or bandwidth. If acting is wrong, reuse another valid take or repair/regenerate only the failed lines within authorization; louder playback or EQ cannot repair the performance.
5. **Then place the voice in the scene.** Add phone/radio, distance, room or occlusion treatment only after the source passes. Preserve consonants, body and emotion; compare dry and processed takes at comparable perceived level. Do not use one fixed telephone bandpass for every source. Establish clip gain and balance after acting/perspective, rather than burying a voice under a filter and calling it fear.
6. **Time to actual speech.** Keep source rate/pitch unless specifically authorized otherwise. Place role phrases at natural semantic/breath boundaries; never omit words, manufacture long internal pauses or speed-stretch acting to hit a nominal slot. Preserve approved caption/picture timing when the repair fits; otherwise realign affected captions/shots under the project's revision scope and recheck adjacent transitions. Apply the shared semantic-caption rules, not fixed word-count cutting.

## Automatic SFX direction and source gate

Spot opening, body and ending from this story's actual script, picture and voice times. Use the shared three functional layers as design roles, **not three always-on tracks**. Start with the minimum location bed needed; add only motivated Foley/events and emotional punctuation. Remove redundant rain, hiss, hum, drones, resonances and tails when they create clutter. Intentional AUDIO CUT may stop selected buses or the whole bed, with a recorded return trigger; silence is not automatically an error.

### First-frame audio gate

**Start with perceptible story-led sound from the first picture frame by default.** Do not automatically reserve two seconds of silence, delay the mix until a title finishes, or add a fixed pre-narration pad. A short click followed by near-silence, an inaudible ambience file, or sub-bass that disappears on small speakers does not fulfill this requirement. Choose an intelligible voice opening, an appropriate scene event or a purposeful nonverbal hook from this story; no mandatory loud hit, ringtone or constant noisy bed.

Fit picture to the approved voice, not an arbitrary silent intro. **Default to intelligible Brian narration as early as the native recording allows**, with no fixed SFX-only title pad. Trim demonstrable leading silence conservatively without clipping the first consonant, and verify the encoded speech onset separately from the first SFX onset; an audio-presence diagnostic cannot identify spoken words. A purposeful nonverbal opening is still possible when specifically chosen for the story/brief: record its reason, first cue, voice onset and the complete interval between them. Do not delay the voice merely to display text.

When an opening question is to be spoken, obtain approval for its exact words and any billable addendum, then join only that addendum to the retained body at natural boundaries. A visible question is not automatically approved TTS copy. Preserve all words, native rate/pitch and the accepted body recording; if the opening duration changes, move affected picture, semantic captions and causal SFX together and verify joins and sync. Keep the title as the hook's readable text rather than burning a duplicate subtitle over identical large words. These rules do not authorize unrelated revoicing, regeneration or public release.

Check the **actual exported file**, not only a timeline cue starting at zero. Inspect the first three seconds for initial silence, a long fade-in and a perceptually empty gap after a token hit, including a mono/mid-band diagnostic. Run `node scripts/b4-opening-audio-check.mjs /absolute/final.mp4` and retain its JSON report. The default 100 ms onset, -36 dBFS mid-band onset and -44 dBFS/600 ms lull thresholds are conservative diagnostic heuristics, not universal audibility laws or listening approval. Resolve flagged gaps against picture and actual listening; never raise the whole bed or add random effects merely to pass a meter.

Intentional opening silence remains possible **only with a recorded story reason, duration and audible return trigger**, consistent with the brief; no habitual silent lead-in. Record such an exception beside the diagnostic, not as a false measurement pass. If the user specifically rejects an empty opening, correct it locally rather than relabeling it intentional. Keep unavailable physical listening/device checks pending.

**Subtle continuous bed, necessary SFX.** Sustain one quiet, scene-matched background foundation through ordinary narration, using clean crossfades when the place/state changes. It should be felt without sounding busy or drawing attention to its loop. A continuously audible alarm, bell, heartbeat, radio static or foreground creature sound is not a neutral bed. Give such event cues motivated starts/stops and short tails instead of letting them run through unrelated lines. Do not stack several competing beds to satisfy continuity; a single restrained room/space texture is enough. Preserve planned AUDIO CUT and story-motivated silence rather than filling them automatically.

Keep the bed clearly below speech and log the chosen separation/automation; for this speech-first B4 workflow, roughly 20 dB or more beneath active narration is a useful starting check, not a universal loudness law or substitute for listening. The opening hook and critical contacts may briefly foreground an event at controlled peaks. Do not put a hit/whoosh on every cut; each cue must have a cause, listener perspective and story purpose. Review the bed alone, then with voice/SFX, in the encoded full mix for clutter, masking and fatigue. A “continuous” bed is not permission for continuous foreground noise.

- **Story events must be audible, not just listed.** Mark critical scripted sounds and their causal order; verify them in the encoded full mix with speech active. Narration saying “glass shattered” does not replace a glass effect. Preserve any required distress, interruption or disconnect after the crash without an unexplained causal gap. Do not add violent sounds to a story that does not call for them.
- **Material/source before sweetening.** Choose an actual recording or convincing generated event with the right material, force, scale and perspective; audition the source itself. For realistic physical events, start at native rate. Do not default to a slowed/pitched duplicate for weight, replayed debris tails or extra noise layers. Add a layer only for a distinct missing function, then compare with it muted; retain the simpler version if identity or causality becomes less natural. Intentional slow motion/stylization must follow this scene, not a blanket B4 preset.
- **One coherent event.** Align the source's actual contact transient, retain its natural body/consequence and avoid doubling an already complete recording. A pane break should read as one break and falling fragments, not repeated explosions. Match remote effects to phone/barrier perspective without filtering away the detail that identifies them. Exact source files, band edges and cue times belong to each project, not this reusable contract.
- **Hook and payoff follow genre.** When a brief asks for a three-second hearing hook, design an immediate story-relevant unsettling cue/contrast, not a mandatory ringtone or a louder scream. Do not import the Dispatch phone, glass, fear, horror palette or scare timing into every B4 story. Quiet clues and removal of sound can be stronger than another impact; prevent painful startle peaks.

## Evidence and acceptance gates

### Story-led ending choices

Use an ending choice only when the brief or story calls for audience participation; it is not a mandatory B4 outro. Preserve the story's last line and allow its reveal to register before a short Brian question. Keep alternatives clear, in-world and distinct, with a readable hold. In a normal rendered video this is a comment invitation, not clickable branching; do not simulate a user selection or add fake hover/cursor interaction.

Give the ending an intentional sound arc rather than accidental silence: retain a motivated transition, foreground the question, then synchronize one sparse causal cue with each demonstrated option or consequence when useful. Both alternatives deserve comparable visual/sound care. Duck effects under words; avoid continuous noise, ticking UI loops or another loud impact just to fill the hold. A deliberate silent beat is allowed. Do not force Dispatch's glitch, telephone, switch, melting face, horror palette or exact question into another story. Record question text, actual voice times, cue perspective, final hold and source rights; reuse the accepted main voice and regenerate only the addendum if needed within authorization.

Keep a compact project-local cast/performance and cue/rights manifest linked to the existing sound script. Record role/line IDs, exact text, provider/model/voice ID, applied settings/non-spoken direction, casting evidence, source paths/fingerprints and provenance, actual in/out, performance/perspective intent, stem/cue IDs, rights status and review results. Planned, generated, acquired/cleared and actually reviewed are different states. Do not invent unsupported runtime manifest fields; use external production artifacts and a supported finishing route.

Execute **dry performance → scene perspective → dialogue-first balance → full-mix/encode review**, with SFX source/material checks before final layering. Keep untouched originals and time-aligned dialogue, ambience, Foley/event, design and optional music stems. Keep secondary voices out of FX so they remain locally repairable. Use localized automation to preserve important events and intelligible speech; do not apply deep blanket ducking that erases every contact. Preserve contrast instead of accumulating constant background noise or relying on a limiter.

Before delivery, check the actual final audio from beginning to end: Brian identity/continuity, secondary-role text and emotional fit, all critical SFX, cause/contact/tail timing, channel perspective, quiet-bed cleanliness, dialogue masking, joins/loop fatigue, mono compatibility and safe peaks. Measure the encoded output against a declared client spec or labeled project heuristic; a LUFS/peak pass is not proof of good acting or convincing glass. Compare disputed repairs in matched-level old/new full mixes and optional effects-only excerpts. Record real listening/device checks separately from automated/tool-assisted diagnostics; leave unavailable checks pending and never claim that meters or static tests heard the film. Fix failed regions and their adjacent transitions only, retaining accepted narration/art/captions elsewhere.

Deliver the complete requested film, not just repair excerpts, with its verified local folder shortcut and checkpoint. The ordinary sample/full-production authorization and final user review remain in force. Do not automatically regenerate accepted media, synchronize installed copies, change READMEs/gallery or publish projects without separate user authorization.
