# Automatic music sourcing, license verification and credits

Background music is included by default in new B1–B9 previews and films under the [completeness gate](b-delivery-completeness.md). Automatically select, verify and mix music across all B styles during planning and finished-video production. This shared B workflow does not depend on B9 publication; A retains its own sound and output contract. Run this workflow without a separate reminder. Preserve the selected style's audio direction and explicit user choices; music matching a sample's mood does not require copying its recording. This workflow grants no permission to publish, spend money, contact an artist or change approved audio.

## Select and verify before use

For a new B film, first search the [bundled audio library](b-audio-library.md). Its 31 music tracks carry original-byte hashes, source snapshots, CC BY 4.0 legal text and required attribution. Export a suitable selection with its evidence/credits into the project, then add the actual edit/synchronization notice. Reuse this verified rights record for the unchanged permitted asset rather than requiring a new online license check or download for each film. These records cover the bundled files only, not similarly named uploads or a reference film's soundtrack. Preserve existing approved audio; use the external-source checks below when the library does not fit the story or the intended use changes.

1. Use known story beats, mood, pacing, voice space and the selected style to find suitable tracks through available tools. Prefer sources with clear, track-specific licenses covering the intended use and authorized downloads. Use existing project/platform/commercial requirements; if unspecified, prefer music explicitly permitting commercial video use and the likely delivery platforms. Do not assume an unknown platform or revenue model was confirmed; ask only when it changes the choice and a clearly suitable alternative cannot resolve it. Motivated short silence remains available with a documented story reason and return/ending cue; omitting the whole component requires an explicit user instruction. Sourcing inconvenience, missing tools or a failed download cannot waive required music.
2. Read the actual track's rights-holder or authorized library page and applicable license/terms, not just a search snippet, uploader claim or “free,” “royalty-free” or “no copyright” label. Verify the recording and composition rights covered, permission to synchronize with video, edit/trim/loop/mix, publish on the target platforms, and commercial/monetized/client use where applicable. Check attribution and other restrictions. A license limited to one platform cannot be assumed to cover another. If an approved license already covers the use, no bespoke author message is required. Never treat download access, a missing metadata license field or credit alone as a license grant.
3. Treat finished-video use, distributing a downloadable finished sample in a repository/install package, and redistributing a raw recording/stem as distinct uses. Check the actual intended distribution; do not demand unrestricted sublicensing when only a narrower use is needed. A repository code license does not relicense third-party music. Do not include raw tracks in a Skill/package unless that distribution is authorized and needed. User-provided or AI-generated music also needs an applicable rights basis; a generated file is not automatically cleared for every use.
4. Download from the permitted source only after establishing a suitable rights basis; do not bypass access controls or payment. Keep evidence with the project: the official license text/export or a permitted screenshot, its URL, track page, retrieval date and selected file fingerprint. Reuse the verified record for unchanged authorized assets and ratio adaptations; recheck if the track, terms or intended use changes. Do not repeatedly download or regenerate approved audio.

## Resolve uncertainty automatically within scope

- If terms are absent, contradictory, limited to noncommercial use for a commercial project, incompatible with the delivery platform, or prohibit the planned edits/distribution, reject that candidate and look for a suitable licensed alternative. Prefer equivalent mood and quality rather than forcing the sample's exact track. A saved rejected-candidate reason prevents recycling the same unresolved source.
- If a clear free alternative satisfies the brief, use it without a new permission question. Existing user authorization for paid sources continues to apply within its actual limits. If payment/new subscription or an artist message is needed and not already authorized, or the user requires the exact restricted track, present the specific missing right/cost and available alternative; do not purchase, send or claim permission. If tools cannot access the authoritative terms or an authorized download, report that concrete limit and use an available valid alternative; never invent a completed search, download or verification.
- For previously approved media, preserve its exact audio and historical records. New sourcing rules do not retroactively establish its rights or authorize muting, replacement or rerendering. Record any existing unresolved distribution limitation separately and keep that limitation in force until applicable evidence or user-approved media changes resolve it.

## Project evidence and ready-to-paste credits

Before final mixing, create/update project-local `music-provenance.json` and `music-credits.md` (or equivalent existing project records). The provenance record identifies:

- Intended use: finished-video destinations, commercial/monetization/client requirements, edits and any repository/package distribution; mark unknowns honestly.
- Each actually used track: title, creator, version/recording ID, official track page, authorized download source, local relative asset path and SHA-256; the rights holder/library and exact license name/version or permission basis.
- Evidence: license/terms URL, checked-at date, saved evidence path, scope/restrictions, required attribution text, performed modifications and the reason the planned use is covered. Record both recording and composition coverage where separately licensed.
- Decision: `cleared_for_declared_use`, `rejected`, `unresolved` or `not_used`, with reason; no universal copyright-safe guarantee. Record whole-component omission only with an explicit user instruction; record motivated short silence with its story reason and return/ending cue. Any `unresolved` track actually used blocks final delivery/public distribution as rights-cleared; a clearly labeled local draft is not a cleared final.

Generate credits from the actual license's instructions, including title/creator/source/license links and edit notices where required. Preserve mandatory wording; if no attribution is required, record that fact rather than inventing an obligation. `music-credits.md` must supply ready-to-paste text for the intended video description/credits and, if applicable, repository/package notices. Include it and the provenance record in the handoff and identify where the user should place the text. Generating a credit file does not mean it was posted externally; publishing remains subject to the user's authorization. For Director-only prompts, do not claim a later provider's unspecified music is cleared: state the unresolved downstream sourcing requirement until the actual track is known.

## Acceptance

Check every track actually heard in the final mix against its file fingerprint and declared use. Confirm evidence is retrievable, mandatory attribution is complete, and the planned delivery satisfies the license conditions. Unknowns cannot pass by a technical encode check or a generic “music verified” label. If a required source fails, replace it within the authorized new-film scope and recheck only affected mix/evidence; preserve unaffected approved work. Listen separately for story fit, narration intelligibility, clean edits and style-appropriate levels: a valid license does not establish audio quality.

These rules govern new sourcing. Existing reference-film credits and any unresolved sample-distribution gate remain attached to their exact media; they are not a reusable music license for new films.
