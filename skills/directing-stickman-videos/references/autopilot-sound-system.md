# B · Story-led sound system

Required for every B preview/film before sound spotting, sourcing and final mix review, across stories, explainers, comedy, history, science fiction, warm everyday scenes and promotional briefs. Act as the project's sound director: translate its meaning, setting, action and emotional arc into an executable sound script. This is a B production rule, not a new Skill, voice preset or universal horror soundtrack. A does not inherit it. Resolve user choice → selected B-style treatment → this common floor. Preserve accepted narration, picture timing and media unless the user requests a relevant change.

## Common B sound contract

Sound must communicate place, physical contact, information or emotion—not decorate every cut. Strong narration cannot rescue thin, unrelated effects. Judge assets by audible material, weight, perspective and fit, not whether the tool is open-source or the library advertises “cinematic.” Cartoon/game-like accents may suit a light explainer; do not use them as default suspense impacts.

Before sourcing, make a compact palette and spotting pass against the approved script, actual voice times and picture. When reference audio is available, study its timing, density, distance, quiet-to-loud contrast and tails, not only its visuals. Record unavailable audio comparison honestly. Do not extract or reuse another creator's mixed soundtrack as effects.

Use the existing shot ledger plus a project-local sound/rights manifest. Each cue or bed records:

- in/out, spoken/visual trigger, sound role and narrative purpose;
- material/source, listener position, distance/occlusion, sync point, gain/envelope and priority;
- asset path/fingerprint, provenance/license and final/temporary/needs-clearance status;
- planned silence/dropout, transitions/tails and review findings where relevant.

Build three **functional layers**, with dialogue/VO kept separately in front:

1. **Place/continuity — 底色环境 (Ambience):** plan an appropriate bed for every scene, including a quiet room's subtle air/clock or outdoor weather. Let it evolve with locations and story state, with clean loops and transitions. A drone is one possible texture, not a requirement. Avoid accidental digital silence; deliberate AUDIO CUT is the explicit exception below.
2. **Physical/spatial events — 具象动态 (Foley/Action SFX):** consequential Foley, props, devices, offscreen activity or creatures. Infer plausible audible details from the actual setting/action, naming close/medium/distant perspective, material, weight and occlusion. Do not invent an unseen door opening, weapon, extra character or other plot event just to add sound. Synchronize implied details only to an established action or explicitly planned scene.
3. **Emotional punctuation — 情绪推进 (Impact/Riser/Cut):** design anticipation, surprise, relief, warmth, humor or revelation at meaningful beats. A restrained chime, pause, soft release or removal of sound may serve better than a riser/impact. Establish hierarchy rather than making every sound a climax.

These are available roles, **not three tracks that must sound simultaneously throughout**. A layer can be absent when unjustified. Music is optional, is not a replacement for Foley/ambience and must not duplicate every impact. Adapt the palette to the selected style and story: a cheerful B2 explanation does not acquire drones, zombies or jump scares merely because it uses B mode.

## Reference-led sound translation and repair

When the user asks to learn a reference's **complete SFX**, cover its full duration before claiming completion. For a long reference, keep a project-local coverage map of overlapping sections, original time ranges and observations; reconcile overlaps rather than counting the same cue twice. Separate tool-estimated timing and interpretation from measured contacts and actual listening. If some audio is inaccessible, name the unstudied range. Save a compact transfer table: reference mechanism → its narrative job → this story's equivalent → cue IDs → validation evidence. Do not copy the original soundtrack or assume that watching frames constitutes studying sound.

Study the **ordinary middle**, not only the opening and climax. Paper flex, a loaded container, a latch, a brush against a surface and a receiver leaving its cradle can establish material and weight more effectively than another trailer hit. Spot only consequential actions supported by picture or text. A still-frame camera push is not, by itself, a physical whoosh. For a different genre, transfer the cause/perspective/contrast relationship—not its zombies, screams, metallic palette or scare frequency.

Use these decisions when turning observations into a mix:

- **Source before sweetener:** identify what moved, contacted or changed state. Select a recording/performance with that audible material and force. A generic click is not interchangeable with paper, plastic, wood and glass. Noise/oscillator sketches may prototype a cue, but cannot be described as a real human breath, scream or material recording. Replace the sketch when its identity is the reported defect; add a designed layer only for a named missing role.
- **Contact → body → consequence:** mark the actual transient *inside* the source file and align it to the contact/reveal, not merely the clip's start. Retain a credible body and distinct decay/debris/room response where the event needs them. A breaking pane followed by distress must read as one causal sequence, not a crash, an unexplained gap, then unrelated screaming. Time the sequence to the approved performance; do not stretch or revoice it to make room.
- **Listener position:** distinguish room, offscreen/barrier and phone/radio sounds. Match a remote cry or breath to its communication channel; it should not suddenly sound like another dry narrator in the listener's room. Preserve enough defining material detail to remain identifiable after filtering. For a close prop, retain dry contact plus a proportionate short room response—not a giant reverb or sub hit.
- **Recurring source, evolving meaning:** retain a recognizable identity for a repeated phone, alarm, lock or approaching threat. Change distance, occlusion, onset detail, room response, spacing or interruption when the plot changes. Do not create progression only by increasing gain, or replay the identical short breath/noise puff as several performances. Repetition can be intentional for a mechanical source; mark that choice.
- **Environment has states:** change the bed when place, time, machinery, weather or subjective attention changes. If a switch removes electrical buzz, preserve plausible remaining air/weather rather than dropping the whole world indiscriminately. Do not add a conspicuous unrelated environmental event just to vary a loop.
- **Hierarchy over blanket ducking:** assign each event foreground/support/background priority. A source-defining contact or critical offscreen clue must survive speech; use localized level/EQ/perspective automation and brief transient protection where needed. Applying the same deep full-band duck to every Foley cue can erase identity. Conversely, a critical effect is not permission to mask words. Reserve the strongest designed body for the few earned payoffs, and clear competing tails around meaningful cuts.

On a “thin / cheap / unimpressive” revision, first inventory what is **actually audible** in the current encoded film. Do not report glass, distress or other cues as missing if they already exist. Solo the relevant stems, name the defect (wrong source, weak contact/body, masking, perspective, causal gap, identical repetition, or lost contrast), then change those cues and adjacent state transitions. More assets, a famous library, stronger bass, compliant meters and extra gain are not evidence that this defect was fixed.

Keep an aligned **old/new full-mix comparison at matched programme loudness**, plus short effects-only auditions of disputed events. Do not peak-normalize every individual cue: that erases the hierarchy being judged. Compare identity, causality, distance, speech intelligibility and event-to-quiet contrast; include the final encoded mix and mono/small-speaker diagnostics. A band-limited render is a diagnostic, not a physical phone-speaker listening test. Record which auditory/device checks actually occurred and leave unavailable checks pending. A completed render or automated analysis is not aesthetic approval. Deliver the complete requested film; excerpts are supplementary, never a silent substitute for it.

## Universal scene/beat sound script

For every B project, save a project-local sound script before acquiring or generating audio. Work at **sentence or meaningful beat level**, not fixed time blocks or a new effect on every word. Cover the opening, body and ending. Start from the exact approved text and actual picture/voice; if timing is not yet available, use sentence/beat IDs and mark timecodes pending. Later align to actual audio rather than inventing precision or changing the speech. Reuse a continuing bed by reference instead of restarting it each sentence.

Use this format in the user's working language (labels may be translated); it is a production artifact, not a mandatory long message or another approval gate:

```text
【本幕环境底色】：地点/时间/空间；底色来源、质感、距离与起伏；有意静默则注明原因。

[句子/桥段 ID；实际 in–out 或“待配音对齐”] | 【配音方案/参数建议】：角色、已选平台/模型/音色；沿用已确认设置，或注明可选试听方案/不适用。
(🔊 背景层)：底色保持、变化或跨幕过渡；标明远近、遮挡和淡入淡出。
(💥 细节层)：动作来源、材质、近/中/远景、同步触发及混音优先级；无必要则写“无新增”。
(🎭 情绪层)：本节点情绪任务；选择铺垫、轻提示、释放、反差、Riser、Impact、AUDIO CUT 或“不加”。
📜 【文本内容】：“本句/桥段的原文，不改写。”
```

Every added cue must answer **what caused it, where it is, and why this moment needs it**. Connect cue IDs to the sound/rights manifest; describe planned sounds separately from acquired/cleared assets. Emotional labels in this plan are not automatically TTS instructions. Do not repeat an audition for every sentence or send timing/FX directions as spoken text.

Choose a palette from the particular story, not from the B number alone. Examples are illustrative, not reusable mandatory soundtracks:

- **Comedy / everyday life:** appropriate room/street air, real phone/cup/footstep contact, brief timing contrasts or a light payoff. No automatic horror bass or cartoon boing on every cut.
- **Warm / pet / relationship:** gentle location detail, paws/fabric/breath at believable distance and a restrained emotional release. Do not make fear cues cute by adding comic effects.
- **Science / technology / business:** relevant laboratory/machine/office tone, evidenced device interactions and clear information cues. Use a source's real sound only when licensed; do not fake unsupported product features or add constant electronic beeps.
- **Science fiction:** location-specific machinery/air, coherent invented device/door textures and purposeful scale/reveal. A spaceship engine does not imply an alarm or countdown absent from the story.
- **History / adventure:** era-appropriate weather/crowd/interior tone, paper/wood/metal/footwear materials and scale. No modern notifications in an ancient scene unless the brief intentionally uses anachronism.
- **Suspense / survival:** apply the conditional module below; threat follows this plot, not a mandatory zombie library.

**AUDIO CUT** is an intentional designed exception to the scene bed, not a broken export: specify which buses stop, what remains (for example breath), duration/return trigger and tail/fade behavior. Near-silence and complete silence are different choices. Follow approved scripted silence; do not fill it to obey an “always audible” slogan or choose a fixed 0.5/2-second gap for every story. Contrast must remain comfortable on headphones, not a painful level spike.

## Voice treatment: recommendations separate from execution

The user-provided tuning engine supplies **conditional audition starting points**, not a new universal B voice preset. Default to each style's approved native named voice/settings, including B4 Brian; preserve accepted audio. Do not force ElevenLabs on Gemini or another selected provider, recast a narrator to suit an emotion, or alternate voices just to vary a continuous monologue. New/additional role casting still follows [actual Google research](autopilot.md#voice-led-production).

When a project explicitly requests vocal tuning, the sound-script row may recommend an experiment supported by the actual provider/model: **Stability 30–40%** for a tension/fear passage, or **65–75%** for steadier explanatory/grand narration. These ranges are not verified performance guarantees; low stability can increase rate/performance variation, and high stability can flatten emotion. Record current values if exposed, suggested values, rationale and the short representative excerpt to audition. Do not change stability sentence by sentence or regenerate an accepted full narration to implement the plan.

“Low, breathy,” “even pace” and similar acting notes describe a proposed performance in the plan; B's normal TTS direction fields remain empty. Only a specific approved project override or an express selected-style role contract may enter supported instructions/settings. [B4's audio contract](b4-audio-direction.md) authorizes story-motivated direction for secondary dialogue roles, with dry-performance review before channel treatment; it keeps Brian's narrator settings fixed and does not change other B styles. A general request for better SFX is not a vocal override. A parameter recommendation alone does not authorize paid auditions. Keep continuous narration/approved chapters and actual audition comparison rather than promising a text label will deliver the desired acting.

Verify exposed controls rather than conflating labels: ElevenLabs similarity is not a universal “Clarity” control, and not all models/platforms expose the same parameters. If unavailable, record “unsupported/not exposed”; never invent a numerical setting, inject settings into spoken text, or claim it was applied. Service capability is not established by a generic prompt. See the [official TTS settings documentation](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/playground/text-to-speech) and check current model/provider behavior before an authorized experiment.

## Suspense / apocalypse module

Use this module for relevant B stories, including B4 suspense/survival—not every B4 topic and not every B style. Aim for controlled cinematic weight, evolving threat and intelligible narration, not guaranteed retention, “dopamine” claims or maximum loudness.

- **Ambient foundation:** low industrial air/drone, deep wind or appropriate location tone can sustain unease beneath speech. Vary texture and intensity over a long film; do not repeat one obvious short loop for ten minutes. Control low-end accumulation and leave room for the voice. Unintentional dead air is a defect; deliberately removing the bed is a storytelling tool.
- **Spatial threat:** build distant → approaching → barrier/close only when the plot supports it. A safe-room sequence might move from distant horde/wind to weighted footsteps, a door rattle and occluded breathing. Change level, high-frequency detail, attack and reverb/room perspective together; simply turning up one roar does not create distance. Zombies, gunshots, wet rips and gore sounds require actual story motivation. A telephone story instead needs ring/receiver, line noise and phone perspective—not an unrelated horde.
- **Contrast/payoff:** mark setups, small warning cues and major reveals. In roughly ten-minute suspense, inspect each 1–2-minute span for tension development or payoff; this is a pacing review prompt, **not a timed jump-scare quota**. Quiet clues, unresolved offscreen sounds and reversals also count. Smaller accents prepare the few largest hits.
- **Intentional dropout:** before a motivated reveal, duck or briefly remove ambience/music, retain breath/room detail if appropriate, then resolve into the event. Choose duration from the actual performance, not a fixed silence template. Shape edges/tails to avoid clicks. Preserve any approved scripted silence; do not refill it because a continuous bed is usually useful.
- **Impact construction:** choose a credible transient + low-mid body/controlled sub + material/debris/reverb tail. A heavy door wants mass and room response, not a toy pop or an unrelated trailer boom. Duck competing layers around the hit, avoid music/SFX double attacks, and do not use painful high-frequency spikes or huge level jumps to imitate impact.
- **Heartbeat / radio static:** useful candidate textures, not mandatory continuous beds. Heartbeat can support a character's fear or subjective focus; radio static belongs to a radio/communication event. Place and filter them accordingly, duck under speech and remove them when their job ends. Avoid repetitive identical pulses, a riser on every cut or constant creature breathing.

Search vocabulary, selected by cue rather than fetched as a bulk checklist:

- Foundation: `industrial drone`, `deep wind`, `room tone`, `distant city`, `fluorescent hum`.
- Threat/contact: `distant zombie horde`, `zombie moan`, `guttural snarl`, `heavy footsteps`, `dragging footsteps`, `occluded heavy breathing`, `door rattle`, `wood scrape`.
- Subjective/payoff: `heartbeat`, `radio static noise`, `cinematic riser`, `metal impact`, `sub impact`, `debris tail`. Use `blood splatter` or `wet rip` only for a justified depicted/offscreen event.

## Candidate sources and rights gate

Select search terms and sources from the approved sound script. Original recordings, legally usable general/Foley libraries and authorized generation remain valid for every genre; do not ban open-source tools or general libraries as inherently cheap. Prefer a well-matched legally usable recording over a famous library name. The following horror-oriented examples are sourcing leads, not a required shortlist for every B project or endorsements that every asset is high-quality, free, copyright-free or cleared for every use:

- **Pixabay:** [zombie](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/search/zombie/) and [cinematic zombie](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/search/cinematic%20zombie/) searches. Check the actual item and [Content License Summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/), including its link to the binding terms. Free use/adaptation is subject to restrictions; it is not public-domain status or unrestricted standalone redistribution.
- **Uppbeat:** [horror/zombie](https://uppbeat.io/sfx/category/horror/zombie), or search its SFX catalog for the cue's material/action. Verify the actual free/paid item, account plan, permitted project/distribution and attribution under the applicable license. The [User Agreement](https://uppbeat.io/user-agreement) distinguishes free and paid SFX and prohibits scripted/automated downloads as checked on 2026-11-01. Do not build an automated downloader; if access would violate current terms, use a lawful alternative or request user-supplied licensed files.
- **Ghosthack:** [official site](https://www.ghosthack.de/) free horror packs may provide drones, risers and impacts. The user-supplied [Free Horror Sound Effects Part 2 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bHcLfdR7yZs) is a discovery lead, **not an audio extraction source or license proof**. Locate the official pack and its included/current license before using files; this policy update has not verified that pack's license.

The Pixabay summary and Uppbeat agreement were read on 2026-11-01; recheck current asset-specific terms at acquisition. Do not freeze catalog counts, free-plan allowances, “industry best” claims or creator names into quality guarantees. If a source is unavailable, say what remains unverified; do not fabricate an approval or silently use temporary audio in a final.

For each acquired asset retain its exact item/pack URL, creator, original filename and SHA-256, license/terms URL or included license, check date, attribution if required, permitted commercial/monetized use, edits, platform/account limits and any available claim/license documentation. Distinguish unknown from cleared. Free/royalty-free does not mean no copyright or guaranteed absence of Content ID claims. Avoid incompatible noncommercial/no-derivatives assets; verify intended editing/layering rights.

Keep licensed library audio in the external project by default. **Permission to synchronize in a video is not permission to bundle raw files into this publicly installable Skill**; redistribution needs separate explicit rights. Recording original Foley or authorized generation are alternatives, not automatic billable fallbacks. Do not buy, subscribe, bulk-download, bypass access controls or require a particular library account to install/select B. Policy changes alone authorize no acquisition, paid calls or publication.

## Mix, runtime and acceptance

- Keep untouched original VO and separate working dialogue, ambience, Foley/event FX, designed hits and music stems. All stems share start, duration and sample rate; retain cue/rights manifests for revision and localization. Do not bake a new voice into FX or duplicate effects already present in a supplied mixed master.
- Sync attacks to actual contacts/reveals and approved speech. Use gain/EQ/ducking and perspective automation to keep words clear; do not stretch speech or add internal pauses to fit effects. Choose low-mid body that translates to phones instead of relying only on inaudible sub-bass. Check mono compatibility and harshness as well as stereo depth.
- Use the delivery specification when supplied. Without one, roughly **−16 to −14 LUFS integrated** and a **−1 to −2 dBTP ceiling** are working web-video starting points, not official YouTube rules or a required loudness target for every genre. Preserve dramatic contrast. Measure the full encoded deliverable, inspect loudness/true peaks, and repair competing layers rather than crushing everything through a limiter.
- Follow [runtime configuration](autopilot-runtime.md); do not invent manifest fields for external stems, spatial mixing or automation. The built-in procedural accents are not a complete cinematic sound system. When the supported route requires a prepared mixed master, make a separate file without altering the accepted original VO, retain stems/timing, use the documented `voice.contains_sfx` handling and omit duplicate added effects. Audio replacement follows the normal scoped sample-approval rules. If the chosen runtime cannot express the required design, document the gap and resolve a supported finishing route rather than claiming the rule implements a new engine.
- Review the actual representative sample and final soundtrack: material/scale, distance progression, plot relevance, clean loops/joins, meaningful quiet moments, distinct hit hierarchy, intelligible words, no clipped/harsh spikes, no copy-pasted cue fatigue and no unauthorized assets. Check headphones and small-speaker/mono playback when available; explicitly mark unavailable listening/device checks. Meters, successful rendering and static tests do not prove listening or acceptance. Add concise meaningful non-speech captions when necessary, consistent with the selected caption style; do not label every decorative rustle.
- On revisions, solo/mute to identify the failing layer, fix only affected cues and recheck adjacent transitions/encode. A request for SFX is not permission to revoice, redraw, republish or regenerate the whole film.
