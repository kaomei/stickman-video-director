# B4 · Story-led opening, typography and audiovisual escalation

Required with the [B4 visual contract](b4-survival-storytelling.md), [picture-animation contract](b4-picture-animation.md), [audio contract](b4-audio-direction.md), [typography acceptance contract](b4-typography-acceptance.md) and [shared QA](autopilot-production-qa.md). The [installed runtime](autopilot-runtime.md#b4-fixed-opening) implements the fixed opening preset and painted/encoded checks; story meaning and creative review remain authored work. Apply only to B4; preserve other styles and accepted media unless their revision is requested.

## Opening design before generation

Record the first three seconds and first ten seconds in the project shot/sound ledger: exact approved words and actual voice times, visible anomaly or dramatic question, focal action/state change, text function, sound cause/perspective, and transition into the body. Preserve supplied copy; a visual hook is not permission to rewrite or reorder narration.

For horror openings, execute the [story-led horror visibility gate](b4-survival-storytelling.md#story-led-horror-visibility-gate): identify the visible threat evidence and cast relationship before generation, then check the encoded picture without relying on labels or sound. Do not limit this gate to the hook; retain it for later threat-bearing beats without spoiling withheld reveals.

- **Earn attention with the story.** Establish a concrete abnormality, consequence, conflict or useful discovery appropriate to the genre. For horror, make the threatening relationship legible at playback size through silhouette, scale, staging, obstruction and controlled contrast. More blood, crushed blacks, extra monsters, painful peaks or faster cuts are not automatic improvements. For non-horror B4, use the story's own stakes; do not import a horror preset.
- **Reserve distinct opening artwork.** When refreshing the hook, create a purposeful new view/state rather than replaying a later climax still with another crop. Inspect opening/body assets together for distant repetition. Do not reveal the ending early, invent plot events or regenerate every accepted later illustration merely for novelty. A motivated callback may reuse an asset when the later action/information actually changes.
- **Progress, do not merely decorate.** Plan readable local acting, prop contact, environmental change or substantive information development, plus time to perceive the result. Carry this into the body's mandatory [picture-motion plan and actual local rendering](b4-picture-animation.md). A well-drawn held pose can carry tension or a reveal between meaningful animated beats; no compulsory limb animation. Camera drift, subtitle turnover, grain and glow alone are not principal-picture action. Apply B4's deliberate-hold exception only within its hard three-second shot/state limits, without forcing inferior limb rigs merely to simulate acting; avoid flash inserts, chained punch-ins and motion that obscures cause and effect. The first-three-second hook and the whole-film three-second maximum both apply.
- **Keep the drawing lock under exaggeration.** Submit the canonical scene-relevant B4 reference and complete style prompt for each new asset. Preserve round white heads, compact white gloves, coherent shoulder–elbow–wrist reach and the outlined flat-color environment. Stronger fear or staging must not turn into realistic anatomy, 3D shading or an unrelated illustration style.

## Text has a dramatic job

### Confirmed opening treatment

For the default **landscape** B4 story hook, stage a **left-side half-shadow scrim** fading into the image, with a short audience-facing question on the left and the story-supported anomaly readable on the right. Preserve the outlined 2D drawing and readable color; avoid a uniformly black/blue horror filter or repeated polished badge template. This is editorial type, not invented in-world evidence. For horror, the right-side threat must actually be unsettling, not a normal friendly pose with an ominous label. Other genres use their own conflict instead of a monster. Portrait uses its separate approved composition below; a materially different treatment may be explicitly chosen by the user.

Use a concise question, usually two short semantic groups, derived from this story; do not repeat another sample's exact wording. Let the setup group appear first, then reveal the **entire remaining question together** after a short readable beat. Never stagger the second group word by word, letter by letter or with a typewriter. The accepted example's words and exact delay are project details, not mandatory copy/timing. Keep the complete question visible long enough to read, and distinguish this hook from later rule/note/prop typography.

**Fixed default typography, not a font suggestion.** Use `b4-hook-v1` for landscape unless the user has authorized a scoped story-adapted treatment: bundled Nunito 900 rounded heavy type, warm-white setup, larger coral payoff, dark outline/offset shadow, and a uniform 1.035→1 scale-settle over 0.2 seconds on the whole payoff block. Preserve stable letter proportions and the left half-shadow. The [font package](../assets/fonts/README.md) is a licensed portable substitute for the accepted sample's system Arial Rounded Bold, not a pixel-identical font claim. Do not silently use Noto Sans, a generic bold fallback, a size-only headline or another agent's preferred style. The supplied font covers Latin/Cyrillic/Vietnamese; an uncovered script requires a declared localized treatment with correct shaping, not invisible fallback or forced English letterforms. Reuse existing scoped authorization without asking the user again; apply the [story-adapted design and independent encoded review](b4-typography-acceptance.md) to any adaptation. Use the [opening helper](autopilot-runtime.md#b4-fixed-opening) with approved semantic groups and coherent art states; it does not write copy or generate assets. Change timing to match real speech within the gate, not speech to match a default title delay.

### Native portrait opening

**`b4-portrait-poster-v1` is the approved portrait default.** The user selected scheme 1 on 2026-11-13: “第一种效果明显更好”. Inspect the bundled [short dynamic reference](../assets/b4/portrait-opening-approved.mp4), [settled frame](../assets/b4/portrait-opening-approved.jpg) and [scope/provenance](../assets/b4/portrait-opening-approved.json) before making a 9:16 opening. This is the composition and typography reference; the five white-glove illustrations remain the drawing-style authority. Its question, cast, clock and death figure belong to that example, not every B4 story.

- **Compose vertically:** reserve the upper area for a centered poster headline and the lower area for the story picture, with a soft tonal transition. Keep the face, relevant glove/prop contact and the visible anomaly readable. Native portrait story art must stage the subject in that lower region. Do not squeeze the landscape left-text/right-picture arrangement into two narrow columns, stretch a horizontal illustration or obscure the anomaly with a title.
- **Keep the selected lettering construction:** quieter rounded setup; narrow heavy poster payoff with a deliberate primary/supporting hierarchy, warm cream and story-appropriate coral emphasis, controlled offset depth and a restrained ink-tone split. Use authored semantic lines, natural letter proportions and measured width including shadow. The approved English study uses Nunito for setup and Anton for payoff. One short hand-drawn accent can resolve with the emphasis; do not add a line under every caption or a divider to the A/B series ending.
- **Animate locally:** setup first; reveal the complete payoff block together, use a uniform 1.035→1 settle over 0.2 seconds and let the local accent finish into a calm readable hold. Different sizes/colors inside one payoff remain one semantic entrance, not staggered words. Preserve the separate coherent picture-state reveal and its intermediate frame; poster text alone does not supply the picture action.
- **Localize the design:** Chinese and other scripts retain equivalent visual weight, spacing, ink finish and whole-phrase rhythm using an actual appropriate font. Declare and check the selected font/shaping. Never horizontally compress glyphs, force Latin condensed letterforms onto another script, mechanically cut at a character count or downgrade another language to a static overlay.
- **Review the actual portrait MP4:** watch the entire opening at normal speed at approximately 360×640 display size, then inspect entry, emphasis, settled/hold and picture-transition frames. Confirm reading order, full-phrase readability, visible micro-animation, clean glyphs/shadows, platform-safe placement and the picture's focal evidence. Record target display/platform and any unavailable real-device check. A horizontal preview, full-size still, font name, valid coordinates or successful encode cannot certify this treatment. Return any visually poor or unreadable result even when technical checks pass.

Use the same approved script and accepted native-rate audio when changing only aspect ratio or opening layout. The example's 1.8-second hook then cuts into its story; that is the example's event timing, not a universal shot duration. Preserve the first-three-second progression gate and time every later shot by its events and actual speech within the hard three-second maximum. Creative acceptance stays pending until the actual new opening is independently reviewed and the user accepts its preview.

### First-three-second visual escalation gate

The first three seconds need a designed progression, not simply a finished title placed over an unchanged illustration. Record the setup group, payoff group, actual entrance frames, one emphasis treatment, principal-picture before/after states and the readable hold in the project ledger. Respect the designed spatial/semantic blocks: when an upper block sets up a lower punchline, show the upper block first and reveal the **whole lower block together**. Do not mechanically choose the first two words as the setup or show every block simultaneously. Align the payoff with the approved spoken phrase where present; never delay narration to wait for typography.

Give the default payoff one clear emphasis using the fixed whole-block scale-settle above. Preserve complete readable words and stable letter proportions; avoid competing bounces, continuous glitching or a second copy in the subtitles. Approved words, semantic line breaks, entrance timing, artwork and the cause of the picture change belong to the story; the default typeface, colors, outline/shadow and emphasis grammar do not drift between agents. A different visual treatment requires a genuine scoped user choice; an existing instruction to adapt this story's typography supplies that scope and needs no repeated confirmation. Its local entry/emphasis/settle must pass the [typography acceptance contract](b4-typography-acceptance.md).

Alongside the text, show at least one **readable principal-picture change** during the opening beat: a coherent change from concern to fear, a small natural recoil, a revealing prop/environment state or the emergence of a story-supported threat/trace. Typography, grain, camera drift and glow alone do not pass this gate. The installed default uses two coherent matching-frame art states with a **0.3-second animated local reveal**, followed by at least 0.5 seconds of readable hold; it must contain real picture-layer alpha keys and a visible intermediate frame. A hard switch between two stills is not completed micro-animation. No compulsory limb animation or distorted puppet rig. Choose a local reveal that preserves the drawing, not a whole-scene dissolve or a face morph with doubled features. A deliberately static opening or different animation grammar may be an explicit scoped user choice, recorded with its purpose; do not assume it by default.

For horror, the change must strengthen the visible threatening relationship, not only make a neutral character wiggle. An optional death-shadow or similar editorial apparition must fit the story, be recorded as subjective/editorial rather than invented factual action, and preserve withheld reveals. It is not a mandatory reaper, extra monster or permission to alter the plot. Other genres use their own stakes.

Review the actual encoded first three seconds at normal speed and at the setup/payoff/reaction frames, with sound muted once. Check reading order, emphasis, visible reaction/reveal, anatomy, style continuity and payoff hold. For expression/reveal transitions also inspect an intermediate encoded frame: keep the common pelvis/two-leg fork, glove contact and stable body coherent, without doubled facial features or limbs. Follow the visual contract's pose/state checks; stronger fear does not excuse broken anatomy. A static screenshot of the final title or a passing text-policy test is not evidence of effective timing, fear or audience retention. Record creative acceptance as pending until reviewed.

`validate` checks the painted title, localized picture-state change and intermediate animation; `preview`/`render` decode actual MP4 frames, save opening evidence/contact sheets, and reject missing timing/settle/art. `approve` rechecks the encoded file rather than trusting an edited report. Zero or nonzero keyframes is not itself a verdict: the default requires actual image-layer reveal keys, but decorative keys alone still fail; coherent expression/prop/environment animation needs no limb keys. A ledger declaration, static cuts, title appearance, camera drift/shake, grain, glow or a flash alone do not establish principal-picture acting. Record any explicit user exception as such; never call an exception a diagnostic pass or infer approval from a successful encode.

### Story-led shot timing

Every B4 opening, body and ending shot has a **hard maximum of three seconds**. Divide the dramatic event into coherent visible actions/reveals, using actual speech pauses and readable results within that limit. An unchanged principal-picture state also cannot stay on screen for more than three seconds across neighboring shots: a different crop, subtitle update, micro-zoom, decorative effect or shot name does not restart its timer. The subject must develop through real action or consequential prop, environment or information state change. Preserve the first-three-second hook and the helper's short opening choreography as well; satisfying either does not waive the other.

Before generating, register each shot's `before`, `after`, causal intent, visible local layers/region and evidence times under the [picture-animation contract](b4-picture-animation.md). Author the intermediate state; a text description does not animate it. A coherent held pose may support tension, reading or reaction when its `motivated_hold` entry gives the purpose and adjacent non-hold `supports_shot`. Keep that hold and its complete shot within three seconds, accounting for unchanged-state dwell across cuts. Do not require extra user approval for an ordinary short hold, but never use `exceptional_hold`, an author-written approval or an opening override as a long-shot exemption. Only a new explicit user instruction changing timing scope is followed as that separate concrete request; no implied waiver. Holds must support real progression rather than excuse an entirely static film.

A cut should change the focal subject, viewpoint, visible action/state or consequential information where the story needs it. Another crop/zoom of an unchanged asset, subtitle turnover, tiny flicker and decorative noise do not supply missing animation or a new meaningful view. Review chronological and distant shots for repetition, including reused art under different text. Preserve actual speech and semantic caption boundaries; never shorten words, add dead air or generate unnecessary art to satisfy a timer.

Separate synchronized subtitles from scene information, a chapter/opening label, an anomalous fact and an exceptional warning. Do not present every early line as the same oversized centered title. Use a small coherent hand-drawn/rounded type family with distinct scale, placement and entrance behavior for these jobs, not a different decorative font on every cut.

**Show facts; do not label every fact.** If the picture already shows a location, garment, action or outcome, omit a second explanatory tag. Do not add routine floating words such as “outside,” a garment color, a year or “all lights off” merely to echo narration. Use a clock, watch or readable phone/computer display close-up for time when the story supports it, rather than a floating time card. Essential synchronized subtitles remain separate. Keep structural titles (for example a large rule number) and meaningful connection/disconnection states when they orient the viewer. Do not invent extra rules or signal states.

Avoid a repeated rounded-card/emoji/badge template. Prefer integrated scene composition and a few justified text moments: a half-shadowed headline, restrained hand-lettered warning or scene-specific mark when appropriate. Vary function and composition, not random fonts/colors. If art/action can express the beat more clearly, remove the decorative label rather than restyling it.

For a rule or longer warning that needs full attention, a full-frame text treatment may temporarily soften the existing scene with a restrained translucent/frosted layer. Reveal semantic units with time to read; preserve context, contrast and safe areas. Do not stack a duplicate full subtitle over the same words. Check the final encoded frame, not just the design canvas.

For a story-motivated ominous written warning, an irregular handwritten note, rough paper edge or restrained ink mark may fit better than a polished tech HUD. This is an optional story adapter, **not a mandatory death-note graphic**. Do not fabricate an in-world note or factual evidence where the script has none; clearly editorial text remains editorial. Roughness must not sacrifice legibility. Do not transfer the Dispatch note, font or exact timing to every story.

## Sound and picture must resolve together

Spot the immediate hearing hook from a real source/event or motivated subjective contrast. No default ringtone, scream or trailer boom. Establish one clear foreground sound job; use a subtle continuous scene-matched bed and sparse necessary causal events around it, as required by the audio contract. Three functional layers do not need to run at once. Remove redundant hiss, wind, hum, drones and overlapping tails before adding weight. Keep deliberate AUDIO CUT with explicit stop/remain/return cues.

Apply the audio contract's [first-frame audio gate](b4-audio-direction.md#first-frame-audio-gate): default to early intelligible Brian narration, not a fixed SFX-only title pad. Do not wait for the question/title entrance before starting perceptible sound, or leave a token hit followed by an empty-feeling lead-in. Inspect the exported first three seconds, not just cue timestamps. If approved as spoken copy, a short question may lead into the retained body; synchronize title groups to that actual voice and move affected picture/captions/SFX together after a duration change. An audible SFX onset alone is not proof of early speech.

Brian remains the fixed narrator. Automatically execute plot-needed role casting/performance and SFX acquisition/mixing under the audio contract and existing cost/rights gates; do not force extra voices. Verify critical events in the full encoded mix, with speech active, not only in an effects-only export. A new rule alone does not authorize revoicing old samples; explicit remake authorization does.

## Acceptance evidence

Review first-ten-second playback, chronological opening/body contact sheet, suspect full-size anatomy/contact crops, text reading time, semantic-event gaps and the complete final mix. Record source identities, actual submitted references, timings, fingerprints, stem paths, loudness/true peak and failures. Static policy tests prove instruction coverage, not attractive art, convincing acting or frightening sound. Mark unavailable physical listening/device checks as pending user review. Deliver the complete requested sample/film and a verified folder shortcut, retaining old versions; no automatic gallery replacement, installed-copy sync or publication.

Run the [portable production check](b4-repeatability-check.md) on the actual project ledger. Its `technical_only` result is not delivery readiness or visual acceptance. Complete separate independent [picture](b4-picture-animation.md#encoded-evidence-and-independent-picture-review) and [typography](b4-typography-acceptance.md#encoded-evidence-and-independent-acceptance) reviews for every shot/text moment in the actual preview/film, plus full continuous playback and cross-section repetition review. Neither review supplies the other's evidence. Do not turn mechanical diagnostics into a claim that viewers found the hook frightening, the type well designed or the sound convincing.
